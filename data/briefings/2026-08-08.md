# Daily Briefing — Saturday, 8 August 2026

## EU Affairs

**Madrid gives Rome until Sunday to lift border controls as the Ceuta fallout splinters Schengen**
Spain warned on Friday that it will take "proportional measures" against Italy if Rome does not end border controls on travellers arriving from Spain by 9 August, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/spain-retaliation-italy-border-controls-travelers-ceuta-migrants/) reports, an escalation of the dispute triggered by last week's mass arrival of unauthorised migrants in the Spanish exclave of Ceuta. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/spain-threatens-retaliation-italy-if-border-controls-not-lifted-2026-08-07/) frames the row as a straightforward retaliation threat over travel restrictions, while the [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) newsletter of 8 August treats the entire European response as detached from the facts: at least 88 people died trying to reach the exclave, Italy declared a one-month "suspension" of Schengen, and Denmark and Finland floated expelling Spain from the free-movement area altogether — even though Ceuta already sits outside Schengen, with passport and visa checks carried out on everyone leaving the territory. Euractiv also notes that of the roughly 80,000 people who entered the exclave, most had been returned within 48 hours, and that the open letter signed by 22 EU leaders, spearheaded by Giorgia Meloni and Mette Frederiksen, makes no mention of Moroccan economic destitution — a third of Moroccans aged 15 to 24 are unemployed and per capita GDP is under one-eighth of Spain's. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/05/pourquoi-le-maroc-na-pas-pu-orchestrer-la-crise-de-ceuta/) goes further still, arguing that attributing the crisis to Rabat misreads Moroccan social dynamics and the singularity of the exclave, and that [Ceuta is not a migration crisis for Europe](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/pourquoi-ceuta-nest-pas-une-crise-migratoire-pour-leurope/) at all.

**Merz's own party weighs replacing him after barely a year**
A growing number of CDU politicians are privately discussing a *Kanzlertausch* — swapping out Friedrich Merz as chancellor — [Politico's Nette Nöstlinger reports](https://www.politico.eu/article/friedrich-merz-chancellor-swap-cdu-afd-germany-political-crisis/). Merz himself appears unconcerned, telling reporters last week that "things will have calmed down a bit by September," but his ratings remain at rock bottom ahead of autumn regional elections in the east where the AfD, now the most popular party in national polls, is expected to do well. Politico notes that replacing a chancellor years before the end of a term would take Germany into largely uncharted territory, in a country with no history of the leadership churn seen in postwar Italy or post-Brexit Britain.

**Europe's summer of fire collides with the industrial turn of the Green Deal**
The EU has spent the past year remoulding the European Green Deal so that new climate legislation must demonstrate an economic advantage to pass, with older policies reassessed and often watered down to help European firms compete with China and the United States — a pivot now [crashing into Europe's climate reality](https://www.politico.eu/article/economic-toll-europe-infernal-summer-climate-change/), according to Politico. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/eu-push-countries-invest-nature-wildfire-costs-spiral-2026-08-06/) puts a figure on the damage: wildfires have burned more than a million acres across Europe this year, and Brussels is pressing member states to invest in nature as firefighting costs spiral. That in turn is [reshaping the aerial firefighting market](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/europes-would-be-water-bombers-race-tackle-future-wildfires-2026-08-07/), where aviation start-ups are racing to displace the Canadair water-bombers that have dominated for decades but need substantial capital for R&D, certification and factories.

## UK Politics

**Burnham devolves a share of income tax to England's metro mayors**
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced one of the broadest reforms of local government finance in decades, [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/03/burnham-e-il-fisco-di-prossimita/) reports: from 2028 the mayors of England's combined authorities — Greater Manchester, the West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, West and South Yorkshire among them — will retain a share of the income tax collected in their territory, with a larger share of business rates retained from 2027 and a tourist tax to follow. The government calls it the largest transfer of powers and resources from Westminster in a generation; the exact percentage will be set in the autumn budget. Phastidio's analysis is sceptical on the detail: the scheme is not a local surcharge and gives no rate-setting power, but tying local revenue to income tax rewards areas that already have high incomes, sharpens the incentive to attract wealthy taxpayers, and exposes local budgets to the economic cycle — with the equalisation mechanisms still undefined. Conservative metro mayors have already said they would rather return the money to taxpayers as rebates, which is not what Burnham's Labour has in mind.

## Economy & Markets

**Trump's 15% polysilicon tariff lands, but not until December**
The US president announced on Thursday a 15% levy on polysilicon and related products, the ultra-pure silicon used in solar panels and advanced chips, framing it as protection for a material "essential" to national security. Per the [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) economy roundup, China accounts for more than 90% of global polysilicon production; Wacker Chemie, whose Tennessee facility is one of only two active US plants, said it was "assessing" the impact, and the European Commission called the levy "in line" with last year's EU–US trade deal. [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/08/06/trump-tariffs-solar-semiconductors-01028009) emphasises the timing rather than the substance: the directive does not take effect until 4 December, after November's midterms and a planned September summit between Trump and Xi Jinping, as the administration manages both voter anger over prices and fragile trade talks with Beijing.

**Ukrainian drone strikes are pushing Russia to export crude it can no longer refine**
Russian seaborne exports of refined petroleum products fell to 1.18 million barrels a day in July from 1.51 million in June — the lowest since the series began in 2016 — after Ukrainian drone strikes knocked out more than a third of Russian refining capacity, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/08/economie-russe-les-drones-ukrainiens-font-flamber-lexportation-de-brut/) reports, citing preliminary S&P figures published on 4 August. Moscow is redirecting the unrefined barrels: seaborne crude exports reached 4.321 million barrels a day, down 7% on the month but up 26% on the year, with shipments to China at 1.017 million barrels a day, up 18% month-on-month. Le Grand Continent reads the apparent paradox as bad news for the Kremlin — crude fetches less than refined product, petrol prices are rising for Russian households, and Moscow is increasingly dependent on a shadow fleet that S&P now puts at roughly 22% of the world total.

**German industry shows 'surprising resilience' as Chinese exports surge**
Destatis reported on Friday that industrial output in the EU's largest economy rose 0.2% in real terms between May and June, driven largely by a 3.6% increase in the automotive sector, according to the [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) economy roundup. Chinese customs data over the same period showed exports to Germany up 17.4% in July while imports from Germany fell 2.7% — a widening imbalance that sits awkwardly beside the output figure.

## Foreign Policy

**Washington expects a Hormuz deal as Iran and Oman finalise a transit framework**
A US official reported progress on Friday between Iran and Oman that could soon reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restore oil exports disrupted by the five-month-old US war with Iran, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-expects-deal-soon-strait-hormuz-sunni-powers-unite-defense-pact-2026-08-07/) reports, with [vessel traffic through the strait dwindling this week](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/vessel-traffic-through-hormuz-dwindles-this-week-markets-watch-iran-oman-talks-2026-08-07/) as markets watch the talks. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/06/que-contient-laccord-que-liran-et-loman-seraient-en-train-de-finaliser-pour-le-transit-dans-le-detroit-dormuz/) supplies the contents: a temporary 60-day arrangement under which inbound traffic uses a northern corridor through Iranian waters and outbound traffic a southern corridor through Omani waters, with no tolls levied and the central corridor to be demined within 30 days — Tehran reportedly willing to let European navies do the clearing, per *Bloomberg*. Iranian deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi told IRNA that agreement in principle has been reached on almost everything, including the traffic map, but cautioned that the deal does not guarantee resumption of shipping: Tehran wants the US naval blockade and reimposed oil sanctions lifted first. Le Grand Continent adds that Trump has now backed away from threatened strikes for the fifth time since 28 February, and that Reuters reported the US has used "virtually all" of its long-range precision missiles during the war — an account the president has denied.

**Saudi Arabia, Turkey and Pakistan pledge mutual defence**

Reuters separately reports that Iraq's prime minister and the Saudi intelligence chief [discussed regional security](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iraqi-pm-saudi-intelligence-chief-discuss-regional-security-militias-delay-2026-08-07/) while Iran-aligned militias delayed their response, and that Trump remains [stuck in a war with no exit in sight](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trumps-iran-dilemma-hes-stuck-war-with-no-exit-sight-2026-08-06/).

**Vilnius warns Moscow could stage a false-flag drone attack on NATO soil**
Lithuanian defence minister Robertas Kaunas made public a military intelligence assessment on 6 August suggesting Moscow could use captured Ukrainian drones against critical infrastructure in the Baltic states, disguising the origin of the strike, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/07/la-russie-prepare-t-elle-une-operation-sous-faux-drapeau-sur-le-territoire-de-lotan/) reports, citing [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/lithuania-says-russia-could-use-ukrainian-drones-against-baltic-region-2026-08-06/). Kaunas called the scenario realistic but stressed there is no indication Russia has decided to act or that an attack is imminent, and said he has not authorised Ukraine to use Lithuanian airspace for strikes on Russia. Le Grand Continent situates the warning in a run of similar statements from NATO's eastern flank — Radek Sikorski, Donald Tusk, President Nausėda and Latvia's Rinkēvičs have all warned of limited Russian provocations — and notes that an explosive-laden drone was found near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft at Leipzig airport the previous day, which German interior minister Dobrindt described as a hybrid attack scenario without naming a culprit. The Kremlin dismissed such Baltic warnings in July as scare tactics to justify "militarisation against Russia".
