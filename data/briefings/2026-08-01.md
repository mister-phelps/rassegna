# Daily Briefing — Saturday, 1 August 2026

## EU Affairs

**Ceuta crisis: dozens die as 50,000 migrants storm Spanish enclave in what Madrid calls a deliberate Moroccan provocation**
Between 40,000 and 50,000 migrants — mostly young Moroccan men — crossed illegally into Spain's autonomous enclave of Ceuta on July 30–31, swimming or climbing around the breakwater that separates the city from Moroccan territory. The initial Spanish death toll stood at 19 according to [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/plus-de-40-000-arrivees-illegales-de-migrants-et-19-morts-que-se-passe-t-il-a-ceuta-lexclave-espagnole-frontaliere-avec-le-maroc/), rising to at least 67 drowned or crushed per the [Reuters Weekend Briefing](https://www.thomsonreuters.com). The influx was four to five times larger than the 2021 crisis, representing more than half of Ceuta's normal population of 84,000. Spain deployed its army and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez flew to the enclave, describing the situation as "an attack, a violation of the territorial integrity of Spain," according to [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/). The sudden withdrawal of Moroccan gendarmes — which [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) and [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/plus-de-40-000-arrivees-illegales-de-migrants-et-19-morts-que-se-passe-t-il-a-ceuta-lexclave-espagnole-frontaliere-avec-le-maroc/) both read as a deliberate Rabat decision — is widely attributed to Morocco's anger over Sánchez's visit to Algeria on July 20. Spain's Supreme Court ruled on July 8 that immediate pushbacks of swimmers cannot be legally applied as they can for those crossing the land fence, though Le Grand Continent notes the government believes returns can still be arranged "without major legal obstacles." By Saturday, the great majority of those who had entered had gone back: a Spanish government official told Politico that more than 48,300 had returned, while [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com) put the 24-hour figure at 37,000.

**EU unity fractures over Ceuta as Italy and Denmark push for Schengen suspension**
The political fallout hardened rapidly across Europe. [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) reports that Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni wrote on X calling the scenes evidence of "uncontrolled illegal immigration" posing "a real threat to the security of Europe's borders," while Italy suspended free air and sea transport with Spain — a move Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani called "a necessary choice." Italy, Denmark and 20 other member states signed a joint letter to EU leaders criticising Spain's migrant regularisation scheme and urging stronger internal border measures, per [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com). Germany's Chancellor Friedrich Merz called on Morocco to take back irregular migrants. France's Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez ordered increased controls at the Franco-Spanish border after Marine Le Pen urged the government to "step up border controls." [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com) frames the reaction as emblematic of how "migration emergencies are increasingly framed less as a shared European challenge than as a test of whether governments are doing enough to repel migrants": neither France nor the European Commission offered explicit political support to Sánchez. On Saturday, Sánchez hit back in a letter to EU leaders, accusing them of acting out of "prejudice" and undermining solidarity, insisting Spain had restored control within 48 hours and that no migrants had moved onward to the Schengen zone, per [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com). [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) frames this as "profoundly" exposing "how the political balance has shifted in Europe," while [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/plus-de-40-000-arrivees-illegales-de-migrants-et-19-morts-que-se-passe-t-il-a-ceuta-lexclave-espagnole-frontaliere-avec-le-maroc/) emphasises that the crisis "seems less to reflect a security failure than an instrumentalisation by Rabat of its own population against Madrid."

**Russia's Olympic Committee reinstated — with an implicit concession on occupied Ukrainian territories**
The International Olympic Committee has reinstated Russia's Olympic Committee after a three-year suspension, but the price was significant. The ROC formally accepted that regional sports organisations in Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson — all annexed by Russia in 2022 — fall under the jurisdiction of Ukraine's National Olympic Committee, according to [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/). Moscow celebrated the reinstatement as a diplomatic win, but Politico notes it has triggered a domestic backlash from pro-war voices who see it as a betrayal of the Kremlin's territorial claims. Pro-war journalist Dmitry Steshin wrote: "The Russian Olympic Committee gave up part of the country's territory just to live it up in style at the Olympics."

**EU launches €30 billion AI gigafactory programme**
The European Commission published a call-for-tender this week for a network of seven AI gigafactories across the EU, as [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com) reports. €5 billion in EU financing will be matched by member state contributions, with the stated aim of attracting enough private capital to reach €30 billion in total investment. Only four of the seven sites will host 100,000 AI chips — a capacity well below US AI giants. Euractiv notes that it took over a year for Brussels to settle on the project's scale, during which time global AI development advanced significantly.

## Italian Politics

**Meloni leads Europe's hard-line response to Ceuta — and positions for 2027 elections**
Italy's posture over Ceuta was the most assertive in the EU (see EU Affairs). Beyond the foreign policy dimension, the Ceuta episode is already feeding into domestic positioning for next year's general election. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/01/la-settimana-phastidiosa-1-agosto-2026/) notes that Foreign Minister Tajani has been floating tax-cut proposals for the 2027 election-year budget law with no credible funding source identified — a pattern the site caricatures as "Anto', fa caldo" (roughly: don't worry about it, it's summer). Austria's Migration Minister Claudia Bauer told Politico that Spain had been "effectively handing out entry tickets to the EU" and backed Meloni's Schengen proposal — framing that the Meloni government is likely to amplify in the months ahead.

## Economy & Markets

**Federal Reserve holds rates for fifth time — markets punish Warsh's credibility gap**
The US Federal Reserve left the Fed Funds rate unchanged at 3.5–3.75% on July 29 — the fifth consecutive hold — in a 9-to-3 vote, with regional Fed presidents Lorie Logan (Dallas), Beth Hammack (Cleveland) and Neel Kashkari (Minneapolis) all dissenting in favour of a rate increase. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/30/kevin-warsh-un-parolaio-alla-fed/) offers a detailed critical analysis: Chair Kevin Warsh, who was appointed by Trump ostensibly to cut rates, has abandoned "forward guidance" in the style of his mentor Alan Greenspan, but markets have not rewarded the opacity. After the decision, yields rose sharply across the entire curve — with the long and ultra-long end hit hardest — a sign that inflation expectations are not firmly anchored. In his press conference, Warsh claimed the market was "doing part of the work of a rate increase" by pushing yields up; Phastidio's Mario Seminerio calls this reasoning circular and draws a comparison with the "transitory inflation" episode under Jerome Powell, warning Warsh may be building toward a forced correction. Trump has already begun saying publicly that Warsh wants to cut rates but "the others" won't let him — positioning Seminerio reads as preparation for a confrontational autumn scenario.

**Iran tightens grip on the Strait of Hormuz; oil rises**
Oil prices rose on Friday after Iran announced it had stopped two vessels attempting to exit the Strait of Hormuz, compounding concerns following a drone attack on ships at an Egyptian Mediterranean port, according to the [Reuters Iran Briefing](https://www.reuters.com). CBS News separately reported that the US and Israel are planning strikes on Iranian energy infrastructure. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/01/la-settimana-phastidiosa-1-agosto-2026/) frames Trump as increasingly "cornered at Hormuz," lacking both the diplomatic leverage and, reportedly, sufficient munitions to force a resolution.

## Foreign Policy

**Gaza: White House announces demilitarisation framework, but parties diverge on what was agreed**
The White House unveiled a framework for Gaza on Thursday under which Hamas would disarm and relinquish governance, while Israel would withdraw its military. President Trump declared both sides had reached "an agreement," but as [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com) both report, Hamas and Israeli officials see significant hurdles and neither characterised the situation in those terms — with a Hamas official pointedly avoiding the word "disarmament." The Houthis, separately, denied plans to impose fees on Red Sea shipping.

**Russia prepares for new mobilisation — Russians are already trying to avoid it**
Searches on Yandex for conscription exemptions have reached levels not seen since autumn 2022, when Moscow announced partial mobilisation, according to [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/les-russes-se-preparent-a-une-nouvelle-mobilisation-et-cherchent-a-leviter/). The human rights group "Allez par la forêt" is now receiving up to 200 requests per day from people seeking to leave the country or exit the front. The Kremlin appears to be laying legal groundwork: the Duma last week passed a bill allowing citizens with criminal records to sign military contracts during "official mobilisation" periods, and training grounds for new recruits have reportedly been under preparation since June. New contract signings have fallen to just 800 per day in the first half of 2026 — half the rate of Q4 2024. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/01/larmee-russe-a-avance-de-1-km%c2%b2-par-jour-en-moyenne-en-juillet/) separately reports that Russian forces advanced an average of 1 km² per day in July.
