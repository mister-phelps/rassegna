# Daily Briefing — Saturday, 25 July 2026

## EU Affairs

**EU and US quietly relieved as new tariff regime proves milder than feared**
The European Union signalled measured satisfaction on Friday after the Trump administration replaced expiring interim duties with indefinite Section 301 tariffs of between 10% and 12.5% on 60 trading partners — citing their alleged failure to prohibit imports made with forced labour. According to the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/a-stark-warning-for-centrists/), EU exports are subject to a 10% duty, and unlike many other countries, this will not be levied on top of existing most-favoured-nation (MFN) rates, which average around 4.8% for the bloc. [Euractiv](https://newsletter.euractiv.com) reports that the Commission "notes positively the fact that this outcome is in line with the US tariff commitments agreed under the EU-US joint statement", and that the new levies provide "positive momentum to continue the work of exploring further tariff exemptions". In net terms, EU exporters face a lower overall tariff burden than they did on Thursday. The transatlantic truce struck at Turnberry last year had committed Washington to cap duties on most EU goods at 15%.

**EU agrees 21st Russia sanctions package, including confiscation of shadow fleet oil**
EU ambassadors signed off on a fresh round of sanctions against Moscow on Thursday, breaking a weeks-long deadlock, [Euractiv](https://newsletter.euractiv.com) reports. The package includes a significant provision allowing member states to sell crude oil and other commodities seized from Russia's shadow fleet of tankers, which Moscow uses to evade the G7 oil price cap. A compromise brokered by Ireland secured Greek support through a renewable one-year exemption permitting companies — including Greek LNG shipper Dynagas — to continue transporting Russian LNG to third countries. The arrangement is subject to annual review, and any adjustment to the G7 price cap is frozen for twelve months.

**China retaliates against EU sanctions with restrictions on 14 defence and tech firms**
Beijing announced it will impose export restrictions on 14 European defence and technology companies after the EU added 14 Chinese entities to its Russia sanctions lists, the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/article/china-strikes-back-at-eu-sanctions-targeting-defense-tech-firms-rheinmetall/) reports. The move marks China's most direct and forceful response yet to EU sanctions listings, which it denounced as "egregious". Recent studies estimate that up to 90% of sanctioned goods still reaching Russia transit through China.

**Kretschmer challenges Merz's anti-AfD firewall ahead of Saxony elections**
Michael Kretschmer, CDU premier of Saxony and a key ally of Chancellor Friedrich Merz, has sparked a major political debate by calling for cooperation with the far-right Alternative for Germany, according to the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-friedrich-merz-michael-kretschmer-challenge-afd-saxony-elections/). "Anyone who still talks about firewalls hasn't recognised the signs of the times," Kretschmer told the *Handelsblatt*. He leads a minority government in Saxony, where elections in September could put the AfD in charge of a German state for the first time.

## EU Politics — France and the Far Right

**PPI study: Le Pen is "the only show in town" as French centre hollows out**
A major study of French voters by British political strategists Deborah Mattinson and Claire Ainsley, both affiliated with the Progressive Policy Institute, concludes that Marine Le Pen and the National Rally are on track to win the next presidential election — not because voters are enthusiastic about them, but because they see no credible alternative, as the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/a-stark-warning-for-centrists/) sets out in detail. Focus groups conducted across Nantes, Marseille and other cities found voters describing France as a country in moral and social disintegration, with all four groups citing the recent murder of an 11-year-old girl by a suspect previously flagged to police as emblematic of a state that has ceased to function. Emmanuel Macron, who leaves office next year, is seen as "almost the personification of everything that they don't like about power". The study was conducted before the 7 July court ruling upholding Le Pen's conviction for financial crimes but permitting her to stand for election. Centrist former prime minister Édouard Philippe — also mayor of Le Havre — is the most plausible challenger in the PPI's assessment, though voters are not yet convinced by him; Johanna Rolland, the Socialist mayor of Nantes, was the mainstream politician mentioned most favourably. The pattern — local executives outperforming national parties — is noted by the authors as a broader phenomenon extending to Andy Burnham's rise in Britain and several American cities.

## Italian Politics

**UPB audit confirms IRPEF has become a wage top-up, with the bill for the middle class**
Italy's Parliamentary Budget Office (UPB) has certified that the country's personal income tax (IRPEF) now effectively functions as a wage-supplement mechanism, integrating the incomes of low-paid workers compressed by involuntary part-time work, [Phastidio.net](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/22/il-phastidioso-upb-e-i-kulaki-spremuti-e-drenati/) reports. The cost, as the site's weekly roundup emphasises, falls as always on middle and upper-income earners. The assessment reinforces long-standing concerns about the structural asymmetry of Italian fiscal policy, in which tax relief at the lower end is funded by an ever-heavier burden on dependent employees in higher brackets.

## Economy & Markets

**US manufacturing jobs fall by 75,000 since Trump inauguration, despite tariffs**
Notwithstanding the Trump administration's sweeping tariff programme, the United States has lost approximately 75,000 manufacturing jobs since the president's inauguration, according to data highlighted by [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/21/malgre-limposition-de-tarifs-sur-la-majeure-partie-du-monde-les-etats-unis-ont-perdu-75-000-emplois-manufacturiers-depuis-linvestiture-de-trump/). The [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/a-stark-warning-for-centrists/) cites analysis by the Economic Innovation Group confirming that nearly every manufacturing subsector has shed jobs since the post-COVID peak. The limited exceptions — electrical components (+9.1% since 2019) and transportation equipment (+5.8%) — have bucked the trend, while food and beverage manufacturing saw gains reverse in 2025, partly attributed to Chinese retaliatory tariffs and immigration enforcement affecting migrant-labour-dependent industries.

**Panama Canal begins cutting Panamax capacity as El Niño drains Lake Gatún**
Starting today, the Panama Canal Authority is reducing daily Panamax lock reservations from 36 to 34 vessels — a 5.6% capacity cut — due to declining water levels in Lake Gatún, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/25/le-canal-de-panama-prochain-goulot-detranglement-perturbe-apres-le-detroit-dormuz-et-de-bab-el-mandeb/) reports. The El Niño phenomenon, which the US Climate Prediction Centre warns could be the strongest in 75 years, is drying out the lake that feeds the canal's locks; during the last major El Niño episode in 2023–24, daily transits fell from a peak of 35 to just 18. Larger vessels transiting through the Neopanamax locks — including LNG tankers — are not affected for now. The announcement compounds existing disruptions to global maritime trade from Iranian operations in the Strait of Hormuz and Houthi attacks in the Red Sea; the canal accounts for 5–6% of world maritime commerce and is growing in importance for US LNG exports.

## Foreign Policy

**US–Iran conflict intensifies: 13 consecutive nights of strikes, Netanyahu to Washington**
The conflict between the United States and Iran has entered a new phase of escalation, with the US military having conducted 13 consecutive nights of airstrikes on Iranian territory as of Thursday — targeting command centres, drone storage facilities, coastal surveillance networks, and maritime assets — according to [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/24/vers-une-nouvelle-campagne-israelo-americaine-contre-liran/). In retaliation, Tehran on Friday launched drones and missiles against US targets in Kuwait, Bahrain, and Jordan. Strait of Hormuz crossings have collapsed to an average of 13 vessels per day since 7 July, down 70% from the 45 daily crossings recorded in the previous month, according to data from Kpler cited by Le Grand Continent. Trump has threatened Iran with "an attack larger than ever", and is due to receive Prime Minister Benyamin Netanyahu at the White House on Tuesday 28 July. Options under consideration reportedly include a joint military campaign with Israel modelled on Operation Epic Fury. The US has reinforced its Landstuhl military medical centre in Germany with 150 additional personnel, and has deployed additional fighter jets and refuelling aircraft to the Middle East from European bases. Iran has denied a *New York Times* report claiming it rejected a US proposal for a provisional agreement; neither side has signalled readiness to return to diplomacy.

**Houthi missiles targeting Saudi oil facilities intercepted over Yanbu**
Air defence systems in Saudi Arabia intercepted two ballistic missiles launched from Yemen on Saturday, targeting oil refineries in Yanbu on the Red Sea, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com) reports. Greek-operated systems carried out the intercept, according to security sources. The attack follows fresh US airstrikes on Iran and comes as the Houthis have separately announced the blockade of Saudi Red Sea ports. A Saudi vessel sustained minor hull damage in a separate Red Sea attack, Saudi state media reported.

**Russia's funeral industry surges as war losses mount**
The number of funeral parlours in Russia increased by more than 20% in the first half of 2026, according to data cited by [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/24/economie-russe-le-nombre-de-pompes-funebres-a-augmente-de-plus-de-20-au-premier-semestre/), reflecting the scale of military casualties sustained by the Russian army in Ukraine. The statistic joins a growing body of indirect economic indicators — including a sharp rise in inheritance cases opened for Russian men aged 20–24 — pointing to losses far exceeding official Russian figures.
