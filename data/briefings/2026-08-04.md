# Daily Briefing — Tuesday, 4 August 2026

## EU Affairs

**EU home affairs ministers meet in emergency session as Madrid refuses to shoulder the blame for Ceuta**
The twenty-seven interior ministers convened by videoconference at 10 a.m. today to confront the aftermath of last week's mass crossing into Spain's North African exclave, with Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner attending. According to the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/the-spain-blame-game/), Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares stressed that Madrid itself requested the talks in order to "remind all EU member states that without solidarity, there is no Europe", after twenty-two governments signed a letter at the weekend attacking Spain's migration policy and several called for its suspension from Schengen. Preparatory talks at Coreper on Monday ran for roughly two and a half hours with what diplomats described as "tense moments": Spain was asked to verify its claims about how many migrants had already returned to Morocco, and [Euractiv's Rapporteur](https://www.euractiv.com/news/migration-spain-braces-for-mass-scolding-at-eu-migration-crisis-meeting/) reports the Spanish ambassador first "harangued his counterparts for half an hour in what one diplomat described as somewhere between a rant and a tantrum" before receiving the pledges of solidarity he had come for. [Politico's Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/eu-searches-for-solidarity-after-ceuta-crisis/) reports, on the basis of eight diplomats from different countries, that the mood among capitals has softened as the immediate crisis eased, and that today's discussion will focus on lessons learned and future instrumentalised migration "rather than assigning blame".

The three sources frame the meeting differently. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/the-spain-blame-game/) presents it as a blame game in which Spain is on the defensive but counter-attacking on solidarity grounds; [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/migration-spain-braces-for-mass-scolding-at-eu-migration-crisis-meeting/) is more sceptical, noting that "ambassadors are diplomatic by definition; ministers are political animals" and warning that the session could still erupt into "another lose-lose clash" depending on how much Giorgia Meloni and Mette Frederiksen need a domestic win; [Politico Playbook Paris](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/playbook-paris/le-risque-du-border-line/) reads the whole episode through French electoral politics.

**Von der Leyen praises Spain's handling of the influx and sets out five border priorities**
Commission President Ursula von der Leyen wrote to Pedro Sánchez on Monday praising the "swift handling" of returns by Spanish and Moroccan authorities, which she said had "successfully prevent[ed] illegal movement onwards to mainland Spain and Europe", and adding: "Migration is a European challenge that requires a European response. And in this, solidarity is fundamental." [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/article/ursula-von-der-leyen-support-spain-ceuta-crisis/) reports she set out five priorities — prevention through cooperation with partner countries, reinforcement of the external border, early-warning systems, dismantling smuggling networks and improving returns — while conceding that "we must do more to further strengthen our borders at critical points", including "the use of physical barriers where needed". The letter was first reported by [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/von-der-leyen-attempts-to-defuse-ceuta-crisis-in-letter-to-sanchez/), whose Brief headlined it "Oil on troubled waters" and framed it sympathetically to Sánchez, stressing that leaders attacked him before the facts were clear and that none of the entrants were transferred across the Strait of Gibraltar to the peninsula. [Euractiv's Rapporteur](https://www.euractiv.com/news/von-der-leyen-attempts-to-defuse-ceuta-crisis-in-letter-to-sanchez/) was cooler, calling the expression of solidarity "perhaps a little belatedly" delivered. Brunner had told Spanish Interior Minister Fernando Grande-Marlaska on Friday that Frontex members could be deployed to Ceuta; Madrid has repeatedly declined to request an increased Frontex presence, insisting border control is a national competence.

**A note on the figures.** The three sources do not agree on the scale of the crossing or its human cost. [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/eu-searches-for-solidarity-after-ceuta-crisis/) puts the number at around 50,000 people with "dozens" dead; Greek Prime Minister Kyriakos Mitsotakis, in a [Politico op-ed](https://www.politico.eu/article/greece-kyriakos-mitsotakis-eu-tools-weaponized-migration/), cites 72 dead; [Euractiv's Brief](https://www.euractiv.com/opinion/the-brief-oil-on-troubled-waters/) says approximately 60,000 entrants and "at least 88 people died"; [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/02/la-crise-de-ceuta-est-elle-la-premiere-ruee-algorithmique-de-lhistoire/) gives 50,000–60,000 crossings and a toll of 67. Reuters put the number still in the enclave at 3,000–5,000.

**Was Ceuta the first "algorithmic rush"?**
[Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/02/la-crise-de-ceuta-est-elle-la-premiere-ruee-algorithmique-de-lhistoire/) has assembled what is known about the role of social media in the 30–31 July surge, which Spanish authorities now consider largely resolved with roughly 48,000 people already returned to Morocco. Two cumulative rumours circulated on the Moroccan side: a distorted reading of a 29 June Spanish Supreme Court ruling — which held that summary pushbacks do not apply to people intercepted at sea swimming in, who must instead receive the ordinary removal procedure with a lawyer, an interpreter and the right to claim asylum — and an outright fabrication that the border would open "at 10 p.m." on the Wednesday. The best-established organising channel was a Facebook group called "Hraga Ceuta", spotted by the Ceuta local press on 27 July and numbering more than 20,000 members, on which users shared Google Maps screenshots of swimming distances around the Tarajal breakwater, photographs of wetsuits and fins, sea conditions, taxi routes to departure beaches and even a whip-round to buy a kayak; it became inaccessible after the crisis, without it being clear whether Meta or the organisers took it down. Crucially, the publication notes that no reference source has produced any metrics on how the information actually circulated, that the platforms have not responded and that the Commission has taken no action under the Digital Services Act — which makes it impossible to establish the rumour's precise causal role. It sets the episode against the far better quantified "TikTok rush" of September 2024, when one video was viewed more than 500,000 times and Moroccan forces detained 4,455 people near Fnideq in five days, and asks how a rumour with unknown metrics produced ten times as many people. Its answer points to pre-existing demand: half of Morocco's population is under 31, and youth unemployment reached 37.2% in 2025, exceeding 45% in urban areas.

## UK Politics

**Burnham promises metro mayors a share of income tax**
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced one of the broadest reforms of local government finance in decades: from 2028, the mayors of England's combined authorities — Greater Manchester, the West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire and others — will retain a share of the income tax collected in their territory, with a larger share of business rates devolved from 2027 and a tourist tax added. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/03/burnham-e-il-fisco-di-prossimita/) notes the government is calling it "the biggest transfer of powers and resources from Westminster in a generation", and that the United Kingdom is currently among the most fiscally centralised countries in the OECD, with income tax collected entirely by HMRC and no local power over rates. The reform is not a new local levy nor a power to set rates: a portion of receipts that currently flows entirely to the Treasury will remain where it was generated, earmarked for transport, housing, urban regeneration, infrastructure and economic development, with the exact percentage to be set in the autumn Budget. Mario Seminerio's analysis is sceptical on the details — richer areas such as London, Greater Manchester and Cambridgeshire generate far more income tax, equalisation mechanisms have been promised but not designed, tying local revenue to income tax creates an incentive to attract high earners rather than poorer households, and income tax is procyclical, so local budgets would swing more sharply in a downturn. He also notes that Conservative mayors have already said they would return their share to taxpayers as rebates, which is not what Burnham's Labour has in mind, and reads the whole exercise as another instalment of Britain's "Italian" trajectory, in which devolution debates end in a net rise in the tax burden.

## Economy & Markets

**Washington intervenes to buy yen, and sells euros to do it**
On 31 July the US Treasury intervened to buy yen for the first time since 1998, in what [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/03/la-doctrine-bessent-fait-du-dollar-la-monnaie-de-la-vassalisation-heureuse/) calls the largest coordinated foreign exchange operation since the post-Fukushima intervention of 2011. The sequence: on Thursday 30 July, with the yen at its weakest since 1986 at 163 to the dollar, Japan's finance ministry launched the largest single-session intervention in Tokyo's history, estimated at $53–59 billion, while the Bank of Korea intervened in parallel; on Friday the New York Fed bought yen on the Treasury's behalf — not against dollars but against euros — in an amount matching Scott Bessent's handwritten Camp David note, photographed by Reuters, reading "Buy Japanese Yen (JPY) $5-10 bil"; on Monday both governments confirmed the action, with Bessent promising not to "hesitate to participate in further joint interventions" to "correct the substantial undervaluation of the yen". Shahin Vallée's analysis for the publication reads this as a discretionary monetary protection extended only to Washington's most aligned partners: selling euros rather than dollars supports the yen without weakening the dollar, leaves Europe absorbing the appreciation without a seat at the table in what HSBC analysts called a "highly unusual, maybe unprecedented" move, and treats the euro as a mere reserve instrument. The article ties the protection to the asymmetric 2025 trade deals — $550 billion of Japanese investment commitments by 2029 with profit splits running up to 90/10 in America's favour, and $350 billion from Korea — arguing that letting the yen and won slide would have rendered Washington's own "tributaries" insolvent. Trump supplied the political gloss, calling the intervention a "signal of friendship" and telling Fox News that "Japan's been very good to us, with the exception, of course, of Pearl Harbor."

**Russia may be losing close to half its active force in Ukraine each year**
A meticulous reading of the Russian federal budget by the German researcher Janis Kluge, relayed by [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/04/combien-de-soldats-russes-combattent-vraiment-en-ukraine/), estimates Russian forces in Ukraine by dividing budget lines for supplementary pay by the per-soldier amounts — 158,000 roubles a month for mobilised men since a November 2022 decree, and a far smaller 15,000 roubles for contract soldiers. On that basis the mobilised fell from roughly 300,000 in early 2023 to just under 170,000 in 2025 while contract soldiers more than doubled to just over 555,000, taking total strength from under 525,000 in 2023 to nearly 725,000 in 2025. Cross-referencing recruitment bonuses against changes in the stock yields the striking figure: some 180,000 permanent losses between mid-2023 and mid-2024 and around 250,000 between mid-2024 and mid-2025, implying annual attrition rates of 43.3% and then 45.3%. Kluge hedges the result with several conditions, but concludes that if they hold, "it would mean that Russia loses nearly half of its active troops in Ukraine each year" — and notes the paradox that Russian propaganda's own two claims, broadly constant headcount and roughly 400,000 new contract soldiers a year, imply the same thing. Early and still unreliable 2026 data suggest the machine may be stalling: around 525,000 active contract soldiers, 30,000 fewer than in mid-2025, and only 800 contracts a day signed in the first half of the year, half the rate of late 2024, fuelling rumours of a fresh mobilisation after September's elections.

**Washington's shadow over Ceuta**
