# Daily Briefing — Friday, 31 July 2026

## EU Affairs

**Ceuta: Spain's biggest migration crisis in years shakes European unity**

Between 40,000 and 50,000 migrants — the vast majority young Moroccan men — crossed illegally into Ceuta, Spain's autonomous enclave on the North African coast, in a single day on Thursday 30 July, overwhelming the city's normal population of around 80,000. At least 19 people died, according to the [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://thomsonreuters.com), with many arriving by sea, swimming around the breakwater separating Spanish and Moroccan territory. Spain deployed the military to restore order and Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez flew to Ceuta, describing the situation as "an attack, a violation of the territorial integrity of Spain." By Friday morning, more than 48,300 of those who had entered had already returned to Morocco, and a senior EU official confirmed to [Euractiv](https://euractiv.com) that no onward movement to mainland Spain or other Schengen states had been detected.

[Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-the-eus-crisis-room/) and the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) both led with the story, framing it as a flashpoint that has divided European governments sharply along ideological lines. EU Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner offered support through Frontex. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/plus-de-40-000-arrivees-illegales-de-migrants-et-19-morts-que-se-passe-t-il-a-ceuta-lexclave-espagnole-frontaliere-avec-le-maroc/) notes that the surge is four to five times larger than the 2021 crisis, and that Spanish security forces had reported "unusual passivity" from Moroccan gendarmes who normally collaborate with Spanish Civil Guards — strongly suggesting Rabat either permitted or facilitated the movement.

The causes remain disputed. [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) points to the likely absence of Moroccan border guards as a possible power play by Rabat, potentially in retaliation for Sánchez's visit to Algeria — Morocco's regional rival — on 20 July. However, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/pourquoi-ceuta-nest-pas-une-crise-migratoire-pour-leurope/) counters this theory in a detailed interview with Patrick Weil (author of *De l'immigration en France*, Grasset 2026): Weil argues that the crisis fell on Morocco's national Throne Day — King Mohammed VI's 27th anniversary — making it uniquely embarrassing for Rabat, and that Morocco's embassy blamed "criminal organisations." Weil also contends the episode should not be read as evidence of a European migration crisis: Frontex recorded just 178,000 irregular crossings across all EU external borders in 2025 — the lowest since 2021. "This is not a submersion. It is a geopolitically structural situation," he told Le Grand Continent. A further factor cited by [Euractiv's Rapporteur](https://euractiv.com) and by Weil was a Spanish Supreme Court ruling of 8 July barring immediate returns of migrants arriving by sea at Ceuta and Melilla, which is said to have sent a signal — widely circulated on social media — that this was the moment to attempt the crossing.

**Europe's right turns Ceuta into a Schengen battle — but Sánchez finds himself isolated**

[Euractiv's Editors' Choice newsletter](https://euractiv.com) is headlined "Europe's Ceuta response exposes Sánchez's isolation," while [the Euractiv Rapporteur](https://euractiv.com) leads with "Meloni exploits Spain's migration crisis." The two framings capture the same dynamic: rather than rallying around Spain, most European governments used the crisis to reprimand Madrid. Italy's Giorgia Meloni threatened Spain's Schengen suspension and Italy announced it was ending free air and sea transport with Spain, with Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani calling the move "a necessary choice." Germany's chancellor Friedrich Merz called on Morocco to return the migrants immediately; Denmark's Mette Frederiksen backed Schengen suspension; Finland accused Madrid of failing to protect the EU's external frontier; Sweden's PM Ulf Kristersson said his government "does not rule out taking the measures required to ensure 2015 does not happen again." France's interior minister Laurent Nuñez ordered increased controls at the Spanish border, partly in response to pressure from Marine Le Pen ahead of next year's presidential election. Austria's migration minister told [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) that Spain had been "handing out entry tickets to the EU" by regularising over a million undocumented migrants. Spain summoned Italy's ambassador; the two countries' foreign ministers sparred publicly online.

**EU launches €30 billion AI gigafactory plan**

The European Commission issued a long-awaited call for tenders to build a network of seven AI "gigafactories" across the EU, backed by €5 billion in EU financing and aiming to attract enough private co-investment to reach a total of €30 billion, as reported by [Euractiv](https://euractiv.com) and [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-the-eus-crisis-room/). Germany, Italy, Spain, Portugal, and Greece are among the partner countries. Four of the seven sites will house 100,000 AI chips each. Euractiv notes that the plan's capacity still lags far behind US AI infrastructure, and that the initiative — more than a year in the making — arrives as environmental concerns about the energy costs of data centres mount. Starting Sunday, a 37-person Commission team will also acquire sweeping new powers to police major AI companies under the EU's AI Act.

**EU weighs shift away from sweeping Russia sanctions packages**

The European Commission declined to confirm whether it would propose a 22nd package of sanctions against Russia, with a spokesperson saying Brussels would "continue to put maximum pressure on Russia's war machine" without specifying a new package. [Euractiv's Rapporteur](https://euractiv.com) reports that Irish foreign minister Helen McEntee told Euractiv the bloc is considering a shift towards "more regular, incremental measures" instead of the current model of large packages that are vulnerable to individual member-state vetoes. High Representative Kaja Kallas, who moved quickly to call for a 21st package after the 20th was agreed in April, has not publicly mentioned a 22nd.

**FIFA's privatisation plan draws Council of Europe rebuke**

UEFA voted unanimously to boycott future World Cups if FIFA proceeds with a plan to sell a stake in its commercial operations to Thrive Capital — whose CEO is Josh Kushner, brother of Jared Kushner — according to [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-the-eus-crisis-room/). Council of Europe Secretary-General Alain Berset told Politico: "Football does not need a shareholder. It needs rules." He plans to launch talks on an integrity framework for the 2030 World Cup when European sports ministers meet in Monaco in November. FIFA said it would proceed with its consultation process, calling reports of its plans "incorrect." EU Sports Commissioner Glenn Micallef suggested the plan could raise questions under EU competition law. Former FIFA president Sepp Blatter told Politico he "completely supports" UEFA's position.

## Italian Politics

**Meloni escalates Spain row, risking a serious bilateral breach**

Italy's response to the Ceuta crisis went considerably further than other European governments'. Giorgia Meloni declared Rome "prepared to take extraordinary measures" and called publicly for Spain's suspension from Schengen, a step with no clear legal basis under EU law, as both [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) and [Euractiv](https://euractiv.com) note. Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani announced Italy was ending free air and sea transport with Spain, calling the Schengen suspension "a necessary choice." Spain subsequently summoned the Italian ambassador; the two foreign ministers' dispute played out publicly online. [Euractiv's Rapporteur](https://euractiv.com) frames the episode as consistent with a pattern: Meloni challenged Sánchez at the last EU leaders' summit over his regularisation scheme, and her government has "particular form for exploiting his woes while boosting his far-right adversaries," while itself having "always been quick to clamour for EU solidarity when faced with inflows of migrants at its own borders."

- *[Euractiv Editors' Choice](https://euractiv.com)*: Denmark's Mette Frederiksen joined Meloni in backing Spain's Schengen suspension, though EU officials confirmed no migrants from Ceuta had moved onward to the Schengen area.

## Economy & Markets

**The Fed stands pat again — and markets punish Warsh's rhetoric**

The US Federal Reserve held interest rates unchanged for the fifth consecutive meeting, keeping Fed Funds in the 3.5–3.75% range, despite all major US inflation readings sitting well above that level. Three regional Fed presidents — Lorie Logan (Dallas), Beth Hammack (Cleveland), and Neel Kashkari (Minneapolis) — dissented in favour of a rate increase. [Phastidio.net](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/30/kevin-warsh-un-parolaio-alla-fed/) offers a sharply critical reading of Fed chair Kevin Warsh's performance: though Warsh promised the Fed "will not waver" in pursuing 2% inflation, he declined to explain why rates remain on hold, falling back on the argument that rising market yields are "doing part of the work" of a rate rise. Markets were unconvinced: yields rose across the entire curve, with the steepest moves at the long end, a signal Mario Seminerio of Phastidio interprets as a "thumbs-down" on Warsh's credibility. Seminerio warns the episode risks echoing the "transitory inflation" misstep of 2021–22, and notes that Trump has already started suggesting Warsh wants to cut rates but is being blocked — laying the groundwork, Seminerio argues, for an "autumn subversive scenario."

**Apple warns on supply constraints; Amazon posts strongest cloud growth in four years**

Apple warned that supply shortages would slow revenue growth in the current quarter, sending shares down 7.3% before the open on Friday, according to [Reuters Business](https://reuters.com) and the [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://thomsonreuters.com). The shortfall raises questions about iPhone demand in the face of an expected price increase. In contrast, Amazon reported its strongest cloud growth in more than four years and raised its annual capital expenditure forecast, citing AI-driven demand. The [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://thomsonreuters.com) also notes that nuclear firm Westinghouse confidentially filed for a US IPO, and that ExxonMobil missed its second-quarter profit estimates.

**Bank of Japan holds but signals further rate hikes**

The Bank of Japan kept interest rates on hold but warned for the first time that underlying inflation could exceed its 2% target, a signal — as reported by [Thomson Reuters](https://thomsonreuters.com) — of further tightening to come in the wake of the government's yen-buying intervention.

**BP launches North Sea sale process**

BP formally opened a sale process for its North Sea business under new CEO Meg O'Neill, as part of a broader portfolio overhaul aimed at cutting debt, per [Thomson Reuters](https://thomsonreuters.com).

## Foreign Policy

**Trump announces Hamas disarmament framework; Hamas and Israel both sceptical**

President Trump announced Thursday on Truth Social that his "Board of Peace" had struck a deal for Hamas to disarm and relinquish governance in Gaza, calling it "a monumental step toward lasting PEACE and SECURITY." A Hamas official described the deal as a draft still under negotiation, and [Reuters](https://reuters.com) and the [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://thomsonreuters.com) both note that US officials acknowledged Israel remained sceptical that Hamas would surrender its weapons. The White House published a framework requiring Hamas to disarm and withdraw from governance while Israel withdraws its military forces from Gaza. [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) describes the announcement but emphasises the "series of significant commitments" whose fulfilment remains uncertain.

**Russia prepares — and dreads — a new mobilisation wave**

[Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/les-russes-se-preparent-a-une-nouvelle-mobilisation-et-cherchent-a-leviter/) reports a surge in anxiety inside Russia about a potential new mobilisation. Yandex search data shows queries for "mobilisation" hit record levels in July, matching the peak of autumn 2022; the human rights group "Go Through the Forest" ("Идите Лесом") is receiving up to 200 daily requests for help leaving the country or the front — a sharp recent increase. The Duma has passed legislation allowing convicts to sign defence ministry contracts during periods of "official mobilisation," while sources cited by The Moscow Times report that training grounds for a major new recruitment drive are being prepared for October. Meanwhile, new contract signings by the Russian army fell in the first half of 2026 to roughly 800 per day — barely half the rate of late 2024.

**Russia's Olympic return: a Donbas concession that sparked a domestic scandal**

The Russian Olympic Committee was reinstated after Moscow quietly acknowledged that its NOC no longer covers the four Ukrainian oblasts it annexed in 2022 — Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, and Kherson — allowing Russia to return to the games in 2028. [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ceuta-crisis-rattles-european-capitals/) reports that the concession has provoked a domestic scandal among pro-war commentators, who see it as a betrayal of Putin's territorial claims. The Kremlin has celebrated the reinstatement as a diplomatic victory, but influential hawkish voices described it as a surrender.

**Drone strike at Egypt's Damietta port raises Suez security concerns**

A drone struck two gas vessels at Damietta, Egypt, near the Suez Canal, according to the [Reuters Iran Briefing](https://reuters.com), raising fresh concerns about the security of Red Sea and Suez oil exports. Egypt confirmed the fire.

**Houthis strike Saudi Arabia from Iraqi territory**

Assessments by Saudi Arabia and regional partners indicate that Houthi forces attacked Saudi Arabia this week using Iraqi territory and in coordination with Iraqi armed groups, according to the [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://thomsonreuters.com) — reflecting deepening coordination among Iran-aligned militias across the region.
