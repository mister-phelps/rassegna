## EU Affairs

**The EU as a bloc in name only: methane regulation, Russia sanctions, and drug pricing all expose fractures**
A sweeping analysis by [Phastidio.net](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/20/ue-un-blocco-in-frantumi/) lays out the multiple fault lines simultaneously straining EU cohesion. On the methane regulation front, a rule entering into force for imports in 2027–28 requires energy importers to verify the emissions profile of imported gas across the entire supply chain — a standard that is technically impossible to meet for US LNG, where gas from thousands of producers is commingled in pipelines before reaching liquefaction terminals. The US, Qatar, Algeria and Nigeria jointly wrote to Brussels requesting a delay and the removal of unworkable traceability requirements; at the same time, Germany, Italy, the Netherlands, the Czech Republic, Slovakia and others have requested suspension from within the Union, fearing supply constraints. According to [Phastidio.net](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/20/ue-un-blocco-in-frantumi/), the Commission appears inclined to salvage the architecture of the regulation while gutting its substance through transitional guidance — saving the principle by abandoning its effect. On Russia sanctions, a growing number of member states — Greece (seeking protection for the Dynagas shipping group, which transported €24 billion of Russian LNG since the invasion), Austria (seeking asset offsets for Raiffeisen), Germany and Portugal (seeking exemptions on Russian fish imports), and Italy and France (seeking relaxed military visa restrictions) — are blocking a new package. The piece argues that voter fatigue with the sanctions regime is becoming a structural constraint on EU foreign policy. Meanwhile, US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer has launched a Section 301 investigation into Germany's pharmaceutical pricing, with a September deadline that could yield new tariffs, and has opened parallel "negotiations" with France on the same issue.

**Germany's chaotic mid-summer cabinet reshuffle weakens Merz ahead of autumn elections**
As [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/25/a-berlin-un-remaniement-ministeriel-chaotique-en-plein-ete/) reports, the German government has undergone a disorderly reshuffle triggered by the resignation on 19 July of Jens Spahn — CDU parliamentary group leader and previously Merz's most influential internal ally — after it emerged that Spahn and his partner had used surrogacy abroad, circumventing Germany's legal prohibition on the practice and the CDU's own social doctrine. The replacement chain has attracted sharp criticism: Thorsten Frei (Chancellery chief) moves to Spahn's legislative role; Nina Warken (Health minister) takes over the Chancellery; and CDU/CSU Secretary-General Carsten Linnemann — who had previously refused the Health portfolio at the time of government formation — has now been installed there, widely read as an "exfiltration" designed to move him away from the autumn campaign he was expected to manage. The dismissal of Transport minister Patrick Schnieder compounded the damage: Schnieder was informed of his removal by SMS, and his designated successor Fritz Güntzler refused the job overnight, leaving Merz without a confirmed replacement and triggering public rebukes from former minister Peter Altmaier and from the Rhineland-Palatinate *Ministerpräsident* Gordon Schnieder, the outgoing minister's brother. The CDU faces three difficult regional elections this autumn in Saxony-Anhalt, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern and Berlin.

## Italian Politics

**Italy's income tax has quietly become a wage subsidy — and the bill falls on the middle class**
Italy's Parliamentary Budget Office (UPB) has published a working note on wages and personal income tax (IRPEF), the findings of which are detailed by [Phastidio.net](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/22/il-phastidioso-upb-e-i-kulaki-spremuti-e-drenati/). The headline figure is a threshold of €35,100 per year (at 2026 prices): above that level, today's IRPEF is *more* burdensome than it was in 1990. Below it — where roughly 84% of employees fall — three decades of bonuses, deductions and contribution relief have made the tax system significantly more favourable. Italian real gross wages per full-time equivalent employee fell 1.6% in real terms between 1990 and 2024, against increases of over 30% in France and Germany and nearly 50% in the United Kingdom and United States. According to [Phastidio.net](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/22/il-phastidioso-upb-e-i-kulaki-spremuti-e-drenati/), the UPB's data show that increased wage inequality is almost entirely a composition effect of involuntary part-time work (which rose from 4% to over 30% of contracts since 1990), not a growing skills premium. The conclusion is stark: IRPEF has ceased to be a redistributive instrument and has become a substitute for wage policy, with the fiscal cost absorbed through fiscal drag on incomes above the threshold. The UPB notes that the model may have reached its limits, since it addresses the symptoms of wage stagnation — productivity, labour market quality, collective bargaining — rather than the causes.

## UK Politics

**Andy Burnham's 'Manchesterism' doctrine takes shape as he settles into Downing Street**
[Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/21/quest-ce-que-le-manchesterisme-la-doctrine-andy-burnham/) publishes a detailed analysis of the governing philosophy of Andy Burnham, who replaced Keir Starmer as Labour leader and Prime Minister after Starmer resigned on 22 June following a disastrous performance in local elections — with working-class older voters defecting to Reform UK and liberal middle-class voters shifting to the Greens and nationalists. Burnham — previously Mayor of Greater Manchester from 2017 to 2026, and only elected to the Commons in the Makerfield by-election on 18 June — was confirmed as party leader with the support of approximately 95% of Labour's 403 MPs and eleven affiliated trade unions. His "Manchesterism" doctrine rests on three pillars: radical decentralisation of political power from Whitehall to combined authorities on the Manchester model (including bus franchising, health and social care budgets, and territorial industrial strategy); public ownership of water, energy and transport; and what Burnham describes as the "end of neoliberalism" combined with a "business-friendly socialism". In his first programmatic speech at the People's History Museum in Manchester on 29 June, he pledged "good growth in every postcode and hope in every heart." The author, Karel Williams, is sceptical of whether decentralisation can be delivered at national scale in a political system still strongly centred on the Treasury.
