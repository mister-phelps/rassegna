# Daily Briefing — Monday, 28 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Leaked German cable shows EU capitals hopelessly deadlocked on the next long-term budget**
A German diplomatic cable dated 22 September, obtained by [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-europes-deadlocked-budget-talks/), records that "no potential areas for compromise were apparent" during a recent meeting of Europe ministers on the Multiannual Financial Framework, and that bilateral talks have produced "no surprises" beyond entrenched positions. Germany, the Netherlands, Austria, Denmark, Finland, Sweden and Belgium pressed for significant cuts, while France was open only to "targeted" ones and joined those ruling out any reduction to agriculture and cohesion — an awkward split given the joint Franco-German negotiating stance announced in July. Ireland, which holds the rotating presidency, must produce its next "negobox" by early October ahead of the leaders' summit on 15 October, a task the cable's author calls a "delicate balancing act". [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-europes-deadlocked-budget-talks/) notes that the weakened political positions of Friedrich Merz and Emmanuel Macron make the concessions required from both capitals less likely, and that a Paris–Berlin pre-deal is a precondition for any agreement.

**Defence ministers take up von der Leyen's "Emergency Security Protocol" for the first time**
EU defence ministers meeting in Brussels today will consider whether the bloc needs its own version of NATO's Article 4 to respond to Russian hybrid attacks, according to three EU diplomats and officials cited by [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-europes-deadlocked-budget-talks/). A senior EU official said that with general agreement the bloc could offer emergency response teams, coordinated communications and "pockets of money". Writing in [POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/paying-the-price-of-trumps-war/), Nicholas Vinocur frames the proposal as a response to the discovery of an explosives-laden drone at Leipzig airport and to doubts about Washington: "Given Trump's aversion to conflict with Russia, there's concern that the U.S. would push back against an official, alliance-wide response to a hybrid attack." He also identifies the two obstacles — member states' unwillingness to cede defence prerogatives to Brussels, and the risk of undermining NATO. High Representative Kaja Kallas is separately expected to press governments to hand Patriot interceptors to Ukraine and to contribute ships to the ASPIDES mission.

**The EPP opens its 2029 anti-far-right strategy divided over what the answer is**
The European People's Party has begun preparing for the 2029 European election, but interviews and internal records show deep splits over how to handle the populist right, [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/europes-divided-conservatives-launch-2029-plan-to-beat-far-right/) reports. Party vice-chair Siegfried Mureșan casts the contest as "anti-European populists against the EPP parties", while Finnish MEP Sirpa Pietikäinen puts the underlying question more sharply: "are we center-right or are we another right-wing party?" [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-europes-deadlocked-budget-talks/) frames the Toulouse political assembly as the informal start of Manfred Weber's "Win2029" task force, which runs on to a retreat in Sicily in October and the Rome congress in spring; [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) emphasises the concrete policy being pushed there, reporting that Merz's CDU will seek tougher migration measures after a bruising run of regional election results. Under a CDU amendment to a Spanish Partido Popular resolution on Ceuta, migrants could be returned without an individual asylum procedure during a "hybrid attack"; a second amendment would move Frontex's fundamental rights observers to the Fundamental Rights Agency in Vienna.

**Brussels drafts accession "roadmaps" for the four enlargement frontrunners**
The Commission is developing proposals that would give Montenegro, Ukraine, Moldova and Albania targeted roadmaps to membership, according to a scoop by [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-membership-action-plans-montenegro-ukraine-moldova-albania/). A forthcoming landmark review is expected to conclude that rules preventing new members from backsliding can be introduced without amending the bloc's fundamental laws.

**The "Made in EU" perimeter turns into a fight over where Europe's supply chains end**
[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/28/siamo-sovrani-fateci-entrare/) reports that the Industrial Accelerator Act presented by the Commission in March, which introduces Made in EU and low-emissions requirements in public procurement and subsidies for steel, cement, aluminium, cars and net-zero technologies, has opened a dispute over the perimeter. France wants it confined as closely as possible to the Union and the single market; Germany, invoking its free-trade tradition, wants Turkey included and prefers to speak of "Made with EU" — Ankara has been in a customs union with the EU since 1996, and German–Turkish goods trade was worth roughly $52 billion in 2025. Phastidio notes the risk Berlin is accepting: Chinese producers have established themselves in Turkey, which could become another bridgehead into the single market. It also flags Ursula von der Leyen's reference to an "associate membership" for Mark Carney's Canada as legally non-existent.

## Italian Politics

**Rome joins the Europe-wide scramble to subsidise fuel**
Italy's state-controlled Eni announced on Friday that it would cap pump prices for the next month, [POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/paying-the-price-of-trumps-war/) reports, citing [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/italys-eni-caps-fuel-prices-meloni-struggles-tame-rising-costs-2026-09-25/). Giorgia Meloni has already cut diesel duty by €2.8 billion and this month announced plans to scrap road tax for 14.5 million cars and motorbikes. POLITICO places the measures in a continental pattern — German lawmakers approved a second temporary fuel tax cut this year on Friday at a cost of €2.5 billion to federal and state budgets, Spain has doubled its summer diesel cuts, and France is offering €100-a-head payments to commuters — and notes that fuel prices are already central to next year's Italian, French and Spanish election campaigns.

**Italy leads the push against an EU-wide gambling levy**
Malta and Italy are leading opposition to a proposed EU gambling tax that some estimates suggest could raise up to €5 billion a year, according to a senior EU official cited by [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/inside-europes-deadlocked-budget-talks/). The idea, floated as a way out of the budget deadlock, is being championed by Paddy Power founder Stewart Kenny, who told POLITICO that if bookmakers "have to pay for the harm some of their products are causing … well, that is a very healthy thing." It falls to Ireland to decide whether the levy makes the shortlist of own resources put to leaders in October.

## UK Politics

**Counter-terrorism police question five men over a foiled plot at RAF Fairford**
Five men arrested in the early hours of Sunday near RAF Fairford, the Gloucestershire base used by US long-range bombers to strike Iran, remain in custody on suspicion of terrorism and explosives offences, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/uk-police-question-five-men-held-airbase-used-by-us-attack-iran-2026-09-28/) reports; about 85 households in nearby Whelford were evacuated while bomb disposal teams examined three vans. [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/crime/article/whelford-explosives-bomb-disposal-evacuated-gloucestershire-06q9z2nmx) reports that the plot was thwarted only because a farmer driving home late saw a group of balaclava-clad men some 300 metres from the perimeter and called 999, and emphasises that the operation appeared sophisticated and caught the police and security services by surprise despite general intelligence about a threat to military bases. The Times also notes that the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps warned in July that Fairford was a "legitimate target" over its use to stage strikes on the Strait of Hormuz. [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/major-incident-uk-air-base-us-forces-raf-fairford/) and [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) both lead on the counter-terrorism investigation into possible Iranian involvement; Donald Trump said the suspects "were looking to do big damage … We had them under view for a long time, and we got 'em."

**Labour conference opens with the chancellor boxed in by fuel prices**
Finance Minister John Healey sets out his economic agenda today in the first major speech of Labour's conference in Liverpool, where [POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/paying-the-price-of-trumps-war/) reports his room for manoeuvre is tightly constrained by rising interest rates and the debt stock. Motorists' groups want the previous Conservative government's temporary fuel duty cuts made permanent, and rural Labour MPs want targeted support. Healey told [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/chancellor-john-healey-fuel-duty-petrol-prices-v5vpqtqtb) he is "conscious of these pressures" and will "do what we can to offer people a bit of breathing space". Separately, [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/andy-burnham-pledge-national-care-service-j72p53mqf) reports ministers are expected to examine scrapping the pensions triple lock as a trade-off to fund free social care for the elderly. Prime Minister Andy Burnham missed the traditional conference football match on Sunday, having been called back to work by the Fairford plot.

**Burnham asks Brussels to treat Britain as European while promising to Buy British at home**
[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/28/siamo-sovrani-fateci-entrare/) reports that Burnham is pressing the EU to recognise the UK as a "trusted partner" inside the Made in EU perimeter — UK–EU automotive trade alone is worth roughly €80 billion a year, with supply chains crossing the Channel continuously — while facing domestic pressure to use the roughly £400 billion of annual public procurement as industrial policy. From October, public projects above £10 million or using at least 500 tonnes of steel must record whether the steel was UK-made and, if not, why not. Phastidio notes the constraint: a 2019 European study estimated 84% of UK public purchasing already went to domestic suppliers and only 2% directly to foreign firms, limiting the macroeconomic gains available from substitution, and argues London is discovering it can be "British enough to be excluded from a European preference and European enough to need it".

## Economy & Markets

**Washington and Beijing cut tariffs on $60 billion of goods**
China and the United States have agreed to reduce tariffs on $60 billion worth of goods traded between them, covering products from US corn and cosmetics to Chinese household appliances and toys, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/china-says-us-trade-truce-extension-creates-space-advance-talks-2026-09-28/) reports. Beijing framed the extension of the trade truce as creating space to advance talks. **Oil at $107 as Hormuz flows recover to two-thirds of pre-war levels**
Brent is trading at $107 a barrel, more than 50% above the same period last year, according to [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/la-guerre-en-iran-entre-dans-son-huitieme-mois/). Trump said the US military was facilitating the transit of large volumes through the Strait of Hormuz, and a US official confirmed that 22 million barrels passed through on Friday evening; several experts put current flows at roughly two-thirds of pre-war levels, driven by a surge in Saudi exports, which Bloomberg data show averaged 5.28 million barrels a day over the first 23 days of September — the fastest rate since the conflict began. Le Grand Continent reads the recovery as a sign Tehran's leverage over the waterway is weakening. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/wall-st-futures-drop-oil-spikes-after-trump-rejects-iran-peace-proposal-2026-09-28/) reports Wall Street futures fell and oil spiked after Trump rejected the Iranian peace proposal.

**Russian oil output heads for its lowest level since 2009**
Forecasts presented on 24 September by Economic Development Minister Maxim Reshetnikov put Russian oil production at 494 million tonnes this year, a 3.4% fall on 2025 and the lowest since 2009, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/27/economie-russe-la-production-de-petrole-va-tomber-en-2026-a-son-niveau-le-plus-bas-depuis-2009/) reports. Moscow attributes the decline to "unplanned maintenance work at refineries", in the words of Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak, but Le Grand Continent points to Ukrainian drone strikes: seven refineries have been halted since the start of September, including Gazprom's Moscow refinery and, this week, Rosneft's Kuibyshev plant and Novoshakhtinsk in Rostov. The resulting damage forced a diesel export ban that expires on Wednesday and which the Kremlin is expected to extend. Oil and gas fell to 13% of Russian GDP last year, seven points below 2022.

**German industry stockpiles rare earths ahead of the EU–China talks**
German companies are rushing to build up critical raw material inventories over the risk that Beijing restricts rare earth exports if next month's EU–China trade talks break down, [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/german-industry-hoards-rare-earths-as-brussels-squares-up-to-china/) reports.

## Foreign Policy

**The war in Iran enters its eighth month with no diplomatic exit in sight**
The US–Israeli war against Iran entered its eighth month today, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/la-guerre-en-iran-entre-dans-son-huitieme-mois/) reports, with no resolution in prospect barely a month before US midterms that could cost Republicans both chambers. Tehran had offered, on the margins of the UN General Assembly, to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks within a week in exchange for the US lifting its naval blockade and oil sanctions and observing a region-wide ceasefire; Trump rejected the proposal but told Axios he expects American negotiators to hold fresh talks with Tehran this week. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-insists-diplomatic-solution-after-trump-rejects-peace-plan-2026-09-26/) reports Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi saying mediators have not officially relayed a US rejection, and that Tehran insists only diplomacy can end the conflict. Le Grand Continent quotes Vali Nasr on why a settlement is unlikely: Iran believes it won the war in the first forty days and wants to preserve the memorandum agreed in Pakistan in June, while "the United States is seeking victory, or at least something President Trump can claim as one."

**Allies fear Moscow will test NATO in the Arctic**
Western allies are concerned that Russia could use Norway's remote Arctic archipelago to test NATO militarily amid growing uncertainty over Washington's commitment to European defence, [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/russia-vladimir-putin-is-testing-nato-on-norways-remote-arctic-islands/) reports. The Kremlin, meanwhile, said Ukraine was "paying the price" for its own attacks on Russia and would continue to do so as Moscow's forces systematically strike targets there, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/kremlin-says-ukraine-is-now-paying-price-its-own-attacks-russia-2026-09-28/) reports.

**The Rassemblement national's slow, incomplete break with Moscow**
An investigation by [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/marine-le-pen-peut-elle-vraiment-rompre-avec-poutine/) examines the gap between Marine Le Pen's recent firmness towards Russia and her party's reluctance to keep funding Ukraine. On 19 September, Le Pen and Jordan Bardella issued a joint statement denouncing Russia's placing of French and European subsidiaries under temporary administration as "an act hostile to the interests of France and the nations of Europe", citing the explosives-laden drone at Leipzig and Polish warnings of escalation — language sharp enough to draw a public rebuke from Dmitry Medvedev. Yet the RN voted against the €90 billion European loan to Ukraine in 2026 and abstained in late 2025 on seizing more than €200 billion of Russian assets in Europe, and Le Pen has said cutting financial support would not be "an act of hostility". Le Grand Continent reads the ambivalence less as personal loyalty to Vladimir Putin than as an old ideological fault line running through the French sovereigntist right, now compounded by a generational split inside the party; it also notes the shift in French opinion, with 52% saying in late September they do not trust Volodymyr Zelensky and Ifop finding support for arms deliveries to Kyiv below 50% for the first time in February.
