# Daily Briefing — Wednesday, 23 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Brussels delists two Russian oligarchs, and the sanctions consensus frays**
EU capitals agreed on Tuesday to extend sanctions on Russia-linked individuals and entities for three years while removing billionaires Alisher Usmanov and Mikhail Fridman from the blacklist, following what the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-removes-two-russian-billionaires-from-sanctions-list/) newsletter describes as "a high-stakes diplomatic play by France". According to [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/interview-eu-must-overcome-russia-sanctions-fatigue-says-dombrovskis/), Paris sought Usmanov's removal on national security grounds after more than a week of wrangling, while Luxembourg had long pushed for Fridman's delisting; Latvia, Estonia and Belgium abstained. Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis told Euractiv that Europe must resist a growing "fatigue" over Russia sanctions — "That's exactly what Russia is waiting for" — while conceding that scrapping the unanimity requirement is not "imminently available", since amending the treaties would itself need all 27 capitals. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-removes-two-russian-oligarchs-form-sanctions-blacklist/) reports that Latvia, Estonia and Lithuania are now moving to impose national sanctions on the pair, and that Ukrainian foreign minister Andrii Sybiha called the decision "shameful and unjustifiable". Politico frames the episode as a French diplomatic win; Euractiv emphasises the precedent it sets, noting diplomats' warnings that sanctions talks are becoming a bargaining table for national interests — as when Greece delayed the 21st package in July seeking an LNG shipping exemption.

**The budget fight hardens: Berlin accuses Costa of losing "touch with reality"**
Germany has rejected European Council President António Costa's push for new EU-wide own resources to fund the 2028–34 budget, with Europe Minister Günther Krichbaum telling [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-antonio-costa-eu-budget/): "One has to wonder whether Mr. Costa has completely lost touch with reality by now." [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/germany-sticks-to-deep-eu-budget-cuts/) reports Berlin's demand for several hundred billion euros in cuts is unchanged, with frugal capitals holding the line. The pressure is already reaching external spending: the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/france-opposes-foreign-aid-cuts-eu-budget/) newsletter revealed Commission simulations involving cuts to the Global Europe instrument as high as 40%, prompting French Europe Minister Benjamin Haddad to warn, "Look at the consequences of the cuts of USAID. I think it's been detrimental, including, by the way, to U.S. interests." Renew MEP Barry Andrews called drastic cuts "a betrayal of everything the EU stands for in the world". Writing on [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/21/ue-barriere-dorate-e-tasse-indorate/), Mario Seminerio notes that Costa's own-resources menu — a turnover-based corporate levy, gambling, crypto and digital taxes — leaves the bloc exposed to US retaliation on the last of these, and expects Recovery Fund debts simply to be rolled over.

**A Spanish counterweight to Ribera inside von der Leyen's cabinet**
Ursula von der Leyen has appointed Cristina Lobillo Borrero as senior adviser for the clean transition in her own cabinet, a move the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/brussels-rising-spanish-power-player/) newsletter reports is widely read in Brussels as a counterweight to Executive Vice President Teresa Ribera. Lobillo led the Commission task force to decouple from Russian fossil fuels during the energy crisis and grew close to chief of staff Bjoern Seibert; an EPP lawmaker, granted anonymity, said she gives the president "a direct line to Spain's center-right at the heart of the Commission" ahead of Spain's 2027 general election, in which the People's Party leads the polls. Commission spokesperson Paula Pinho said only that Lobillo was chosen as "one of the most respected and senior energy officials" in the institution. Ribera, for her part, told Politico: "We have different profiles… I trust her." One Commission official argued that being in von der Leyen's direct line of command "makes her the most influential Spanish official in Brussels".

**Kallas's diplomatic service faces being cut to the skeleton**
The European External Action Service is expected to survive the Commission's coming reorganisation but may emerge severely diminished, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/rapporteur-one-eu-budget-three-scenarios/) reports. The EEAS will keep formal responsibility for the EU's 145 delegations worldwide, though how many stay fully staffed depends on the 2028–34 budget talks; one official involved said external relations would be among the first areas cut. "EEAS will be a skeleton within the next two years," a Commission official told Euractiv. Diplomats cited German-led pressure to curb administrative costs alongside tensions with von der Leyen over control of foreign policy, with one adding that "Kallas did not help herself either", a reference to gaffes including her [comparison of Israel to apartheid-era South Africa](https://www.euractiv.com/news/exclusive-kallas-israel-apartheid-remarks-deepen-eu-foreign-policy-crisis/).

**Macron asks Brussels to relax fuel rules as the energy squeeze bites**
Emmanuel Macron has written to von der Leyen asking the EU to temporarily ease fuel quality standards to lift European diesel and jet fuel output, warning of a looming shortage. Per [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/macron-proposes-easing-fuel-quality-rules-in-face-of-crisis/), the French president argues the "exceptional and temporary" change could raise refinery output by between 5% and 20%; he also proposed raising the permitted biofuel share in B7 diesel and delaying methane reporting rules. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/macron-asks-von-der-leyen-to-ease-fuel-rules-to-tame-rising-prices/) reports French ministers unveiled domestic measures to relieve pain at the pump the same afternoon. The pressure is not confined to Paris: [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/rapporteur-one-eu-budget-three-scenarios/) reports Slovak PM Robert Fico has demanded an EU leaders' summit on the oil crisis, with pump prices approaching €2 a litre and his government weighing a deal with dominant refiner Slovnaft.

**Canfin: Europe must learn to say no to China**
In a doctrine piece for [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/22/leurope-doit-apprendre-a-dire-non-a-la-chine/), MEP Pascal Canfin argues the EU must convert the single market into an instrument of power before the October deadline in the Šefčovič–Wang Wentao consultations and the European Council of 15–16 October. He notes every member state ran a goods deficit with China in 2025, totalling €360 billion against just €21 billion in services, and cites an ECB estimate that 29 million European jobs — 27% of euro-area employment — are exposed to Chinese competition. China now accounts for 37% of world manufactures, over half of global steel and 80% of solar panels, with the OECD attributing 60% of its market-share gains over twenty years to subsidies rather than productivity. Canfin calls for scrapping the lesser-duty rule, creating "un équivalent européen de la Section 301 américaine", and tightening the Industrial Accelerator Act's "Made in Europe" definition from 80 trade partners to concentric circles. The definitional fight is already live: [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/sweden-targets-putins-frozen-cash/) reports the EPP is split, with German members favouring a more inclusive membership and the Spanish wanting to tighten the criteria.

## Italian Politics

**Italy stays in the excessive deficit procedure, and nobody quite knows where €11bn went**
Revised Istat data confirmed on Tuesday that Italy's 2025 deficit came in at 3.1% of GDP, above the EU ceiling, killing Rome's hopes of an early exit from the excessive deficit procedure. Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti acknowledged the figures "not without regret", telling [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/the-brief-big-guns-eye-up-big-funds/): "Unfortunately, Italy will not exit early this year from the excessive deficit procedure, as we had hoped," while insisting Rome could still leave in 2027. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/sweden-targets-putins-frozen-cash/) frames this as a blow to Giorgia Meloni's pitch as one of Europe's budgetary good pupils, noting Italian growth remains among the lowest in the eurozone and that its debt burden is set to overtake Greece's. Writing on [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/23/la-breccia-a-via-venti-settembre/), Mario Seminerio reconstructs the arithmetic: the Superbonus weighed roughly €8.1 billion against €2 billion forecast, while €2.488 billion in extra capital spending surfaced between April and October 2025, including €1.3 billion of public-private partnerships reclassified onto the balance sheet. Citing Luciano Capone, he concludes the state "spent 0.5 points of GDP without realising it" — some €11 billion, "half a budget", outside the radar of the Treasury and the Ragioneria generale. The practical consequence: while Italy remains in the procedure, the defence and energy flexibility clauses cannot be netted out, potentially locking it in for years.

**Germany's middle class picks up the social insurance bill**
From 2027, the income ceilings for Germany's four social-insurance branches rise sharply, with the increases mostly political rather than mechanical. Writing on [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/22/arriva-il-conto-ai-kulaki-tedeschi/) and drawing on a Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung report of a labour ministry draft, Mario Seminerio notes that a contribution-stabilisation law passed by the Bundestag on 10 July by 318 votes to 284 raises the health-insurance ceiling by an extra €300 a month, while a bill taken up by health minister Carsten Linnemann pegs the long-term-care ceiling to the private-health-insurance access threshold of €77,400 gross. Someone above all three ceilings faces up to €2,915 a year in extra contributions, of which €1,543 falls on the employee alone; a proposed 2028 pension-rate rise to 19.9% would add a further €1,381. The SPD wants an additional solidarity levy on the privately insured, with a general half-point rate rise as the fallback if the row blocks reform. Seminerio's argument is that Berlin is raising the base rather than the rates, and that "l'agnello sacrificale, anche in Germania, sarà la classe media".

**The European Fiscal Board says the rules are enforced by indulgence**
An assessment published on 21 September finds that enforcement of the Stability and Growth Pact depends increasingly on the Commission's discretion, according to [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/21/ue-barriere-dorate-e-tasse-indorate/). Spain escaped excessive deficit scrutiny after invoking a national defence clause even though its 2025 spending overshot the recommended path by 0.4% of GDP with only 0.1% going to defence; Hungary benefited from a broad reading of defence flexibility and Slovenia from a delayed EDP opening. The Board calls for "systematic and impartial" enforcement to restore the rules' credibility. The piece also cites an ECB estimate that internal single-market regulatory barriers are equivalent to a tariff of 45–60% on goods — the backdrop to the Italo-Austrian anti-gold-plating alliance now backed by at least 17 governments, which Seminerio dismisses as "una pia illusione" because gold-plating exists precisely because national sectors lobby for it.

## Foreign Policy

**Trump threatens to "annihilate" Iran, then promises a deal after the midterms**
In a UN General Assembly address running a little over thirty minutes — half the length of last year's — Trump threatened to "annihilate" Iran if no way forward could be found, while simultaneously insisting he expects a peace deal this autumn. [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/22/trump-iran-annihilation-unga-speech-01087902) reports he later said talks with an Iranian delegation had gone "great" and that he was confident of a deal straight after the midterms. Spanish PM Pedro Sánchez, who skipped a UNGA reception with Trump, called the speech "ideological… not backed and supported by facts and data", according to a preview clip of *The Weekly Show with Jon Stewart* cited by [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/brussels-rising-spanish-power-player/). **Washington, Copenhagen and Nuuk sign the Greenland deal — and Europe claims the win**
The US, Denmark and Greenland signed an agreement in New York on Tuesday amending the 1951 defence treaty, ending nearly two years of Trump's pressure on the island. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/trump-denmark-greenland-sign-deal-bid-end-arctic-standoff-2026-09-22/) reports the deal allows a larger US military presence, with [two new US bases](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/us-open-two-new-military-bases-greenland-sources-say-2026-09-21/) to open; [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/donald-trump-signs-fantastic-greenland-security-deal-denmark/) records Trump calling it "fantastic". [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/22/au-groenland-la-strategie-europeenne-pour-resister-a-trump-a-fini-par-payer/), which publishes a full French translation of the text, reads it instead as a substantive win for Copenhagen, the EU and France: it reaffirms "la souveraineté et l'intégrité territoriale du Royaume du Danemark", survives any future Greenlandic independence, and obliges Denmark and Greenland to keep an independent Greenland within NATO. Most of the expanded US access — upgrades at Pituffik, new defence zones at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig, free movement and overflight — was largely available under the 1951 accord, and despite a White House push on oil, uranium and rare earths since January, "la coopération minière" appears once with no binding clause. The substantive economic provision is an investment-screening rule excluding non-NATO, non-EU states from control or significant influence in sensitive sectors, aimed at China and Russia.
