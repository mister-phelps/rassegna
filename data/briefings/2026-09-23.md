# Daily Briefing — Wednesday, 23 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**The multiannual budget enters its endgame, and none of the three routes out is easy**
European affairs ministers met in Brussels on Tuesday for a final round of talks before the Irish presidency circulates a revised negotiating box in early October. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-begins-budget-deal-race-to-beat-le-pen-spectre/) reports the Commission and Council are working to three scenarios, the most optimistic of which still requires a gruelling sequence of summits on 15 October, 13 November and 17 December — with European Council President António Costa potentially keeping leaders locked in for up to five days — followed by a consent vote in a Parliament far more fragmented than the one that took five months to ratify the 2020 deal. A Plan B emergency summit is already pencilled in for late January or early February; failure there would push agreement past spring 2027, [potentially leaving the EU to negotiate with Marine Le Pen in the Élysée](https://www.euractiv.com/news/rapporteur-the-eus-merz-problem/). Ireland's Europe Minister Thomas Byrne, who chaired the General Affairs Council, called the timetable "ambitious" and told ministers to name their three priority spending programmes and say whether they would accept higher national contributions; as the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/how-the-budget-fight-could-hit-the-eus-global-reach/) noted, with the debate livestreamed, candour was unlikely.

**Brussels has modelled a 40 per cent cut to development aid — and read it as a warning shot**
The Commission has internally simulated slashing the €80 billion Global Europe programme by as much as 40 per cent over the seven years from 2028, EU officials and diplomats told the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/how-the-budget-fight-could-hit-the-eus-global-reach/). Because much of the EU's overseas delegation staffing is paid from that envelope, the exercise found hundreds of posts in DG MENA, DG INTPA and DG ENEST would have to go, likely through non-replacement — "if you cut the development budget by 40%, then mechanically you need to reduce jobs by 40%," one official said. Three diplomats described a related DG BUDG presentation to EU ambassadors — showing what happens to other programmes if the ceiling falls while agriculture, cohesion and security are ringfenced — as a "scare tactic" and a "horror scenario" designed to jolt deadlocked capitals rather than a serious proposal. Oxfam's Hanna Saarinen said the EU "cannot claim global leadership while considering cuts of nearly half its budget for international cooperation."

**Germany escalates, accusing Costa of losing "touch with reality"**
Berlin, backed by five other capitals, has become markedly more vocal in demanding cuts. Discussing Costa's proposals for new EU-level own resources, German Europe Minister Gunther Krichbaum said: "One has to wonder whether Mr. Costa has completely lost touch with reality by now," [per Politico's new Brussels Playbook Capitals](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/sweden-targets-putins-frozen-cash/). [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/exclusive-eu-split-over-how-much-to-cut-long-term-budget/) reports Krichbaum insisted Germany's demand for several hundred billion euros in cuts is unchanged. The two outlets frame the pressure differently: Politico presents it as a personal broadside at Costa, while Euractiv situates it in Friedrich Merz's domestic weakness — keeping national contributions down is a red line for an increasingly embattled chancellor facing further regional votes.

**Latvia blocks the rollover of Russia sanctions, putting nearly 3,000 listings at risk**
EU ambassadors resumed talks at 8 a.m. on Tuesday after failing overnight to agree the renewal of designations covering almost 3,000 people and entities, which could otherwise have lapsed at the end of the day. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/latvia-blocks-eu-deal-to-renew-russia-sanctions-listings/) traces the stand-off to France's demand that Russian-Uzbek tycoon Alisher Usmanov be delisted on "national security" grounds — which diplomats link to a possible prisoner exchange involving two French nationals — prompting Slovakia to seek the same for Russian-Israeli billionaire Mikhail Fridman. Paris and Bratislava offered to extend future renewal cycles from six months to three years; Riga refused. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-countries-move-towards-russia-sanctions-deal/) frames the likely landing zone as a straight trade — the two billionaires come off the list in exchange for extending sanctions on hundreds of others — while Euractiv emphasises Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha's judgement that the proposed delistings are "unacceptable."

**Sweden's incoming prime minister: take the frozen Russian assets, and scrap the foreign-policy veto**
Social Democrat leader Magdalena Andersson, poised to return as prime minister at the head of a left-leaning coalition, told [Brussels Playbook Capitals](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/sweden-targets-putins-frozen-cash/) in an exclusive interview that the EU should use Russian assets frozen in Brussels to fund Ukraine: "Take them, use them." For frugal Sweden, she argued, this is "a better way" than enlarging the EU budget; on Belgium's legal objections over seizing assets held by its depository, her answer was blunt — "solve the legal questions." Andersson also called for qualified-majority voting on foreign policy, a reform she intends to push as EU procedures evolve with Ukrainian accession, and criticised outgoing premier Ulf Kristersson for focusing too narrowly on the Baltic while neglecting the global south. "Sweden is back," she said.

**€130 billion for defence and space — but industry wants to know who counts as European**
The European Competitiveness Fund would channel some €130 billion towards defence and space financing in the next budget, but Europe's aerospace and defence industry has written to MEPs warning the money will not build up the bloc's industrial base without a "clear European preference" governing procurement, [Euractiv reports](https://www.euractiv.com/news/the-brief-big-guns-eye-up-big-funds/). The concern follows a US push to replace "European preference" with a "made with Europe" or "made in NATO" formula; notably, the industry letter endorses UK eligibility given its weight in European defence. Pulling the other way, about a dozen member states are circulating an unofficial paper arguing the ECF's defence window should stay open to third countries, [according to Politico's Morning Defense](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/how-the-budget-fight-could-hit-the-eus-global-reach/). The same fault line runs through the EPP, where German members favour a more inclusive "Made in Europe" definition and Spanish members want it tightened.

**Meloni's man in Parliament urges Brussels to prepare for President Le Pen**
EU institutions and the EPP must be ready to work with Marine Le Pen if she wins the French presidency, just as they came to terms with Giorgia Meloni in Italy, ECR co-chair Nicola Procaccini told [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/giorgia-meloni-eu-lieutenant-urges-brussels-to-work-marine-le-pen/). He is untroubled by the National Rally's plan to halve France's EU budget contribution — his group "doesn't believe in a super-giant budget" — and said the "pragmatic, frank, open, honest and coherent" Meloni method would be the best path for Le Pen. Procaccini also urged the EPP to show "courage" and embrace majorities with parties to its right; the EPP maintains it does not negotiate with the far right but merely states its position. ECR lawmakers, he acknowledged, already act as informal mediators with the Patriots for Europe: "there's not a playbook … it happens sometimes, often, but it's not a scheme."

## Italian Politics

**Italy fails to escape the excessive deficit procedure early**
Revised data published on Tuesday confirmed Italy's 2025 budget deficit at 3.1 per cent of GDP, above the EU's 3 per cent ceiling and enough to deny Rome an early exit from the excessive deficit procedure. Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti acknowledged the figures "not without regret," insisting Italy could still leave the procedure in 2027, [as Euractiv reports](https://www.euractiv.com/news/italy-stays-trapped-in-eus-fiscal-straitjacket/). [Politico's Brussels Playbook Capitals](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/sweden-targets-putins-frozen-cash/) sharpens the point Meloni will find hardest: she had sought credit as one of Europe's budgetary good pupils, yet growth remains among the lowest in the eurozone — the Commission forecasts it will be the lowest next year — and Italy's debt burden is set to overtake Greece's. Euractiv frames the setback primarily as a blow to Meloni's re-election bid next year.

## Economy & Markets

**Germany raises welfare contribution ceilings, with the bill falling on upper-middle incomes**
From 2027, German employees earning above the highest of three social insurance thresholds — €8,850 gross a month, or €106,200 a year — will face up to €2,915 a year in extra contributions across employee and employer shares, of which €1,543 falls on the employee, [Phastidio reports](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/22/arriva-il-conto-ai-kulaki-tedeschi/), citing [ministry figures seen by the FAZ](https://www.faz.net/aktuell/wirtschaft/sozialversicherung-bis-zu-2915-euro-hoehere-abgaben-fuer-gutverdiener-201247832.html). Only the first of the three increases is the routine indexation to wage growth: the health insurance ceiling rises by an extra €300 a month under a stabilisation law passed on 10 July by 318 votes to 284, while the long-term care threshold is pegged to the higher income floor governing access to private health insurance, making that exit harder to reach. Mario Seminerio's framing is that Berlin has chosen to widen the contributory base rather than raise headline rates, which targets upper-middle earners precisely because they are numerous enough to close the fiscal gap — and that 2028 already holds a further 1.3-point rise in the pension rate, to 19.9 per cent.

**Diesel at $6.50 a gallon revives the case for a US export ban**
A growing number of Republicans are pressing the White House to suspend fuel exports, with US diesel passing $6.50 a gallon on Tuesday — close to double the roughly $3.50 seen at the start of the year, [Le Grand Continent reports](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/22/face-a-la-hausse-des-prix-trump-va-t-il-suspendre-les-exportations-de-carburant/), citing AAA data. Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry has called for a 90-day diesel export ban, echoed in the Senate by Chuck Grassley; Representative Tim Burchett has tabled bills to bar gasoil exports until January 2027 and to trigger export controls at a $5 national average. Energy Secretary Chris Wright and Interior Secretary Doug Burgum have both signalled the administration is no longer ruling it out. Le Grand Continent's analysis is that any ban would likely backfire: refiners would cut crude throughput, pushing up diesel, petrol and kerosene alike, while global supply tightens further with Gulf output disrupted by the war and the Strait of Hormuz blockade, and Russia poised to extend its own diesel export ban past September. US pump prices for petrol rose 49 per cent between February and September, against 24 per cent on average across the EU.

**Europe responds to the same shock with rule-bending and windfall taxes**
Emmanuel Macron has written to Ursula von der Leyen asking the EU to temporarily relax methane and fuel-composition standards so refineries can raise jet fuel and diesel output by between 5 and 20 per cent, [according to Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/macron-asks-von-der-leyen-to-ease-fuel-rules-to-tame-rising-prices/) and [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/the-brief-big-guns-eye-up-big-funds/); French ministers unveiled domestic measures on pump prices the same afternoon. Prague has gone the other way, proposing a temporary 50 per cent windfall tax on large refiners and fuel producers in 2026, a cap on fuel margins, daily publication of maximum petrol and diesel prices and a temporary cut in diesel excise duty from October. Finance Minister Alena Schillerová said the Czech Republic would act without waiting for an EU-wide solution.

## Foreign Policy

**Trump threatens to "annihilate" Iran, then predicts a deal after the midterms**
In a 38-minute address to the UN General Assembly, Trump mentioned Iran seventeen times — as often as the word "American" — defending the war and calling on all nations to impose "total economic isolation" on Tehran until it halts attacks on merchant shipping, abandons its nuclear ambitions and ends support for terrorism, [Le Grand Continent reports](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/22/les-5-points-a-retenir-du-discours-neo-conservateur-de-trump-aux-nations-unies/). Having threatened to "annihilate the Islamic Republic … and do it quickly," he then said: "I think we'll do a deal, right after the midterm elections." Trump indicated that Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner had held a three-hour meeting with an Iranian delegation on the margins of the summit, [per POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/doomsday-in-new-york/), whose correspondent was in the hall and judged the speech notably shorter and less combative towards Western allies than last year's. Le Grand Continent reads the address as neo-conservative in substance, noting that Trump mentioned Europe only three times and only in relation to its security; Forecast emphasises the mood music, calling Trump's bilateral meetings uncharacteristically "jolly". Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian addresses the Assembly on Wednesday and is expected, in the words of Politico's Nahal Toosi, to "cast Iran as the victim."

**Trump rejects any international framework for AI, on the eve of a UN Security Council briefing**
"The United States totally rejects any attempt to construct a globalist scheme to control the artificial intelligence being spoken of so much right now," Trump told the Assembly, adding that the US leads China "by a lot" and that he would not "stifle growth of anything that's going to be bigger than the Industrial Revolution" ([POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/doomsday-in-new-york/)). [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/22/les-5-points-a-retenir-du-discours-neo-conservateur-de-trump-aux-nations-unies/) notes he dismissed the AI researchers calling for restraint as "the same people who said we'd all be dead in 12 years from global warming," and reads the remarks as deflating expectations ahead of his 24 September summit with Xi. The timing is awkward: OpenAI's Sam Altman and Anthropic's Dario Amodei brief the Security Council on Wednesday to argue for agreed international safety standards, days after 22 countries corralled by Finland's Alexander Stubb signed a joint statement demanding AI stay "under human direction, oversight and control" ([Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/20-countries-urge-to-strenghten-oversight-of-ai-to-keep-it-under-human-control/)). Forecast reports Beijing is equally cool, with a state-backed newspaper casting Amodei's safety essay as an attempt to "curb China's AI development"; Washington gossip now has Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent — who set up an AI safety hotline with Vice Premier He Lifeng over the weekend — as a possible AI czar.

**Washington, Copenhagen and Nuuk sign the Greenland deal**
The United States, Denmark and Greenland signed an agreement in New York on Tuesday amending the 1951 defence treaty, ending nearly two years of American territorial pressure. Trump characterised it as granting the US "permanent control over security and all other needs" ([Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/donald-trump-signs-fantastic-greenland-security-deal-denmark/)), and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/trump-denmark-greenland-sign-deal-bid-end-arctic-standoff-2026-09-22/) reports it allows a larger US military presence, including [two new bases](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/us-open-two-new-military-bases-greenland-sources-say-2026-09-21/) at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig alongside an expanded Pituffik. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/22/au-groenland-la-strategie-europeenne-pour-resister-a-trump-a-fini-par-payer/), which has translated the full text, reads it very differently: as a substantial win for Denmark and the EU, with French backing. The agreement reaffirms Danish sovereignty and Greenlandic self-determination, survives any future independence (which would carry an obligation to remain in NATO), and — despite a White House push since January for access to Greenland's oil, uranium and rare earths — mentions mining cooperation exactly once, with no binding clause. Its most substantive economic provision bars states and investors from outside NATO, NATO partnership and the EU from control, significant influence or access to non-public information in sensitive sectors, a clause aimed squarely at Chinese and Russian investment. It also authorises the "Golden Dome" missile shield and cannot be amended except by mutual consent.

**Zelensky's twenty meetings, and two objectives he may not achieve**
Volodymyr Zelensky packed at least twenty meetings with leaders, US lawmakers and executives into his UNGA schedule and secured an hour with Trump, ahead of his own address on Wednesday afternoon, [POLITICO Forecast reports](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/doomsday-in-new-york/). His aims, per columnist Jamie Dettmer, are a pause in Russian air strikes and a replenishment of Patriot interceptors — "it is doubtful he can pull off either," given US stocks depleted by the Iran war and Moscow's read of Ukraine's position entering another wartime winter. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/zelenskiy-meet-trump-un-push-energy-truce-with-russia-2026-09-22/) frames the meeting around a push for an energy truce; [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/world/russia-ukraine-war/article/trump-ukraine-russia-oil-drone-strikes-fg3hx2dqc) reports the pressure running the other way, with Trump said to have pressed Zelensky to halt Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries. Separately, Ukraine's deputy prime minister for European integration Vsevolod Chentsov told Politico's Brussels Playbook Live that Kyiv is open to a Black Sea grain truce: "Why not? We need to start somewhere, and step by step, eventually we can discuss something more comprehensive" ([Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/ukraine-open-to-grain-truce-with-russia-deputy-pm-vsevolod-chentsov-says/)). Macron, meanwhile, said Trump was "very amenable" to accelerating deliveries of missile interceptors to Ukraine ([Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-begins-budget-deal-race-to-beat-le-pen-spectre/)).
