# Daily Briefing — Sunday, 27 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Europe's refined-fuel exposure becomes the continent's central economic vulnerability**
The Houthi seizure of the port of Mocha and the advance on Perim island on 10–11 September gave the movement de facto control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait, and with the Strait of Hormuz still under Iranian blockade, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/26/bab-el-mandeb-le-controle-houthiste-fait-peser-un-risque-sur-les-approvisionnements-europeens-en-diesel-et-kerosene/) reports that the European Union's weak point is not crude but refined product: before the attacks of late 2023, 66% of EU kerosene imports and 58% of diesel imports travelled routes through the strait, against 17% of crude. Citing a Global Trade Policy Observatory note, the publication records that the Gulf's share of EU diesel imports rose from 2.4% in 2013 to 31.6% last year — a substitution for Russian barrels that swapped one dependency for another, and the Gulf refineries concerned, including Ruwais in the UAE and Mina Abdulla in Kuwait, are precisely those struck by Iran. European refineries cannot make up the shortfall: capacity is down roughly 13% since 2000, seven member states retain no significant capacity, and the medium-sour crudes the plants were built to process have fallen from 31% of EU imports to 6%. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/26/bab-el-mandeb-le-controle-houthiste-fait-peser-un-risque-sur-les-approvisionnements-europeens-en-diesel-et-kerosene/) notes that Washington, under pressure from domestic prices, is now considering restricting or suspending diesel exports — which would remove Europe's main non-Gulf source and strain the transatlantic relationship.

**The diesel shock reaches bond markets and the political agenda**
[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/26/la-settimana-phastidiosa-26-settembre-2026/) frames the same shock primarily as a monetary and fiscal problem: Mario Seminerio argues that bond yields rising "everywhere" are the real global threat, and that inflationary pressure is set to increase through the diesel and refined-products channel, with Republicans in Washington so alarmed by the diesel crisis that the remedy risks being worse than the disease. He also asks how prepared Italian firms — particularly energy-intensive ones — actually are for another energy shock. [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/diesel-world-economy-government-tc7sk8kf7) approaches it from the forecourt, with Ben Spencer explaining why filling up has become so expensive for British motorists. The framings are complementary rather than contradictory: a supply disruption in the Red Sea, a real-yield repricing, and a retail price spike are three faces of one story.

**An anti-gold-plating alliance runs into the logic of national protection**
A group of EU member states wants to attack bureaucratic excess and the additional national rules layered on top of EU legislation, but [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/21/ue-barriere-dorate-e-tasse-indorate/) calls the effort a pious illusion, on the grounds that those national add-ons function as non-tariff barriers protecting domestic interests and will therefore be defended by the same governments nominally signing up to simplification.

## Italian Politics

**Italy stays inside the excessive deficit procedure — and the MEF admits it lost track of €11bn**
Istat has confirmed that Italy remained above the 3% deficit-to-GDP threshold, meaning it does not exit the EU excessive deficit procedure a year early as the government had hoped. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/23/la-breccia-a-via-venti-settembre/) sets out the arithmetic: 2025 GDP was revised up by some €7bn, lifting revenues by just under €2bn, but €2.488bn of previously unrecorded capital spending appeared between April and October 2025 — €700m from Anas and Ferrovie and €1.3bn under public-private partnerships reclassified as "on-balance" — leaving €350m of additional deficit and a ratio just above the line. Quoting Luciano Capone in *Il Foglio*, Seminerio notes that taken together with the April revision of Superbonus costs the combined error is roughly €11bn, "half a budget" spent without the Treasury noticing. The practical consequence is that the flexibility clauses on defence and energy cannot be netted off while Italy remains in the procedure, which risks keeping it there for years; Seminerio also dismisses Giuseppe Conte's call to "unblock" the €13bn earmarked for the Messina bridge, pointing out that the money must still be found before it can be redirected.

## Economy & Markets

**Russian oil output to fall to its lowest level since 2009**
Forecasts presented on 24 September by Russian economic development minister Maxim Reshetnikov put 2026 crude production at 494 million tonnes, a 3.4% fall on last year and the lowest figure since 2009, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/27/economie-russe-la-production-de-petrole-va-tomber-en-2026-a-son-niveau-le-plus-bas-depuis-2009/) reports, with a recovery to 500 million tonnes projected for next year and 505 million in 2028–29. Moscow attributes the decline to "unplanned maintenance work at refineries" in the words of deputy prime minister Alexander Novak, but the publication attributes it principally to Ukrainian drone strikes: seven refineries have been halted since the start of September, including Gazprom's Moscow plant and, this week, Rosneft's Kuibyshev refinery and the Novoshakhtinsk plant in Rostov. The damage forced Russia into a diesel export ban that expires on Wednesday 30 September and which, on Bloomberg reporting cited by Le Grand Continent, the Kremlin is preparing to extend to protect domestic supply. Oil and gas fell to 13% of Russian GDP last year, against 20% in 2022.

**Germany's middle-to-upper earners face a 2027 contribution rise**
[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/22/arriva-il-conto-ai-kulaki-tedeschi/) reports that from 2027 the German increase in income ceilings for welfare contributions will mean a sharp rise in the effective burden on middle and upper-middle incomes, adding further weight to a group already heavily taxed — a fiscal choice with obvious implications for the eurozone's largest economy and its consumption outlook.

## Foreign Policy

**Iran holds to diplomacy after Trump rejects the seven-day plan**
The Reuters Iran Briefing leads on Tehran insisting on a diplomatic solution after Trump's rejection of the peace plan, noting that despite the president's public statements the mediators facilitating US–Iran contacts have not officially relayed a US rejection — leaving the proposal formally alive ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-insists-diplomatic-solution-after-trump-rejects-peace-plan-2026-09-26/)). Foreign minister Araqchi has said it is now for Washington to accept the seven-day plan ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/irans-araqchi-says-now-up-us-accept-7-day-plan-2026-09-25/)), while an Iranian official has ruled out nuclear concessions ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-will-make-no-nuclear-concessions-iranian-official-says-2026-09-25/)) and the Iranian army has declared its readiness for a renewed US attack ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/irans-army-voices-readiness-potential-renewed-us-attack-2026-09-27/)). In an interview with [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/24/en-iran-khamenei-est-en-train-de-gagner-la-guerre/), Vali Nasr offers a sharper framing: Trump and Netanyahu set out to topple the regime and may instead have entrenched the assassinated guide's doctrine.

**Sánchez uses the UN rostrum to attack the demolition of multilateralism**
Speaking to the General Assembly on 24 September, Pedro Sánchez denounced what he called a "planned and coordinated" attempt at demolition of the multilateral order, built on selfishness, ignorance and cruelty, without ever naming Trump — "we all know who they are and what their modus operandi is". [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/26/la-doctrine-sanchez-pour-refonder-lordre-mondial/), which publishes the speech in full, highlights his programme: abolition of the Security Council veto on the principle that no country should be both judge and party, a strengthened International Criminal Court, rebuilt non-proliferation architecture, strict regulation of the "techno-oligarchs", and a Latin American woman as the next secretary-general, which he called a historic debt. Sánchez also restated Spain's refusal to raise military spending to 5% of GDP, its arms embargo on Israel and its characterisation of events in Gaza as genocide. The publication compares the speech's reach to Mark Carney's January address at Davos.
