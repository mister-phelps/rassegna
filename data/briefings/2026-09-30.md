# Daily Briefing — Wednesday, 30 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Western Balkan laggards push back against a two-tier enlargement**
Kosovo and North Macedonia are using Ursula von der Leyen's regional tour to argue they are being relegated to a second tier of candidates, [POLITICO's Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/western-balkan-hopefuls-press-their-case/) reports. The Commission is preparing [more defined accession plans, with indicative timelines, for Albania, Montenegro, Ukraine and Moldova](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-membership-action-plans-montenegro-ukraine-moldova-albania/), leaving the rest without a route to the finish line. Kosovo's prime minister Albin Kurti told Playbook that von der Leyen "can make that case" for candidate status and that any pre-accession scheme "must be a pathway to full EU membership, not a parallel process or a substitute"; North Macedonia's foreign minister Timčo Mucunski said his country deserved "to be in a much more advanced position than we are right now". Playbook undercuts the pitch with an internal Commission assessment estimating that Kosovo has completed only 24–26% of its Growth Plan commitments, and quotes a senior Commission official promising that "the more reforms they do, the more access to EU funds they get". Von der Leyen is in Albania today, meeting President Bajram Begaj and Prime Minister Edi Rama.

**Parliament and Council both stall on the 2028–34 budget, and the net payers want deeper cuts**
The next multiannual financial framework is jamming at both ends. In the Parliament, the Greens' budget negotiator Damian Boeselager told [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/western-balkan-hopefuls-press-their-case/) that turf wars between the budget, agriculture and regional policy committees over the National and Regional Partnership Plans — covering almost half of total spending — mean "we're so far behind that we won't even have a Parliament position by the end of next year if we continue at the same pace", with party leaders meeting today to break the deadlock. On the Council side, four EU diplomats told POLITICO that governments expect a negotiating box [more than €100 billion smaller](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-budget-cuts-100b-farmers-regional-funding-2028-2034/) than June's, with Italy, Spain and Poland lobbying the Irish presidency against the squeeze. [POLITICO's Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/deutschland-sucht-die-super-investoren/) adds the other side of the argument: Friedrich Merz and the heads of government of Austria, Denmark, Sweden, Finland and the Netherlands have written to demand the Commission proposal be "cut by several hundred billion euros", insisting that "all budget headings must contribute to these savings" — pointedly including agriculture.

**Romania's designated prime minister fails his confidence vote**
Siegfried Mureșan, the PNL MEP and EPP vice-president nominated after the fall of the Bolojan government, secured only 182 votes in parliament today against the 233 required, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/30/en-roumanie-le-premier-ministre-siegfried-muresan-na-pas-obtenu-la-confiance-du-parlement/) reports. President Nicușor Dan must now either call early elections — which he has so far ruled out, with the hard-right AUR leading the polls — or nominate a candidate from the PSD, most likely Sorin Grindeanu. AUR, which brought down the previous government jointly with the PSD over its austerity measures, told parliament it will back no government until Călin Georgescu is released; Georgescu was remanded in custody for 30 days yesterday in a fraud and organised crime case. Le Grand Continent notes that with the deficit at 7.9% of GDP in 2025 — the highest in the Union — Bucharest now risks having its sovereign rating cut to junk.

**Antisemitism allegations against Bardella destabilise the National Rally**
An investigation by Mediapart alleges that Jordan Bardella wrote antisemitic messages on social media in 2013, when he was a young activist, including the claim that "the Jew has to dominate other peoples, crush them and rob them". [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/le-pen-faces-fallout-after-bardella-accused-of-antisemitic-remarks/) reports that Bardella denies the claims outright, is taking legal action, and said Mediapart's aim was to "kill [his] political career"; Marine Le Pen, sitting beside him, said the "system" would "do everything — including the most despicable things" to unseat her as presidential front-runner. Pierre Moscovici, among the ministers of Jewish heritage allegedly targeted, told POLITICO that Bardella "will have to demonstrate that all of this is false", and CRIF president Yonathan Arfi wrote that if the details prove true they deal "a crushing blow to the National Rally's strategy of shedding its toxic image". POLITICO frames this as [the Le Pen clan tightening its grip on the party](https://www.politico.eu/article/marine-le-pen-jordan-bardella-national-rally-family-2027-election/) rather than loosening it. Nothing in the reporting indicates independent verification of the 2013 messages.

## Italian Politics

**Vannacci's new party posts a modest first score in Calabria**
Futuro Nazionale, founded by former general Roberto Vannacci after he left the League in February, contested its first election on Sunday and Monday in the Reggio Calabria by-election. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/le-candidat-de-futuro-nazionale-le-parti-du-general-roberto-vannacci-obtiendrait-9-des-voix-en-calabre/) reported its candidate Domenico Giannetta on around 9% on partial counts, behind Forza Italia's Fabio Roscioli on 54% and the centre-left's Francesco Benedetto on 36%; [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/antisemitism-scandal-rocks-le-pens-campaign/) puts the final figure at 10.94%. The two outlets frame it differently: POLITICO calls it "enough for the general to claim a breakthrough but not to threaten" Giorgia Meloni's coalition, and stresses that turnout of 22.34% — against 49.4% in 2022 — "raises questions about whether the result says much about Vannacci's wider appeal". Le Grand Continent is more interested in the structural point, noting that Futuro Nazionale grows at the League's expense and that Meloni now faces organised opposition to her right for the first time. It also records that Vannacci published an AI-generated image of Forza Italia undersecretary Matilde Siracusano the day before polls opened, condemned across the political spectrum.

**Rome's tax tsar wants fewer tax breaks, and proposes new ones**
Ahead of the budget season, deputy minister Maurizio Leo gave the Corriere della Sera interview that [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/29/troppe-tax-expenditures-ne-servono-altre/) treats as a study in self-contradiction. Asked whether the government will cut tax expenditures worth some €110 billion a year across 625 separate items, Leo proposed a round table with all parties and the social partners rather than a plan. He then defended the proliferation of flat taxes — 15% for the self-employed, 12.5% on government bond interest, 26% on financial income, 21% on rents, 5% on collectively bargained pay rises, 1% on productivity bonuses — conceding this "may seem to contradict the principle of progressivity enshrined in the formal constitution", but invoking "a material constitution that must accommodate real situations". Mario Seminerio's verdict is that the measures are "meagre and devoid of economic logic", and that their cost falls on the shrinking base of full income-tax payers. Further flat taxes are floated: 5% on year-on-year pay rises for under-35s, paired with a tax benefit for the employer, and an extension of the flat rental levy to shop leases.

## UK Politics

**Burnham stakes a second term on scrapping the triple lock**
In his first Labour conference speech as prime minister, Andy Burnham pledged to abolish the pensions triple lock from 2030 to fund a national care service free at the point of need, to reopen the Brexit deal, and to reform the voting system. [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/andy-burnham-tears-labour-conference-speech-triple-lock-eu-care-s92fjfj90) puts the saving at £50bn by 2050 and the cost to pensioners at an average of £2,000 each in real terms, and quotes Burnham conceding a "political price" but insisting "someone has to go through the pain barrier and rip the plaster off". He also announced the repeal of Thatcher-era bans on public ownership of water, new council powers to seize substandard rented homes, and a state-owned competitor to National Grid. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/uk/burnham-says-britain-should-weigh-eu-options-including-rejoining-bloc-2026-09-30/) reports him going further on Europe, saying Britain should consider all options for its future relationship with the EU including ultimately rejoining. The Times emphasises the emotional register of the speech and closes on supportive YouGov polling of 4,503 respondents; Reuters treats it as a deliberate escalation of his European strategy.

**London asks Brussels for industrial inclusion while pushing "Buy British" at home**
[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/28/siamo-sovrani-fateci-entrare/) sets Burnham's request that the UK be treated as a "trusted partner" inside the perimeter of the Commission's Industrial Accelerator Act against the domestic pressure on him to favour British suppliers in the roughly £400bn a year the UK public sector spends on procurement. The stakes are concrete: UK–EU automotive trade is worth about €80bn a year, with supply chains crossing the Channel continuously, so excluding British components from the European-content definition would hurt continental manufacturers too. Seminerio notes the same fight inside the EU, with France wanting the perimeter confined to the single market and Germany pressing for Turkey's inclusion under a "Made with EU" formula, given €52bn of German–Turkish goods trade in 2025 and Turkey's role in German automotive supply chains. His scepticism about the British side is sharpened by a 2019 estimate that 84% of UK public purchasing already goes to domestic suppliers: "London is discovering that after Brexit it can be simultaneously British enough to be excluded from a European preference and European enough to need it."

## Economy & Markets

**France's spread with Germany blows out, and nobody is talking about it**
Shahin Vallée, formerly Emmanuel Macron's economic adviser at Bercy, tells [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/30/la-dette-francaise-est-en-train-de-decrocher/) that France has entered a zone of "historic danger" in three weeks: the OAT–Bund spread was under 70 basis points in the spring, around 85bp in late August and now exceeds 110bp, meaning France borrows 1.1% more dearly than Germany. He estimates the move has added the equivalent of €15bn a year in debt-servicing costs over a decade — roughly one and a half times the justice budget — or close to €100bn cumulatively, and warns of a "reflexive snowball effect" as rates exceed nominal growth. The reference point he offers is 211bp in November 2011, at the height of the sovereign debt crisis. "Nobody seems to have grasped the danger zone we are entering." The fiscal backdrop, per [POLITICO](https://pro.politico.eu/news/225221), is French debt of nearly €3.6 trillion in the first half of 2026, or 119% of GDP against 115.2% a year earlier, with Sébastien Lecornu's government due to propose a €54 billion squeeze in the 2027 budget tomorrow.

**Merz courts investors as the Wirtschaftswende stalls**
Friedrich Merz addresses around 70 chief executives today at Siemens's Berlin headquarters at a meeting of the Made for Germany initiative, whose 143 member companies have pledged over €800bn of investment by 2028 — a figure the initiative says has risen only "slightly". [POLITICO's Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/deutschland-sucht-die-super-investoren/) is sceptical, noting that Merz took office promising an economic turnaround and that "there hasn't been much of a turnaround so far". Jens Südekum, chief adviser to Lars Klingbeil, says "private investment is still very weak" despite enormous needs in AI and decarbonisation; the SPD parliamentary group's economic spokesman Sebastian Roloff calls the alliance "sadly not the really big sensation, because most of it is only pledges". ESMT Berlin's Jörg Rocholl says foreign interest remains high but "the honeymoon following the formation of the new government last year is over". The government is trailing an Invest-in-Germany summit in Berlin on 19–20 October, at which investors can book 15-minute slots with ministers via an app; against Google's €5.5bn data-centre commitment and GlobalFoundries' €1.1bn Dresden expansion sit Volkswagen and Mercedes weighing German plant closures.

**An awkward venue for a defence of the trading system**
G20 trade ministers meet today and tomorrow in Milwaukee, which [POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/the-eye-rolling-at-the-end-of-the-world/) calls "something of an awkward locale" given eighteen months of US tariff actions against allies and adversaries alike. USTR Jamieson Greer intends to raise rules against tariff discrimination, manufacturing overcapacity, forced labour in supply chains and the weaponisation of food trade, and Washington wants allies aligned against cheap Chinese steel. POLITICO's Oliver Ward reports that "the ultimate prize — closer alignment on U.S. steel tariffs — will likely be out of reach", though monitoring measures may be possible; Beijing blocked a joint statement at the G20 finance ministers' meeting earlier this month. White House trade counsellor Peter Navarro, meanwhile, told Ottawa its days of special treatment are over: "Memo to Canada: You cannot do that."

## Foreign Policy

**American forces leave Iraq after more than two decades**
[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-forces-exit-iraq-emboldening-irans-proxies-islamic-state-2026-09-30/) reports that US forces have ended their mission in Iraq, "raising fears of a security vacuum that could embolden Iranian-backed Shi'ite militias and allow Islamic State to regroup". The framing is deliberately double-edged: Reuters pairs images of Iraqi security forces celebrating the withdrawal of the US-led coalition with the strategic warning, and runs a companion explainer on [how Washington continues to control Iraq's oil revenues](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/how-us-controls-iraqs-oil-revenues-2026-09-30/) — troops leave, financial leverage remains. The withdrawal comes with the US–Iran war still running; Reuters has published a documentary from aboard the [USS George Washington in the Strait of Hormuz](https://www.reuters.com/investigates/special-report/iran-crisis-uss-george-washington/), drawing on interviews with more than a dozen sailors and flagged by the agency itself as "one side of the Iran war". Mediators made a fresh push this week to advance a peace deal, per [POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/the-eye-rolling-at-the-end-of-the-world/).

**Russia expands its army for the fourth time this year as Berlin gives up on talks**
Vladimir Putin signed a decree on Monday raising the authorised strength of the Russian armed forces to 2,441,630 personnel, of whom 1,550,500 are military — the fourth such increase since March, adding 47,860 military posts in six months, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/30/poutine-augmente-la-taille-de-larmee-russe-pour-la-quatrieme-fois-en-six-mois/) reports. It comes alongside a planned 17.1 trillion rouble (about €178bn) defence budget for 2027, 27% above the sum originally earmarked and 32% up on 2026, and Ukrainian intelligence assessments that Moscow intends to mobilise a further 300,000 combatants by the end of 2027. Against that, Friedrich Merz said after Sergey Lavrov's mocking read-out of his meeting with foreign minister Johann Wadephul that Russia is "obviously not interested at all in serious talks at this point in time", adding that "Russia will not win this war" and that continuing it "will have a price", per [POLITICO](https://www.politico.eu/article/friedrich-merz-sergey-lavrov-russia-not-interested-serious-negotiations/) and [Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/deutschland-sucht-die-super-investoren/).

**Trump's "morally binding" AI pact, and Europe's shrug**
Donald Trump said tech executives signed a voluntary AI safety accord at a White House lunch yesterday, describing it as not legally but "morally binding" and "almost like a constitution", and floating a ten-person industry oversight committee, per [POLITICO](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/29/ai-execs-morally-binding-constitution-01097855); [Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/deutschland-sucht-die-super-investoren/) lists the signatories as Sundar Pichai, Dario Amodei, Mark Zuckerberg, Greg Brockman, Jensen Huang and Elon Musk, with four agreed stages of control, and frames the exercise as self-regulation designed to forestall legislation. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/trump-host-zuckerberg-anthropics-amodei-other-ai-titans-tuesday-2026-09-29/) is blunter, with White House correspondent Steve Holland suggesting the move "may be more about midterm politics than AI guardrails". Trump separately signed an executive order [renaming the technology "super intelligence"](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/29/trump-signs-executive-order-renaming-ai-to-super-intelligence-01098494). [POLITICO Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/the-eye-rolling-at-the-end-of-the-world/) reports European lawmakers largely unmoved by American doom warnings — a YouGov survey found 56% of Britons worried about AI's effect on everyday life against 19% concerned chiefly about human survival — while EU tech chief Henna Virkkunen says Brussels will [keep pushing for global AI safety rules](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-to-trump-we-will-keep-pushing-for-global-ai-safety-rules/) regardless of Washington's position.
