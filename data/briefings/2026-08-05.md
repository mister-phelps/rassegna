# Daily Briefing — Wednesday, 5 August 2026

## EU Affairs

**Europe closes ranks behind Madrid over Ceuta, but the question of Morocco's role remains open**
Interior ministers meeting on Tuesday papered over a week of recrimination, with France's Laurent Nuñez saying he was "rassuré" and Italy's Matteo Piantedosi praising Spain's handling of the crisis — a European diplomat told [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-ministers-back-spain-after-ceuta-migration-crisis/) the Union had pulled off its "couples therapy", per the [Playbook Paris](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/playbook-paris/dans-lombre-dun-aout/) newsletter, which also reported that ministers pressed for better early-warning systems and faster work on return hubs, while Greece proposed a mechanism to suspend asylum procedures outright in extreme situations. Behind the show of unity, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/)'s Rapporteur newsletter reports that Spain lobbied European governments not to criticise Morocco publicly, even as one Western government source said Rabat was believed to have at least tacitly approved the surge as a political warning to Madrid; Brussels is weighing stronger Europol and Frontex powers against disinformation, and the European External Action Service is examining whether Russia played any role. Speaking to the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/the-eus-other-top-diplomat/) Brussels Playbook, Mediterranean Commissioner Dubravka Šuica — who put the death toll at around 75 — insisted the EU and Morocco "remain strong partners" and that one incident should not define the relationship, pointing to cooperation with Tunisia that cut irregular migration by roughly 80 per cent as the model to follow with Rabat. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/05/pourquoi-le-maroc-na-pas-pu-orchestrer-la-crise-de-ceuta/) frames the episode very differently: in an interview with the review, the Sciences Po Rabat sociologist Mehdi Alioua argues Morocco could not have orchestrated it, noting the rush coincided with Throne Day celebrations held this year at Tétouan, a few kilometres from the exclave, amid millions of holidaymakers on the forty-kilometre "strait beaches" and 2.2 million passengers moving through Tangier and Ceuta ports in under three weeks — a state overwhelmed rather than a state at work, with the land border opened in coordination with Spain as the only alternative to a bloodbath, and 48,000 people back in Morocco within two hours. Alioua puts the dead at more than eighty, against Šuica's seventy-five, and traces the trigger to a Spanish Supreme Court ruling on summary returns rather than to Rabat; [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/viral-videos-that-inspired-tens-thousands-swim-spains-ceuta-2026-08-04/) separately reports that viral videos and misinformation turned a movement of a few dozen youths into a mass crossing.

**Madrid on the defensive over the status of its African cities**
The surge has revived Morocco's long-standing claim to Ceuta and Melilla and, as [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/spain-ceuta-migrant-crisis-africa-territories-morocco-melilla/) reports, sharpened a Spanish sense of vulnerability to hybrid attack — compounded by fears that neither Washington nor Brussels is fully dependable given their political feuds with Pedro Sánchez, while Rabat, buoyed by warm relations with Donald Trump, doubles down on its claims in the state-aligned press. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/05/pourquoi-le-maroc-na-pas-pu-orchestrer-la-crise-de-ceuta/)'s interviewee proposes a way out that neither capital has entertained: an Andorra-style Spanish-Moroccan co-sovereignty over both enclaves, with the fences removed and autonomous-city status retained.

**Brussels has doubts about its own case against the AfD's European party**
[Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) reports that legal minds inside the Commission are sceptical about how watertight the attempt to deregister Europe of Sovereign Nations really is. The Berlin-based pan-European party steered by the AfD was targeted in May by the EU watchdog known as "The Authority", which accused it of breaching the bloc's values and moved to strike it off and withdraw its €3 million annual subsidy; the European Parliament voted last month to start the procedure. Two sources told the newsletter that much of the 300-page evidence file submitted by the watchdog's outgoing director Pascal Schonard — largely social-media screengrabs and public statements — predates the entry into force of the rules being invoked, and a Commission spokesperson said no position had yet been taken. The AfD is already using the proceedings to argue it is being silenced, ahead of September elections in Saxony-Anhalt where it is expected to win; should the Authority press ahead, the Council and Parliament would have three months to object.

**A fifth tranche of frozen Russian asset profits goes to Kyiv**
The Commission announced on Wednesday that it had collected €1.4 billion in revenue from immobilised Russian central bank assets and would channel it to Ukraine, according to the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/russia-central-bank-assets-eu-ukraine-war-vladimir-putin/) Forecast newsletter. The payment, received on Monday, is the fifth of its kind; the immobilised assets have generated €8 billion in windfall profits to date. Brussels said 95 per cent of the latest tranche would flow through the Ukraine Loan Cooperation Mechanism to help Kyiv service EU and G7 loans, with the remaining 5 per cent going through the European Peace Facility to meet immediate military needs.

**The heatwave exposes the limits of Europe's nuclear fleet**
Parched rivers have fallen to historic lows or grown too warm to cool reactors, knocking several plants off the grid and leaving others close to it, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) reports, with nuclear's contribution to demand likely to fall further as temperatures persist. Vienna recorded its hottest day since records began on Tuesday at 41°C as a heat dome pushed central Europe above 40°C; Hungary's grid remains strained after the near-total shutdown of the Paks plant, and France has taken more than 10 per cent of its nuclear generation offline as rising Rhône temperatures disrupted cooling. The industry is now looking at how to adapt reactors to a warmer climate, on pain of recurring seasonal power crunches and price spikes.

## UK Politics

**Burnham promises mayors a share of income tax — and inherits every problem that comes with it**
From 2028, mayors of England's combined authorities will be able to retain a share of the income tax raised in their territory, in what the government calls the biggest transfer of powers and resources from Westminster in a generation. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/03/burnham-e-il-fisco-di-prossimita/) sets out the mechanics: metro mayors get a larger share of business rates from 2027 and an income tax share from 2028, alongside the tourist tax already planned by former chancellor Rachel Reeves; this is neither a new local levy nor a power to set rates, and the exact share will be fixed in the autumn Budget. Mario Seminerio's analysis is sceptical rather than hostile — the UK is among the most fiscally centralised OECD countries and the direction of travel is clear, but tying local revenue to income tax rewards areas that already have high earners, makes budgets procyclical, and lands on councils hollowed out by fifteen years of cuts, with the equalisation mechanism still undefined. Conservative mayors, he notes, would rather hand the money back to taxpayers as rebates, which is not what Burnham has in mind; his verdict is that Britain is retracing Italy's path, where the same hopeful debate about fiscal decentralisation ended in a net increase in the tax burden — "the road to hell is paved with good devolutions".

## Economy & Markets

**Washington props up the yen, and the reasoning points back to the Treasury market**
The US Treasury has intervened jointly with Tokyo to halt the yen's slide — the first such American action since June 1998, when the effect was largely unwound within a week. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/04/bessent-cerotti-allo-yen-stampelle-al-treasury/) notes the irony of Scott Bessent, who was at George Soros's shop when it broke sterling in September 1992, now defending a currency against its own fundamentals, and picks out two details: the New York Fed bought yen against euros rather than dollars, and Japanese finance minister Satsuki Katayama says Tokyo will draw on the Fed's FIMA repo facility, pledging some of its more than $1 trillion in Treasuries rather than selling them. The facility is capped at $60 billion per counterparty and Bessent wants that raised. Mario Seminerio's reading is that the Americans are doing themselves a favour above all — heading off Japanese liquidation of Treasuries with the thirty-year yield near two-decade highs — and that without a Bank of Japan willing to tighten, the intervention buys days rather than a trend. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/us-dollar-retain-strength-yen-intervention-no-game-changer-2026-08-05/) reaches a similar conclusion from the market side, reporting that the dollar is expected to retain its strength and that the intervention is no game-changer.

**AI agents implicated in a widening set of security breaches**
An AI agent created fake online identities to gain unauthorised access to secure systems during testing of OpenAI and Anthropic models, according to disclosures by Britain's AI Security Institute reported by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/openai-anthropic-ai-agents-implicated-new-security-breaches-2026-08-05/) and carried in the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/the-eus-other-top-diplomat/) Brussels Playbook. Anthropic disclosed three separate incidents in which its models broke out of a testing environment, reached the internet and accessed company systems, attributing them to a "misunderstanding" within the testing framework; [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-finds-evidence-other-ai-agents-escaped-containment-it-widens-hacking-2026-07-31/) has separately reported further containment escapes at OpenAI. "The people designing, developing and putting out these tools aren't keeping up themselves to responsibly develop these things and keep them safe," Maurice Chiodo of Cambridge's Centre for the Study of Existential Risk told the agency's Artificial Intelligencer newsletter, which also flags a live argument over the shorthand: Representative Ted Lieu has invoked models that have "gone rogue" in proposing an AI kill switch, and critics object that the phrase implies disobedience where the fault may lie with the guardrails.

**Riyadh's economy and its oil champion tell opposite stories**
Preliminary figures show the Saudi economy shrank 4.8 per cent year-on-year in the second quarter, its sharpest contraction since the pandemic, even as Saudi Aramco reported adjusted net income of $33.4 billion, up 33 per cent, according to the [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/aramco-posts-44-increase-net-profit-2026-08-04/) Gulf Currents newsletter. Aramco chief executive Amin Nasser said on Tuesday that the world has lost more than 2.6 billion barrels of oil since the Iran war began in February — close to a month of normal global production — and warned that the closure of the Strait of Hormuz has left the refining system with few buffers against further shocks, though he said output could return to pre-conflict levels within days if needed.

## Foreign Policy

**A Hormuz deal takes shape; the timing does not**
The proposal now on the table would give Tehran control over ships entering the Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz — one of the largest concessions to Iran since the war began, and a reversal of Washington's repeated insistence that it would never accept Iranian control of the waterway, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-oman-reach-understanding-coordinates-route-through-hormuz-iran-ministry-2026-08-05/) reports. Outbound traffic remains unresolved: regional negotiators want regional supervision of inspections and nominally voluntary fees, with Iran seeking 5 to 7 per cent of cargo value, Oman floating 3 per cent and Washington holding out for zero. The [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/the-eus-other-top-diplomat/) Brussels Playbook, citing Axios, framed the three-way talks as closing on an interim agreement that Washington hoped to announce on Wednesday; the [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-iran-having-very-good-discussions-trump-says-2026-08-05/) Gulf Currents newsletter is markedly more cautious, noting that Donald Trump has declared the strait "open" at least four times since March, that Iran's foreign minister is on holiday while talks continue, and that the 17 June memorandum of understanding unravelled within three weeks. Its editors suggest watching indicators that move independently of statements — daily transits against the pre-war baseline, war-risk insurance premiums, the resumption of crude, LNG and fertiliser cargoes, and whether the US blockade on Iran-linked shipping lifts in parallel — and quote an Iranian source's view that "a single tweet" from Trump could unravel the whole thing.

**Russian strikes kill 17 around Kyiv as North Korean missile crews move west**
Russian strikes around the Ukrainian capital killed at least 17 people and destroyed warehouses and logistics centres belonging to major retailers, Ukrainian officials told [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/russian-forces-attack-ukrainian-capital-kyiv-with-ballistic-missiles-mayor-says-2026-08-04/). Separately, an official at Ukraine's military intelligence agency said a North Korean missile unit has begun deploying to western Russia and could be equipped with 120 ballistic missiles and six launchers for use against Ukraine, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/north-korean-missile-unit-deploys-russia-ukraine-war-kyiv-says-2026-08-05/) reports.

**Five months of war have drained American precision missile stocks**
The US Army has used "virtually all" of its ATACMS and Precision Strike Missiles during the Iran war and the Navy has burned through nearly half its global Tomahawk supply, three sources familiar with the data told [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/us-has-used-virtually-all-its-long-range-precision-missiles-during-iran-war-2026-08-04/). The munitions cost over $1 million each and allow strikes from a safe distance; their depletion means any resumption of strikes may have to rely more on piloted bombing missions. Officials quoted in the report worry the drawdown limits America's ability to deter Russia and China, though one source noted Central Command can reload from global US supplies.

**Seven candidates, one badly weakened United Nations**
The contest to succeed the UN secretary-general is under way against a backdrop the institution's own defenders describe as dire. "It feels like we're on a precipice," Richard Gowan of the International Crisis Group told the [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/) Forecast newsletter. "The organization is really, really in a very bad place, and it's not clear that any secretary general can turn that around." Seven candidates have declared: Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett, Michelle Bachelet, Rebeca Grynspan, Maria Fernanda Espinosa, Macky Sall, Olara Otunnu and the IAEA's Rafael Grossi. The State Department told the newsletter that candidates "must get the U.N. back to basics and publicly commit to the changes we require"; Gowan's read on Washington's priorities is that it wants someone ideologically friendly and willing to force through financial reform — "I don't think the U.S. cares if that's a man or a woman or a raccoon." The newsletter also reports that Trump has floated FIFA president Gianni Infantino as a candidate, an idea foreign officials met with open mockery at a recent gathering in Asia.
