# Daily Briefing — Thursday, 17 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Von der Leyen's Canada "associate membership" offer draws a tariff threat from Trump**
The single line in Ursula von der Leyen's State of the Union address proposing that Canada become "the first associate member of the EU" has dominated Brussels for a second day. According to the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-wants-canada-to-become-associate-member-von-der-leyen-says/), the line was absent from the embargoed text circulated to capitals beforehand, apparently to prevent leaks, and [blindsided EU governments](https://www.politico.eu/article/ursula-von-der-leyen-blindside-eu-canada-associate-member-pitch/). Overnight, Donald Trump called the idea "laughable" and threatened tariffs: "If I think it's at all a hostile act, I will put very serious tariffs or stop trading with Europe on many things" ([Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/16/trump-threatens-to-impose-serious-tariffs-on-europe-if-canada-joins-eu-as-associate-member-01081525); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-warns-eu-tariffs-over-canadas-potential-associate-membership-calls-it-2026-09-17/)), though Berlin Playbook notes he then qualified it — good intentions would be "completely fine", bad ones would draw "heavy tariffs". Canada is keeping its distance from the label: incoming EU ambassador Jonathan Wilkinson said "before you actually get to the point where you decide what you're going to call something, you should actually know the shape of it", per the *Euractiv* SOTEU newsletter. Mark Carney addressed the European Parliament in Strasbourg at 11:30 this morning.

The framings diverge sharply. *Euractiv Rapporteur* treats the proposal as opening "a can of worms" over what membership means and who qualifies — citing a diplomat's observation that ten member states have still not ratified CETA — and notes it lands as Iceland has voted against restarting accession talks and negotiating clusters with Ukraine remain stuck. [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/article/ursula-von-der-leyen-mark-carney-europe-world-middle-powers/) instead reaches for the Brexit precedent, arguing that a meaningful new tier of membership "seems distant at best" given the impossibility of treaty change, while conceding the realistic gains — trade, Arctic security, possible Canadian participation in EU student-exchange schemes. Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra called it "terra nova"; MEP and EU law professor René Repasi said doing it properly would require treaty change, with the European Economic Area a likelier model. Brussels will not extend the same offer to post-Brexit Britain, [Politico reports](https://www.politico.eu/article/brussels-wont-offer-associate-eu-membership-to-brexit-britain/).

**SOTEU judged long on institutional invention, short on legislation**
Beyond the Canada line, von der Leyen's 75-minute address proposed a European Security Council including Canada, the UK, Norway and Ukraine, a "Congress of Europe", a European Emergency Security Protocol modelled on NATO's Article 4, and a new European Instrument for Strategic Enablers. She also signalled an overhaul of Russia sanctions strategy — "the pressure on Russia must continue to increase… and this does not always require new sanctions, but above all the enforcement of existing ones" — per *Euractiv*, which judged the speech to have "amped up emotion at the expense of detail". Politico notes the security council sits outside NATO and may [create friction with the alliance](https://www.politico.eu/article/ursula-von-der-leyen-eu-nato-turf-war/). French MEP and presidential hopeful Raphaël Glucksmann told *Euractiv* the Ukraine passages — normally her strongest ground — were "just symbols", asking what the speech delivers while "500 industrial workers are losing their jobs every day". On AI, Politico reported her call for talks on pausing development [could not stem extinction-risk fears](https://www.politico.eu/article/von-der-leyens-call-for-talks-on-pausing-ai-cant-stem-extinction-risk-fears/), while Executive Vice-President Teresa Ribera said the Commission is [open to antitrust exemptions](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-open-to-discuss-antitrust-exemption-to-ai-safety-coordination-ribera-says/) allowing AI firms to coordinate on safety.

**Budget talks stall as Berlin presses for cuts and Washington objects to "Buy European"**
European Council President António Costa is in Dublin today, completing a tour of all 27 leaders ahead of an informal end-of-year deadline for the next seven-year budget. Ireland, holding the Council presidency, will circulate a revised "negobox" with new figures before the 15 October leaders' summit. Wednesday's meeting of the 27 ambassadors was described by two diplomats to the *Politico Brussels Playbook* as "the most useless session so far" and "smoke and mirrors", with member states using incompatible metrics to calculate contributions; Germany renewed its call to cut the budget by "hundreds of billions of euros", and France warned that doing so would end Germany's rebate. Separately, *Euractiv Rapporteur* reports a US non-paper sent to EU deputies warning that "Buy European" rules inside the proposed €400 billion competitiveness fund could restrict European defence firms' access to US procurement and "slow" European rearmament; Washington could review Buy American waivers covering defence procurement agreements with 19 member states.

**Merz's grip tested before Sunday's German elections**
Eight CDU state premiers issued a written declaration of solidarity with Chancellor Friedrich Merz after the party's defeat in Saxony-Anhalt and ahead of votes in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern on Sunday, per the *Politico Berlin Playbook* and *Euractiv*. The letter does not mention Merz by name, and Markus Söder did not sign it — CSU floor leader Klaus Holetschek said Söder had already positioned himself clearly, "so it isn't necessary", while CDU parliamentary leader Thorsten Frei dismissed the argument over the missing name as "hair-splitting". Signatories disagree on the letter's half-life: some told Berlin Playbook it lasts only until Sunday evening, others that the backing would survive catastrophic results. Merz is meanwhile struggling to deliver promised fuel-price relief before the vote, with Frei suggesting 1 October is achievable; Politico has published [scenarios for whether he survives](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-friedrich-merz-scenarios-elections-cdu/).

**France's 2027 budget and fuel prices collide with the presidential race**
Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu is due to set out the broad lines of the 2027 budget in an interview today, a day before the text must go to the Haut Conseil des Finances Publiques, according to the *Politico Playbook Paris*. Centrist lawmakers are already modelling a stalemate in which the government resorts to ordonnances — permissible if parliament has not ruled within 70 days — which would nullify all parliamentary amendments. Amid renewed fuel-price rises and polling suggesting a gilets jaunes revival, Lecornu has extended targeted sectoral aid to 31 December; Marine Le Pen proposed cutting VAT on energy from 20% to 5.5%, costed by allies of Édouard Philippe at €12–13 billion, and declined to detail the €125 billion savings plan she will present in early October. She also said France can no longer fund Ukraine financially. *Euractiv* adds that Le Pen pledged to cap France's net EU contribution at €6 billion, claiming payments could reach €45 billion; Senate estimates put the average annual gross contribution under the next budget at €36 billion, or €42 billion without new EU own resources. Gabriel Attal delivers a speech in Reims today proposing a federal "United States of Europe" and criticising von der Leyen on Chinese competition, the unimplemented Draghi report and Ceuta.

**Migration crisis instrument heads for October leaders' summit**
The migration crisis tool announced in the State of the Union is expected to feature in EU leaders' strategic discussion on migration in October, an official told *Euractiv Rapporteur*. Conceived after the mass arrivals in Ceuta and long advocated by Migration Commissioner Magnus Brunner, it is intended to give the bloc a mechanism for sudden large-scale flows — though several MEPs and diplomats said they were still trying to establish what it adds to the existing toolbox. Brunner travels to Madrid today for ministerial meetings covering Ceuta. In the Spanish parliament, Pedro Sánchez and Alberto Núñez Feijóo clashed over the issue, with Feijóo asking why thousands remain stranded six weeks on and Sánchez replying that suspending the EU–Morocco association agreement, as Feijóo has urged in Brussels, would make matters worse.

## Italian Politics

**Rome joins the European turn towards selective tax relief**
Writing in [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/15/ridurre-le-tasse-aumentando-le-tasse/), Mario Seminerio places recent Italian fiscal measures within a wider European pattern of governments responding to fiscal strain by cutting taxes selectively rather than spending — citing the flat tax on contract renewals, Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti's proposed tax relief for young workers, and the *cedolare secca* on shop rents now under consideration. His objection is one of arithmetic rather than principle: forgone revenue has to be covered, spending has become politically untouchable, and the residual method is to raise other taxes, which in turn weighs on the growth needed to fund the welfare state. On the youth measure specifically he notes that nobody has explained what happens when the beneficiaries age out and normal income tax rates resume. No Italy-specific story appeared in the Politico, Euractiv, Reuters or Times newsletters today.

## UK Politics

**Burnham squeezed between tax-cut promises and an empty Treasury**
More than 800 pubs, hotels and bars have signed a public appeal to Prime Minister Andy Burnham to honour his February pledge to halve hospitality VAT to around 10%, in line with the European average, according to [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/15/ridurre-le-tasse-aumentando-le-tasse/), citing the Guardian. Burnham has already spent £100 million cutting business rates for the sector by 20%, which prompted the further "VAT's the problem" campaign; the industry is simultaneously absorbing higher employer National Insurance contributions and a rising minimum wage. The fiscal room is not there: Chancellor John Healey, who left the Defence brief under Keir Starmer over a refused £10 billion, has since found the cupboard bare and deferred defence allocations to next year's spending review. Meanwhile the first ministers of Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland — all now independence-minded — met in Cardiff and issued a communiqué suggesting Burnham could be the last prime minister of the United Kingdom, demanding the power to call secession referendums and declaring their futures lie inside the EU.

**Bank of England holds at 3.75% as inflation pressure builds**
The Bank of England's rate decision was expected to leave policy unchanged, per the *Politico Forecast* agenda. The Monetary Policy Committee held Bank Rate at 3.75%, with three of nine members voting for an immediate quarter-point rise, after inflation reached 3.1% in August and with warnings that it could exceed 4% in early 2027 as energy costs feed through (additional context via [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/monetary-policy/the-interest-rate-bank-rate)). The hold leaves Britain out of step with the Federal Reserve, which raised rates overnight, and the European Central Bank, which raised last week.

**NHS England agrees deal on Enhertu after Trump's pharmaceutical tariff threat**
NICE approved AstraZeneca and Daiichi Sankyo's breast cancer drug Enhertu on Wednesday night, making it available in England from today for around 1,000 women a year with incurable metastatic disease, extending life by an average of six months, [The Times reports](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/healthcare/article/nhs-england-deal-breast-cancer-drug-enhertu-q5x9k3skr). The drug was rejected in 2024 as too expensive under criteria that did not classify metastatic breast cancer as sufficiently "severe", while remaining available in Scotland and elsewhere in Europe — a gap AstraZeneca chief executive Sir Pascal Soriot used to press for reform of NHS drug funding. The Times links the April change to NICE's cost-effectiveness criteria directly to Trump's threat of a 100% tariff on pharmaceutical exports to the US unless Britain spent more on medicines.

## Economy & Markets

**The Fed raises rates for the first time in three years and Trump objects**
The Federal Open Market Committee raised the federal funds target by a quarter point to 3.75–4% on a unanimous vote, its first increase since 2023, and signalled more to come, [Phastidio reports](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/17/fed-il-tasso-oltre-lostacolo-trump/): sixteen of the eighteen participants submitting projections see at least one further rise before year-end. Chair Kevin Warsh said the Fed had "removed a dose of accommodation" — implying policy is not yet neutral — and dismissed the neutral rate as "useful academically but with no operational effect on the decisions taken today". Phastidio reads the move as the Fed reasserting independence from political power, while noting it is rather less independent of markets, which had threatened turmoil on a hold; it also flags the central contradiction Warsh acknowledged, that higher rates cannot lower crude prices when the oil is physically absent. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/markets/wealth/fed-builds-credibility-hawkish-turn-leaves-investors-edgy-2026-09-17/) frames the result as investors gaining confidence in the Fed's inflation-fighting resolve while remaining uncertain how far it will go, whereas [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/business/economics/article/us-fed-interest-rates-ngrwggsrp) attributes the underlying inflation squarely to the White House's war with Iran. Trump said rates [should be "1% or lower"](https://www.reuters.com/business/trump-says-us-interest-rates-should-be-1-or-lower-2026-09-16/) and complained the board is "very hostile".

**Congress hands Trump potential new tariff powers in a Russia sanctions bill**
The House passed the late Lindsey Graham's Russia and Iran sanctions bill overnight by 262–159, after it cleared the Senate 86–11 in early August, per the *Politico Berlin Playbook*. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/16/en-visant-la-russie-le-congres-americain-va-t-il-donner-de-nouveaux-pouvoirs-a-trump-en-matiere-de-tarifs/) sets out the European exposure: the text allows tariffs of up to 100% on the five largest importers of Russian crude and the five largest importers of Russian gas, plus countries judged to be "facilitators" of sanctions circumvention — a deliberately vague category left to White House discretion, with broad waiver powers attached. On USTR data compiled by the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, Slovakia is among the top five crude importers, France and Belgium among the top five gas importers, and Hungary appears in both. Russian gas reached 100% of Belgian LNG imports in July, while CREA data for August show Hungary has become the Union's largest importer of Russian energy. Georgetown's Peter E. Harrell warns the absence of guardrails could let Trump treat the EU as a single country and tariff the bloc as a whole. Democrats in the House objected on precisely these grounds; [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/16/house-russia-sanctions-bill-trump-01081210) notes Washington observers doubt Trump will use the tools in full against buyers of Russian energy.

The stress is visible in the physical market: [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/number-ships-transiting-strait-hormuz-falls-three-wednesday-data-shows-2026-09-17/) reports that only three ships transited the Strait of Hormuz on Wednesday. **Ukraine mounts a second major counter-offensive of the year at Lyman**
Andriy Biletsky, commander of Ukraine's 3rd Army Corps, confirmed on Tuesday that forces have retaken some 85 km² around Lyman in northern Donetsk oblast and forced Russian fuel supply lines back into Voronezh oblast, roughly 100 km away, [reports Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/16/larmee-ukrainienne-mene-a-lyman-sa-deuxieme-contre-offensive-majeure-de-lannee/). It is the first significant Russian retreat in this sector since 2022. The operation, codenamed "Vivaldi", has been conducted under the same media blackout as the southern counter-offensive earlier this year, which recovered 745 km²; analyst Clément Molin dates its start to late May, and the ISW assesses that Ukrainian gains probably exceed Biletsky's announcement. Biletsky describes the 85 km² as only the first phase. Kremlin-adjacent sources say Putin wants the whole of Donetsk oblast within six months — the fifteenth deadline he has set the army since 2022, none of which has been met. Separately, *Euractiv Rapporteur* reports Volodymyr Zelenskyy saying he is ready to hold elections "tomorrow" but that voting is impossible amid fighting, citing legal constraints, security risks, millions of citizens abroad and the occupied territories.
