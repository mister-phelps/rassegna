# Daily Briefing — Saturday, 26 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Leo XIV takes an anti-nationalist message into the French campaign**
Pope Leo XIV arrived in Paris on Friday for a four-day visit that lands squarely in the middle of France's presidential race. According to the [Politico Brussels Playbook Capitals newsletter](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/an-american-pope-in-paris/), his [first public words](https://www.politico.eu/article/pope-leo-urges-urgent-dialogue-in-warning-to-populist-forces-in-france/) greeted "all those" in France "whose diverse origins have enlivened and enriched the nation and its democracy", and he argued that "true secularism does not mean excluding religion from the public sphere" — read as an implicit rebuke of far-right proposals to ban headscarves in public. Antonio Spadaro, undersecretary of the Vatican's Dicastery for Culture and Education, told Politico that Catholics drawn to nationalism often have legitimate concerns about identity but have been "offered the wrong answer": "The pope will not tell the French to be less French. Roots are a good thing. The trouble begins when roots are used as walls." Politico frames the trip primarily as a political intervention — polls before the 2022 presidential and 2024 European elections found roughly 40% of Catholics intending to vote for Marine Le Pen's National Rally, and Le Pen, Jordan Bardella, Edouard Philippe, Gabriel Attal and Bruno Retailleau are all expected at Saturday's mass — while [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/25/le-precedent-le-sens-de-la-visite-apostolique-historique-de-leon-xiv-en-france/) emphasises the ecclesial and symbolic register: the first official papal visit to France since Benedict XVI in 2008, a mass on the Place de la Concorde with more than 600,000 expected, a stop at the Maison Bakhita migrant shelter, a meeting with victims of clerical abuse at Lourdes that victims' associations have already called insufficient, and a closing address in Metz honouring Robert Schuman. Le Grand Continent also notes the tension in that final speech between Leo's integral pacifism, restated in his encyclical *Magnifica Humanitas*, and the EU's current rearmament drive. Emmanuel Macron will join the pope in Metz on Monday; per the Politico newsletter, Friedrich Merz, Giorgia Meloni, Rob Jetten and Ursula von der Leyen have all declined invitations to attend.

**Mélenchon closes on second place as France's runoff narrows to the extremes**
A poll conducted exclusively for [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/25/far-left-melenchon-on-track-for-presidential-face-off-against-le-pen-poll-suggests) puts far-left candidate Jean-Luc Mélenchon level with centre-right former prime minister Edouard Philippe on 19% of voting intentions for next spring's presidential election. **Brussels quietly doubts Kyiv's $27 billion funding gap**
[Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com) devotes its Saturday edition to picking apart Volodymyr Zelenskyy's claim last month that Ukraine faces a budget shortfall this year of $27 billion, equivalent to more than 10% of GDP. Euractiv notes the figures do not reconcile — "$8-10 billion" for weapons plus "nearly $20 billion" for salaries and unspecified "other areas of funding" does not sum to $27 billion — and that neither the European Commission nor the IMF has been able to confirm them after weeks of discussion. Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis told Euractiv that "by and large, [the] budgetary needs for Ukraine for this year seem to be broadly covered", while allowing that Kyiv needs more military support; the Commission has also resisted calls from some member states to accelerate disbursements from the €90 billion loan, of which only €3.2 billion in budget support has been paid out alongside €11.7 billion for defence procurement. Maksym Samoiliuk of the Kyiv-based Centre for Economic Strategy points out that some $29.5 billion in external financing is currently held up by the Ukrainian parliament's failure to pass reforms. Euractiv's conclusion is that politics, not economics, is driving the demand. Separately, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/trump-administration-meet-ukraine-assistance-deadline-2026-09-25/) reports that the Trump administration has made binding commitments to spend almost all of the $400 million Congress approved for military aid to Kyiv, ensuring the money does not lapse at the end of this month.

## Italian Politics

**Rome stays in the excessive deficit procedure — and the MEF did not see it coming**
Istat confirmed this week that Italy's 2025 deficit came in at 3.1% of GDP, above the EU's 3% threshold and identical to Eurostat's April estimate, keeping Rome inside the excessive deficit procedure. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com) reports Economy Minister Giancarlo Giorgetti's response — "unfortunately, Italy will not exit early this year from the excessive deficit procedure, as we had hoped" — while pointing to a possible exit in 2027. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/23/la-breccia-a-via-venti-settembre/) frames the same numbers as a public-accounting failure rather than a fiscal one: 2025 GDP was revised up by some €7 billion and revenues by nearly €2 billion, which alone would have delivered a 2.97% ratio, but €2.488 billion in previously unrecorded capital spending appeared between April and October 2025 — €700 million from Anas and Ferrovie, €1.3 billion from public-private partnerships reclassified from "off-balance" to "on-balance" — leaving €350 million of additional deficit and no exit. Citing Luciano Capone in *Il Foglio*, Phastidio puts the combined April and September revisions at roughly €11 billion, or half a percentage point of GDP: "the state spent 0.5 points of GDP without realising it." The practical consequence, Phastidio notes, is that Italy cannot net off the defence and energy "flexibility" clauses from its deficit ratio while it remains in the procedure, and risks staying there for years — though it dismisses the arithmetic itself as largely a fiction, since "the last word belongs to the markets, not the European Commission."

## Economy & Markets

**Europe's diesel problem moves from the Gulf to the Red Sea**
The Houthi seizure of the port of Mocha on 10–11 September and their advance towards Perim island has given them de facto control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait, closing one of the few routes still partially circumventing Iran's blockade of Hormuz. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/26/bab-el-mandeb-le-controle-houthiste-fait-peser-un-risque-sur-les-approvisionnements-europeens-en-diesel-et-kerosene/), drawing on a Global Trade Policy Observatory note, sets out the exposure: Gulf crude was only about 7% of EU imports last year, but before the 2023 Houthi attacks 58% of Europe's diesel imports and 66% of its jet fuel travelled routes through Bab el-Mandeb, and the Gulf's share of EU diesel imports has risen from 2.4% in 2013 to 31.6% in 2025 as it replaced Russia. Diesel passed $200 a barrel in the United States in early September, roughly twice pre-war levels, with comparable European moves, while Gulf and Russian diesel exports are running 1.6 million barrels a day below February. European refineries cannot make up the gap: capacity is down about 13% since 2000, seven member states have no significant refining left, and the medium sour crudes the remaining plants were built for have fallen from 31% to 6% of imports. Emergency jet fuel reserves fell 35% between January and June. The transatlantic dimension is the immediate risk — Le Grand Continent notes Washington is weighing restrictions or a suspension of diesel exports, and the [Politico newsletter](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-launches-diplomatic-offensive-to-stop-trumps-diesel-export-ban/) reports a 90-day US export ban under consideration and an EU diplomatic offensive to stop it. Politico frames the week as a bad one for "European drivers (and anyone who moves anything)", pointing to a 500-truck go-slow on Portugal's main north-south motorway over diesel prices and recent French fishermen's port blockades; [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/26/la-settimana-phastidiosa-26-settembre-2026/) emphasises the macro channel instead, arguing that the refined-products shock will feed inflation just as real yields rise, and that rising bond yields "everywhere" are the real global threat.
