## EU Affairs

**Spain's Vox sees a continental opening as four big member states head to the polls**
Spain's far-right Vox believes a run of elections across Europe could hand nationalist parties the combined weight to pull Brussels rightwards, senior figure Jorge Buxadé told the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/spains-far-right-eyes-a-great-revolution/). "For the first time in history, there is a possibility that all four nations could have conservative and patriotic governments," said Buxadé, who leads Vox's delegation in the European Parliament, referring to Spain, France, Poland and Italy — "and that would, of course, be the great revolution." Per the same newsletter, the People's Party leads Spanish polling on 33 per cent with Vox on 17 and Pedro Sánchez's PSOE on 26, according to [Politico's Poll of Polls](https://www.politico.eu/europe-poll-of-polls/spain/), enough for a PP–Vox majority. Buxadé said a Vox-backed government would use national vetoes in the European Council and stop automatically accommodating Commission initiatives: "We won't be a government that goes to Brussels to beg." PSOE organisation secretary Rebeca Torró countered that "the People's Party and Vox, although they have different acronyms, are exactly the same," and argued the campaign itself would trigger "real mobilisation."

**EU leaders close ranks behind the ICC after Washington sanctions its president**
Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and European Council President António Costa [threw their weight behind the International Criminal Court](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-leaders-back-icc-president-tomoko-akane-after-us-sanctions/) on Wednesday, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/trumps-wake-up-call-to-seoul/) reports, after Secretary of State Marco Rubio sanctioned ICC President Tomoko Akane and senior trial lawyer Abdoulaye Seye on Tuesday. Washington accuses the court of targeting officials from states that reject its jurisdiction, a reference to its pursuit of Israeli officials over alleged crimes in Gaza; the court maintains it has jurisdiction over alleged crimes committed in Palestinian territories. Dutch Foreign Minister Tom Berendsen separately [condemned the sanctions](https://www.politico.eu/article/netherlands-tom-berendsen-slams-trump-sanctions-icc-president-gaza-investigator/) as part of a campaign against the tribunal, which is headquartered in The Hague.

**Satellite imagery points to new Russian drone launch sites within reach of eleven NATO states**
[Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/18/la-russie-construit-des-rampes-de-lancement-de-drones-a-portee-des-pays-de-lotan-2/), drawing on satellite images and leaked Russian documents first reported by The Telegraph, says Moscow has built drone launch ramps at ten bases near Europe, three of them on occupied Ukrainian territory. Sites along the Belarusian and Ukrainian borders hold at least 59 ramps capable of launching fixed-wing drones without a runway, and the territory of eleven NATO members falls within range. The publication notes Russia can produce roughly 3,000 Geran-4 and Geran-5 drones a month — the Alabuga industrial park alone accounts for 6,000 units a year — and that a Spanish fighter operating under NATO command destroyed an apparently Russian drone near Galați on Sunday, the fourth downed over Romania this year. Additional context via The Wall Street Journal, cited by Le Grand Continent: US intelligence now judges a "limited Russian attack" on a NATO country possible as soon as this autumn, a shift from earlier assessments that Putin would not test the alliance while the Ukraine war continued.

## Economy & Markets

**Yields at multi-decade highs as sovereign and AI borrowing compete for the long end**
Government bond yields have reached multi-year or multi-decade highs in the United States, Japan, the eurozone and the United Kingdom, [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/19/debito-finche-la-musica-suona/) argues, driven by persistently high public deficits, heavy long-dated issuance by AI hyperscalers financing data centres, commodity-price pressure from the Hormuz and Ukraine wars, and a growing share of sovereign paper held by leveraged hedge funds for trading rather than allocation. Mario Seminerio describes a bear steepening of the curve and raises the spectre of fiscal dominance, with central banks losing independence de facto if not de jure; he also notes that the US Treasury's response — shortening the maturity of new issuance to avoid locking in high duration costs — buys relief today at the price of greater rollover risk later. China is the outlier, with yields at fresh lows amid deflation and private savings migrating into equities and technology listings. [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/08/19/treasury-buy-back-debt-bond-market-pain-01041461) reports the Treasury announced on Wednesday it would buy back more of its own bonds, partially reversing a selloff in longer-term debt; Phastidio frames the underlying dynamic as structural and unresolved, while Politico presents the buyback as a tactical response to days of bond-market pain.

Politico frames the deal chiefly as a possible gateway to the broader USMCA review due this year, covering close to $1 trillion in annual trade, whereas Reuters emphasises the immediate deadline averted.

## Foreign Policy

**Ukraine's ousted defence minister calls for wartime elections, opening the deepest domestic rift of the war**
Mykhailo Fedorov, sacked as defence minister in July, used a video address on Tuesday evening to [call for elections](https://www.politico.eu/article/ousted-ukrainian-defense-chief-fedorov-demands-elections-in-challenge-to-zelenskyy/) despite the ongoing Russian invasion — the most serious internal challenge to Volodymyr Zelenskyy since 2022, according to both [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/spains-far-right-eyes-a-great-revolution/) and [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/ukraines-ousted-defence-minister-calls-wartime-elections-2026-08-18/), which reports he argued the country faces a crisis of governance. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/19/dans-un-appel-aux-ukrainiens-fedorov-defie-zelensky-texte-integral/), which published a full translation, notes the address followed Zelenskyy's decision on 18 August to confirm Ievhenii Khmara as defence minister, closing the door on Fedorov's return. In the speech Fedorov attacks a system in which "the main criterion for appointing a person is not professionalism… but personal loyalty", and argues that "democracy cannot be held hostage by Russia." Le Grand Continent adds the political arithmetic: an April KIIS poll put Valery Zaluzhny on 66 per cent trust, Fedorov on 63 and Zelenskyy on 56, and quotes Zelenskyy-aligned MP Oleksandr Merezhko calling wartime elections "very dangerous and unrealistic" and a gift to Putin. Politico and Reuters treat the address primarily as a challenge to Zelenskyy's authority; Le Grand Continent situates it within a governance and anti-corruption argument, and against a backdrop of Ukrainian battlefield gains of 745 km² over six months.

**Hormuz remains the fault line as Washington and Tehran both deny talks**
[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trump-says-no-talks-planned-with-iran-tehran-says-strait-hormuz-still-shut-2026-08-18/) reports Trump said no talks were taking place with Iran and insisted the Strait of Hormuz was open, contradicting Tehran's assertion that the waterway remains shut to shipping. Traffic data supports the gloomier reading: six commodity vessels crossed on Tuesday, down from nine the previous day and below a ten-day average of eleven, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/hormuz-traffic-slows-uncertainty-over-waterway-persists-2026-08-19/) reports, citing Kpler. The Reuters Gulf Currents newsletter frames the week as a conflict running "on autopilot": the 60-day window for US–Iranian talks expired on Monday, a senior Iranian official spoke of shifting from "defensive to fully offensive", and on Tuesday the UAE moved from missile alert to all-clear to a [financial embargo on Iran](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/uaes-financial-embargo-iran-after-missile-threat-puts-rivalry-back-spotlight-2026-08-19/) within a single evening. Abu Dhabi said it detected two ballistic missiles launched from Iran targeting maritime traffic; Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called the account "baseless".

**Seoul reassesses the alliance after Trump scales back joint drills**
Trump's announcement that the US would "substantially reduce" joint military exercises with South Korea has rattled Seoul well beyond the drills themselves, [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/trumps-wake-up-call-to-seoul/) reports, with every major South Korean newspaper — conservative and liberal alike — running editorials warning about the state of the alliance. Politico notes Trump linked the move to Seoul's refusal to help on Iran while courting Kim Jong Un. President Lee Jae Myung has cast the moment as an argument for greater self-reliance and for completing the long-planned transfer of wartime operational control, saying South Korea's defence capacity "ranks among the highest in the world"; Unification Minister Chung Dong-young welcomed the decision in the hope it leads to US–North Korean talks. The conservative People Power Party sees an alliance in need of repair; liberals see room for autonomy and diplomacy.
