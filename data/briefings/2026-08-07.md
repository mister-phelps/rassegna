## EU Affairs

**Iceland's referendum turns into a test of how far Brussels dares go**
Three weeks before Icelanders vote on 29 August on whether to reopen EU accession talks, the two Brussels newsletters that led on the story frame it in opposite directions. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/icelands-eu-moment/) reports that Iceland and Montenegro could be lined up to join together in 2028, with eight diplomats and officials describing Brussels as open to a joint package; Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos told the newsletter Iceland is a "special case" thanks to its EEA and Schengen membership and that talks could take "one to two years", while officials argued Iceland's wealth would make it a net contributor effectively financing Montenegro's accession. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/exclusive-iceland-asks-brussels-to-stay-out-of-eu-referendum/) reports the opposite pressure: Reykjavík has asked the Commission behind closed doors to stay out of the campaign, two EU officials said, with senior EU figures nonetheless weighing how to help the Yes camp without offending the government — an account a Commission spokesperson denied. Politico emphasises Icelandic confidence, quoting Foreign Minister Þorgerður Katrín Gunnarsdóttir's promise to go on the "attack" in negotiations and her prediction of a Yes; Euractiv emphasises drift, noting [polls are tied](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-membership-would-free-iceland-from-krona-inflation-machine-campaigner-says/), rural Iceland turning against, and no cabinet minister confirmed for a domestic debate. Former president Ólafur Ragnar Grímsson told [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/icelands-eu-moment/) that a state of 400,000 people with "six or seven hundred officials" in core ministries would be lost in the EU's bureaucratic "jungle".

**Seven governments criticise the EU's Sahel envoy appointment**
France, Spain and five other member states have objected to the way Danish career diplomat Birgitte Nygaard Markussen was appointed to a senior European External Action Service role, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/exclusive-france-spain-criticise-controversial-eu-foreign-policy-appointment/) reports. The post went to Markussen after Slovenia's new right-wing government [blocked](https://www.euractiv.com/news/slovenia-blocks-ex-foreign-minister-from-top-eu-job-in-domestic-spat/) former Slovenian foreign minister Tanja Fajon's candidacy at the last minute. Belgium, Spain and Romania abstained at a testy meeting of ambassadors, unhappy that the appointment had become entangled in a domestic political dispute, while France criticised the transparency of the procedure and its handling outside official channels. Romania separately raised concerns about the nationality balance among senior EEAS officials.

**Merz's own party openly debates replacing him**

Spain has warned Italy it will take "proportional measures" if Rome does not end border controls on travellers arriving from Spain by 9 August, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/spain-retaliation-italy-border-controls-travelers-ceuta-migrants/) reports. ## UK Politics

**Burnham promises metro mayors a share of income tax**
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced what the government calls "the greatest transfer of power and resources from Westminster in a generation", [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/03/burnham-e-il-fisco-di-prossimita/) reports. From 2027 English combined authorities will retain a larger share of business rates, and from 2028 a share of the income tax paid by residents in their area; a tourist tax, already envisaged by former chancellor Rachel Reeves, will also be introduced. Phastidio stresses this is not a new local levy nor a power to set income tax rates, but a redirection of revenue that currently flows entirely to the Treasury, earmarked for transport, housing, regeneration and infrastructure — with the exact share to be set in the autumn Budget. The site's analysis is sceptical on design: richer areas such as London, Greater Manchester and Cambridgeshire would gain far more, equalisation mechanisms are undefined, tying local budgets to income tax makes them pro-cyclical, and Conservative mayors have already said they would return their share to taxpayers as rebates. Phastidio reads the reform as further evidence of Britain following an "Italian" path, where fiscal devolution debates ended in a net rise in the tax burden.

## Economy & Markets

**Europe's heat becomes a market problem**
Record temperatures across Europe are pushing up food prices and straining supply chains and heavily indebted economies, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/environment/europes-getting-hotter-markets-are-taking-note-2026-08-07/) reports, adding a second shock for markets already unsettled by the Iran war's effect on energy. The UN Food and Agriculture Organization said [world food prices rose in July to their highest in more than three years](https://www.reuters.com/world/world-food-prices-hit-3-year-high-july-weather-war-weigh-fao-says-2026-08-07/), with adverse weather and escalation in the Gulf and Black Sea supporting crop markets. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/economic-toll-europe-infernal-summer-climate-change/) frames the same summer as a political problem: the EU has spent the past year reshaping the Green Deal around industrial competitiveness, requiring new climate legislation to demonstrate an economic advantage, and that pro-industry pivot is now colliding with the cost of the warming it was meant to be deprioritising.

**Washington intervenes to prop up the yen — and, arguably, the Treasury market**
Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has joined Japanese authorities in intervening to halt the yen's slide, an operation [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/04/bessent-cerotti-allo-yen-stampelle-al-treasury/) reads primarily as an American favour to itself: the aim is to prevent Japanese liquidation of US Treasuries at a moment when the 30-year yield is near two-decade highs. Tokyo will draw on the Federal Reserve's FIMA repo facility — created in 2020, capped at $60 billion per counterparty, and which Bessent wants enlarged — to obtain dollars against its Treasury holdings rather than selling them. Phastidio notes the New York Fed bought yen against euros rather than dollars, and that the last comparable US intervention to strengthen the yen, in June 1998, was largely unwound within a week; the analysis argues nothing will hold unless the Bank of Japan tightens, which Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi is reluctant to allow.

## Foreign Policy

**Iran and Oman near a Hormuz framework as Trump looks for an exit**
Iranian deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi said Tehran and Muscat are close to finalising a framework for commercial navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/06/que-contient-laccord-que-liran-et-loman-seraient-en-train-de-finaliser-pour-le-transit-dans-le-detroit-dormuz/) reports, citing IRNA. The arrangement would run for 60 days: inbound traffic through a northern corridor in Iranian waters, outbound through a southern corridor in Omani waters, no tolls or levies, and demining of the central corridor within 30 days — with Tehran reportedly willing to let European states carry out the clearance. Gharibabadi described it as an exercise of "effective sovereignty" over the strait, and said resumption of traffic would still require Washington to lift its naval blockade and the sanctions reimposed when the ceasefire collapsed. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trumps-iran-dilemma-hes-stuck-war-with-no-exit-sight-2026-08-06/) frames the same negotiation as a trap for Donald Trump: with the war in its sixth month, he is caught between an interim deal that would hand Tehran control over the strait it never had before the conflict, and escalation that risks a longer crisis. Le Grand Continent adds that this was the fifth time since 28 February that Trump has threatened massive strikes and then pulled back, and notes a Reuters report — denied by Trump — that the US has exhausted almost its entire global stock of long-range precision missiles. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/vessel-traffic-through-hormuz-dwindles-this-week-markets-watch-iran-oman-talks-2026-08-07/) reports vessel traffic through Hormuz dwindled this week as markets watched the talks.
