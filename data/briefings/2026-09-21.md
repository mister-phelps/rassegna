# Daily Briefing — Monday, 21 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Merz's collapse at home turns into a European budget problem**
Two more state election defeats have left Friedrich Merz fighting for his political survival and Brussels fearing for its next seven-year budget. The far-right AfD finished first in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania while Die Linke topped the poll in Berlin; according to the [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/afd-edges-spd-in-meckpomm-as-berlin-swings-left/) *Rapporteur* newsletter the CDU's preliminary score in Mecklenburg was 4.9 per cent — below the threshold for entering the state parliament, and its worst state result on record — after shedding roughly ten points in Berlin, eight in Mecklenburg and twenty in Saxony-Anhalt inside a fortnight. Merz called the outcome "a disaster" but vowed in a televised statement to stay on, arguing that his reform programme is "the antidote to the authoritarian poison" spreading through German society, and telling his party that "what needs to be done now requires backbone, steadfastness, and patience." The framing diverges sharply: [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-far-right-secures-another-win-in-key-regional-election-exit-poll-says/) treats this as a story about Merz's failing strategy for containing the far right, [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/world/europe/article/german-state-elections-afd-friedrich-merz-x675srzcl) as the CDU's worst defeat on record, while [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-begins-budget-deal-race-to-beat-le-pen-spectre/) emphasises the consequences for the 2028–2034 budget — Germany heads a six-country "frugal" camp demanding [cuts of several hundred billion euros](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-budget-must-be-cut-by-several-hundred-of-billions/) to the Commission's €2 trillion plan, and one diplomat told Euractiv that Merz "is now backed into a corner by the rise of the far right," with positions running to 20 per cent below the Commission's opening proposal. Merz has cancelled his trip to the UN General Assembly, per the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ai-talk-no-action/) newsletter, and meets the Länder minister-presidents on Thursday to discuss the European budget.

**Russia sanctions regime goes to the wire over a single oligarch**
EU ambassadors met from 9 a.m. today in what three diplomats described to the Politico Brussels Playbook as the final chance to renew restrictive measures on some 3,000 Russia-linked individuals and entities before they expire at midnight on Tuesday. The sticking point is France, which alongside Slovakia is [pushing to lift sanctions on the Russian-Uzbek billionaire Alisher Usmanov](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-france-alisher-usmanov-russia-sanctions/) — Euractiv reports the move is tied to a secretive prisoner exchange intended to free two French nationals held in Azerbaijan, while Politico refers to a single detained French citizen. "Ultimately, it's up to the French: will they let the entire sanctions regime collapse?" one diplomat told Politico. Euractiv adds a possible way out that Politico does not report: a bargain tightening EU visa restrictions on Russian nationals in exchange for easing the measures on Usmanov, leverage that eastern flank states believe they now hold after France and Italy [blocked a visa ban](https://www.euractiv.com/news/rapporteur-eu-gives-russians-more-tourist-visas/) on Russians who had fought in Ukraine back in July. An emergency one-week extension has already been used up, and compromise talks collapsed on Friday.

**Seventeen governments launch a push to pause EU lawmaking**
An Austrian-led coalition is demanding a twelve-month freeze on new EU legislation in favour of what Vienna's Europe minister Claudia Bauer calls a "year of simplification". Politico obtained the discussion paper, signed by Austria, Germany, Italy, Poland, Denmark, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Slovenia, Lithuania, Portugal and Hungary: it demands rigorous competitiveness screening of all new proposals, five-yearly impact reviews of existing rules, and a year devoted to stocktaking — "not deregulation, but an exercise in legislative deep-cleaning". [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/21/ue-barriere-dorate-e-tasse-indorate/), citing the *Financial Times*, reports that the initiative is in fact Italo-Austrian and that at least 17 governments were due to sign the resulting "Alliance for the reduction of the bureaucratic burden" declaration in Brussels today, with Ursula von der Leyen calling for "a pact against gold-plating" and the ECB estimating that internal regulatory barriers in the single market are equivalent to a tariff of 45–60 per cent on goods. The two sources read the initiative very differently: Politico presents it as a warning shot at the Commission from the deregulatory camp, while Phastidio argues the exercise is largely futile, since gold-plating exists precisely because national sectors lobby their own legislators to erect non-tariff barriers against EU rules that disadvantage them.

**Costa looks to new EU taxes rather than cuts**
European Council President António Costa told [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-budget-taxes-government-antonio-costa-deal-money-raise/) that headline cuts to the next budget will founder on national resistance once negotiators reach the detail. "It's easier to say 'I want to reduce the global volume' but it's much more difficult to reach a consensus to cut on the different policies funded by the budget," he said, recounting his tour of most EU capitals: "I ask, 'Who wants to cut on defence?' No one. 'Who wants to cut on competitiveness?' No one. 'Who wants to cut on agriculture?' No one." Everyone agrees on trimming EU administrative expenditure, he noted, but that is only 6 per cent of the total; an EU official told Politico there is greater appetite to cut the €200 billion earmarked for foreign aid. Costa's answer is new own resources, favouring levies that raise genuinely new revenue — vapes and e-cigarettes rather than the proposed tobacco tax, which capitals dislike because it taps money they already collect. Phastidio is blunter about what this means, noting that own resources amount to raising the overall tax burden on Europeans, and running through the alternatives now circulating: a turnover-based corporate levy known as CORE, and European Parliament proposals to tax gambling, cryptocurrencies and digital firms — the last already covered by national digital services taxes and exposed to retaliation from Washington.

## Italian Politics

**Meloni moves to ban face coverings in schools and cap foreign pupils**
Giorgia Meloni has announced two legislative proposals that would [ban face coverings in Italian schools and limit the number of foreign students per classroom](https://www.politico.eu/article/giorgia-meloni-ban-veils-italy-schools-limit-foreign-students/), according to Politico. **Rome co-sponsors the Brussels deregulation drive**
Italy is a co-author rather than merely a signatory of the simplification alliance launched in Brussels today, which Phastidio describes as an Italo-Austrian initiative. The same piece turns the argument back on Rome: Meloni's complaints about "European barriers", it argues, sit awkwardly with the fact that Italian governments have long been champions of gold-plating, the practice of layering national rules on top of EU legislation that creates many of those barriers in the first place.

## UK Politics

**More than half of Labour MPs sit on majorities smaller than their disability caseload**
An analysis by [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/labour-mps-pip-benefits-disability-welfare-andy-burnham-bgxb2s35d) finds that 212 of Labour's 366 seats in England and Wales — 58 per cent — contain more personal independence payment claimants than the size of the MP's majority, and that more than 100 Labour MPs have more claimants for mental health conditions alone than votes to spare. Wes Streeting is the most exposed cabinet minister, with 6,284 Pip claimants in Ilford North against a majority of 528. Sir Stephen Timms is leading a review of the benefit, having concluded in a July interim report that it was "no longer fit for purpose", in part because of the rising number of claims for mental health and invisible disabilities. Ministers are alert to the risk of a repeat of last year's welfare rebellion; Andy Burnham has said he will not be "squeamish" about cutting the welfare bill to fund defence, but warned against "crude cuts" in favour of a "preventative" approach.

**The Iran war has halved the chancellor's fiscal headroom**
Slowing growth and the rising cost of borrowing attributed to the Iran war have [wiped £12 billion from the government's fiscal headroom](https://www.thetimes.com/business/companies-markets/article/iran-war-and-slow-growth-slash-chancellors-headroom-in-half-2s3xht3hh), cutting it in half, The Times reports — a squeeze that lands directly on the welfare and defence trade-off above, and on a budget already the subject of a public argument over a mansion tax.

**Burnham heads to New York**
The prime minister travels to the UN General Assembly this week for a first meeting with Donald Trump, per the Politico Forecast newsletter, which expects him to back [Volodymyr Zelenskyy's request](https://www.politico.eu/article/what-andy-burnham-needs-from-donald-trump/) for additional Patriot missiles. New Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, who last month led a coalition of Western allies in sanctioning illegal Israeli settlements, attends his first G7 foreign ministers' meeting in New York today; Forecast flags the possibility of a public clash with Israel and its supporters in the Trump administration, and notes that France, the UK and Canada will chair a session on a two-state solution on Tuesday afternoon.

## Economy & Markets

**Oil retreats as diplomacy hopes return, but the diesel squeeze persists**
Crude [slid to an eleven-day low](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/oil-rises-after-houthi-attack-saudi-capital-2026-09-20/) on hopes of US-Iran diplomacy and resumed Saudi exports, according to Reuters, helping [Wall Street futures rise as AI stocks rallied](https://www.reuters.com/business/wall-st-futures-rise-ai-stocks-gain-oil-prices-slide-2026-09-21/). The relief is narrow: Reuters separately reports that the [global diesel shortage is likely to persist into 2027](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/global-diesel-shortage-likely-last-into-2027-storage-tanks-drain-2026-09-21/) as storage tanks drain, that [vessels are only trickling through the Strait of Hormuz](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/vessels-trickle-through-strait-hormuz-mideast-tension-persists-2026-09-21/), and that [most Gulf bourses closed lower](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/most-gulf-bourses-red-regional-friction-despite-diplomatic-hopes-2026-09-21/) despite the diplomatic signals.

**Fed signals it is not finished**
Chicago Fed president Austan Goolsbee said [strong demand may be adding to US inflation](https://www.reuters.com/business/feds-goolsbee-strong-demand-may-be-adding-us-inflation-no-ambiguity-how-fed-2026-09-21/) and that there is "no ambiguity" about how the Fed would respond, per Reuters. The remarks follow last week's decision, analysed by [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/17/fed-il-tasso-oltre-lostacolo-trump/), in which the Fed raised rates for the first time in three years and signalled further increases — a move Phastidio reads primarily as an assertion of independence from Donald Trump, who responded by repeating what it calls demented economic theories.

**French public debt hits a post-1978 record**
France's public debt is set to reach 119.3 per cent of GDP this year, its highest level in records going back to 1978, with the deficit climbing to 5.4 per cent, [Euractiv reports](https://www.euractiv.com/news/frances-debt-climbs-to-highest-since-1978/) citing the finance ministry. Debt is forecast to rise to 121.7 per cent in 2027, when the government plans €54 billion in budget cuts; France remains under special EU monitoring with its deficit well above the 3 per cent limit. Phastidio has been tracking the same hunt for €30 billion in the 2027 budget, arguing that Paris is relying on "savings" and special offers on selected taxes rather than genuine consolidation — and, in today's piece, that France's repeated overshoots go unsanctioned for the reason Jean-Claude Juncker once gave: "because France is France".

## Foreign Policy

**AI dominates a diminished General Assembly**
World leaders [returned to the UN](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/world-leaders-return-un-amid-wars-middle-east-ukraine-2026-09-20/) with wars running in the Middle East and Ukraine, but the Politico Forecast newsletter reports that the debate over AI safety — triggered by warnings from current and former Anthropic researchers — has taken over the corridors, with every delegation arriving armed with action plans and Sam Altman due to brief the Security Council on Wednesday. Forecast argues the venue itself tells the story: Xi Jinping is skipping UNGA entirely, flying instead to a two-day White House summit, with a Trump-Xi bilateral on Thursday. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has already met Vice Premier He Lifeng to prepare the ground, [proposing a US-China AI safety notification mechanism](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/us-treasurys-bessent-chinas-he-launch-talks-ai-trade-critical-minerals-2026-09-20/) and a new bilateral dialogue focused on national security, Reuters reports. Politico's China correspondent Phelim Kine is sceptical of substantive movement, noting that Washington and Beijing "are coming to the AI conversation from such radically different places", though both have an interest in controls preventing the development of new pathogens or access by non-state actors.

**Iran and the US trade threats as the regional war widens**
[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/iran-warns-against-new-escalation-by-us-allies-2026-09-20/) reports that Trump warned Iran would either fail economically or see its leadership wiped out if it did not make a deal, while the Iranian military promised to retaliate harshly to any fresh attack, after Houthi strikes — including on the Saudi capital — escalated the conflict. Trump's address to the assembly on Tuesday will seek to justify the war on the grounds of preventing Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, per Forecast, and he will meet Gulf leaders the same day; both the Iranian and Israeli leaders are on the speakers' list. France, meanwhile, will summon Iran's ambassador after Tehran [sealed a French language centre](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/france-vows-response-after-iran-closes-language-centre-tehran-2026-09-20/) attached to its embassy.

**Russia's managed election delivers United Russia its largest ever majority**
With over 95 per cent of ballots counted, Russia's central electoral commission announced preliminary results giving United Russia 57.8 per cent on a turnout of 59.3 per cent, [Le Grand Continent reports](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/21/le-parti-de-poutine-obtient-le-plus-grand-nombre-de-sieges-de-son-histoire-a-la-douma/) — 355 Duma seats, up 31 on 2021 and the largest bloc in the party's history, including 208 of the 225 single-member constituencies. Applying the Shpilkin method to results from more than 20,000 of roughly 89,000 polling stations, the analyst Piotr Zhizhin estimates turnout was inflated by about nine points and the United Russia vote by about sixteen, putting the party's real score at 41.9 per cent; some 27 per cent of stations reported average turnout of 90 per cent and a United Russia score near 70. Le Grand Continent also notes that 86 veterans of the war in Ukraine were elected on the party's list, a quarter of its deputies and 19 per cent of the chamber. Politico frames the vote through opposition figure Maxim Katz's verdict that ["there are no decent parties left"](https://www.politico.eu/article/there-are-no-decent-parties-left-sham-elections-underscore-putins-grip-on-russia/), and notes that Ukraine [hit Moscow oil refineries with a mass drone attack](https://www.politico.eu/article/ukraine-drone-attack-moscow-elections/) on the final day of voting.

**Moscow plans a 78 per cent increase in attack drone output**
Documents from a Russian state company obtained by [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/russias-secret-plan-to-supercharge-kamikaze-drone-production/) show a blueprint for expanding carbon fibre production that would translate into an additional 28,000 weaponised drones a year — a 78 per cent increase. Reporter Graham Lanktree links the plan to a drone attack on a passenger train near the Polish border this month and the failed drone attack at Leipzig airport in August: "Moscow has been bringing the conflict to Europe's doorstep." Zelenskyy has said he will meet Trump in New York this week, hinting the meeting "could change a lot", and addresses the assembly on Wednesday night.

**Washington, Copenhagen and Nuuk to sign an Arctic security deal**
The United States, Greenland and Denmark will sign an agreement on "strengthened security in the Arctic and North Atlantic Area" at UNGA, per a [Danish government statement](https://stm.dk/en/press/press-releases/expected-agreement-on-strengthened-security-in-the-arctic-and-the-north-atlantic-area) cited by Politico. The Euractiv *Rapporteur* newsletter adds the detail that the unpublished text gives Washington permanent military access and bars adversaries from bases or sensitive investments on the island; while Copenhagen and Nuuk stress that Danish sovereignty and Greenlandic self-determination are preserved, a US official said the agreement would allow Washington to build facilities without Danish or Greenlandic approval and would remain in force should Greenland become independent.
