# Daily Briefing — Monday, 21 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Merz vows to stay on after a double state-election defeat leaves the CDU at historic lows**
Germany's chancellor faces the gravest moment of his premiership after the far-right AfD came first in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Die Linke came first in Berlin. According to [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/20/mecklembourg-pomeranie-occidentale-et-berlin-lafd-progresse-la-gauche-en-position-de-gouverner-les-deux-regions/), first estimates put the AfD on 37 per cent in "Meckpomm" — almost double its 2021 score — narrowly ahead of Manuela Schwesig's SPD on 35.5 per cent, with the CDU scraping the threshold on 5.5 per cent, the worst regional result in the party's history; in Berlin the CDU shed eight points to 20 per cent while Die Linke took 24.5 per cent on a campaign built around rents and renewed calls to expropriate large landlords. Turnout rose sharply in both Länder, to 79 and 75 per cent respectively. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-far-right-secures-another-win-in-key-regional-election-exit-poll-says/) reports that Merz went before cameras within minutes of the first results, calling the outcome a "disaster" but insisting that "what needs to be done now requires backbone, steadfastness, and patience"; the [Euractiv newsletter](https://www.euractiv.com/) framed the night more starkly still, as the CDU's worst showing in any election since the Second World War. Le Grand Continent emphasises the parliamentary arithmetic rather than the drama — the AfD cannot form a government in either Land, and red-red-green coalitions are the likeliest outcome in both — while Politico's Nette Nöstlinger stresses the leadership question, noting that Merz pre-empted criticism but that "we will have to wait for tomorrow to see if people are actually going to speak up publicly against him."

**Greenland deal hands Washington permanent security control, with the fine print still unpublished**
The agreement announced by President Trump on 18 September has landed better in European capitals than many feared, though its terms remain largely undisclosed. Per [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/19/que-sait-on-de-laccord-que-donald-trump-a-annonce-sur-le-groenland/), Trump says the United States will have "all the freedom necessary to take the measures required" in Greenland, plus a veto over foreign military bases and investments deemed sensitive, with the arrangement surviving any future Greenlandic independence; neither Copenhagen nor Nuuk has confirmed the investment veto, which had repeatedly been a red line in negotiations. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said the deal strengthens security in the Arctic and North Atlantic while recognising the Kingdom of Denmark's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and Greenland's Jens-Frederik Nielsen called it "in everyone's interest". The [Politico Sunday Crunch](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/sunday-crunch/building-the-orange-wall/) notes the existing 1951 defence arrangement already granted Washington considerable latitude — a point Danish commentators have used to mock the deal as a restatement of the status quo — with [signature expected](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/18/trump-denmark-greenland-new-security-agreement-01084764) on the margins of the UN General Assembly. Le Grand Continent adds a colder reading: the deal ends a running source of NATO friction and hands Trump a diplomatic win as the Iran war drags on, but it is unclear whether a president who called anything short of annexation "unacceptable" will be satisfied with terms obtainable through ordinary diplomacy.

## Italian Politics

**Meloni moves to ban face veils in schools and cap foreign pupils per class**
The prime minister used a party youth event on Sunday to announce two measures aimed squarely at her coalition's base. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/giorgia-meloni-ban-veils-italy-schools-limit-foreign-students/) reports plans to ban veils in Italian schools and to limit the number of foreign students. Additional context via [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/09/20/meloni-announces-ban-on-burqa-and-niqab-in-italian-schools-and-cap-on-foreign-students): the measure would prohibit burqas and niqabs, extend to any head covering worn according to Islamic tradition for girls up to 14, and require parents of foreign pupils struggling to integrate to learn Italian; a statutory cap on foreign students per class — the figure was not specified — is to go to a Cabinet meeting shortly and would make Italy the first European country to adopt such a rule. Non-Italian citizens currently account for 11.6 per cent of school enrolments.

## UK Politics

**Davey bids for Tory defectors with a £2bn fuel duty cut as the Lib Dems position for a snap election**
The Liberal Democrat conference in Brighton has been dominated by tax. Per the [Politico Sunday Crunch](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/sunday-crunch/building-the-orange-wall/), Ed Davey is calling for a 10p cut in fuel duty — worth 12p a litre at the pumps — and the scrapping of January's planned 3p rise, a £2bn package lasting three months. In a [Sunday Times interview](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/ed-davey-courts-tory-voters-with-call-for-10p-fuel-duty-cut-bx5zdb7ds), Davey called taxes "historic high" and said the cut is the party's "main vehicle" for lowering the cost of living, promising details on permanent, affordable cuts in Tuesday's speech. Politico frames the week as a positioning exercise — a bigger "orange wall" as the only thing standing between Britain and what Davey calls Farage and Badenoch's "Trump satellite state" — while the Times reports the underlying electoral strategy is narrower: consolidating disgruntled ex-Conservatives unhappy with Badenoch's net zero and ECHR positions. Politico's Poll of Polls has Labour on 24 per cent, Reform UK 22, the Conservatives 18, Greens 11 and the Lib Dems 10.

**Burnham meets Trump at UNGA with Patriots for Ukraine at the top of the list**
The prime minister's first in-person encounter with the US president comes on Tuesday in New York rather than the Oval Office. [Sunday Crunch](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/sunday-crunch/building-the-orange-wall/) reports the Sunday Times understands the closed-door meeting is intended to run at least 30 minutes, organised partly through No. 10 chief of staff James Purnell's contacts at the US Embassy; Bloomberg reports Burnham's priority is persuading Trump to release Patriot air defence missiles to Ukraine, possibly via a joint event with Macron and Zelensky. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/what-andy-burnham-needs-from-donald-trump/) lists the flashpoints: North Sea drilling, sanctions on Israeli settlers, defence spending, a social media ban, and the Chagos and Falkland Islands. Bridget Phillipson, on the broadcast round, offered the emollient line that "the US is about more than one individual" and that presidents and prime ministers "come and go, but we have an enduring relationship."

## Economy & Markets

**The Fed raises rates for the first time in three years and signals more to come**
[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/17/fed-il-tasso-oltre-lostacolo-trump/) reports the FOMC lifted rates a quarter point to 3.75–4 per cent in a unanimous vote, with sixteen of the eighteen participating members projecting at least one further increase before the end of 2026. Chair Kevin Warsh told the press conference the committee had "removed a dose of accommodation" — meaning policy is not yet neutral — and dismissed the natural rate of interest as "academically useful but with no operational effect" on the decision. Seminerio reads the move as the Fed asserting its independence from political power but not from markets, which had threatened turmoil had rates been held, and notes the awkward admission at the heart of it: higher rates cannot reduce crude prices when the shortage is physical. His broader worry is that US inflation has run above target continuously for five years, and that a growing share of Treasuries is now held by hedge funds able to exit at speed. Trump, unsurprisingly, [did not take it well](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/17/fed-il-tasso-oltre-lostacolo-trump/), restating his view that rates should fall when the economy is doing well.

## Foreign Policy

**Ukraine strikes Moscow refineries as Russia's managed elections close**
[Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/ukraine-drone-attack-moscow-elections/) reports Ukraine hit oil refineries around Moscow with a mass drone attack on the final day of voting in Russia's parliamentary elections — a ballot from which anti-war candidates were sidelined. Opposition figure Maxim Katz's verdict, quoted by [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/there-are-no-decent-parties-left-sham-elections-underscore-putins-grip-on-russia/), was that "there are no decent parties left." Separately, a [Politico scoop](https://www.politico.eu/article/russias-secret-plan-to-supercharge-kamikaze-drone-production/) by Graham Lanktree and Phelim Kine, drawing on Russian state-company plans, reports a blueprint for expanded carbon fibre production that would translate into an additional 28,000 weaponised drones a year — a 78 per cent increase. Lanktree links this directly to Europe: "with a drone attack on a passenger train near Poland's border this month and the failed drone attack at Leipzig airport in August, Moscow has been bringing the conflict to Europe's doorstep."

**A UN week dominated by AI, with the most consequential meeting happening in Washington**
World leaders gather in New York this week with wars in the Middle East and Ukraine on the agenda, as [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/world-leaders-return-un-amid-wars-middle-east-ukraine-2026-09-20/) reports. The [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/forecast/ai-talk-no-action/) newsletter argues the topic that has actually captured the diplomatic corps is AI safety, following recent warnings from current and former Anthropic researchers: OpenAI's Sam Altman briefs the Security Council on Wednesday, while Trump points to a promised "AI Force" and AI czar that, Politico notes, [nobody outside the Oval Office can define](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/09/19/tech-industry-scratches-head-over-ai-force-01085319). Tellingly, Xi Jinping arrives in the United States on Wednesday but is skipping UNGA entirely in favour of a two-day White House summit. Politico's Phelim Kine is blunt about the prospects: the two sides are "coming to the AI conversation from such radically different places" that little beyond vague cooperative language should be expected, though both have a shared interest in preventing the technology from enabling new pathogens or reaching non-state actors.

**Houthis claim first strike on Riyadh since the escalation began**
[Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/saudi-civil-defence-sends-all-clear-after-danger-warning-capital-riyadh-2026-09-19/) reports flames and smoke seen near King Khalid International Airport after the Iranian-backed Yemeni group claimed missile and drone attacks on "sensitive" sites in the Saudi capital.
