# Daily Briefing — Wednesday, 29 July 2026

## EU Affairs

**Merz draws the EU budget battle lines — demanding hundreds of billions in cuts**
German Chancellor Friedrich Merz dramatically escalated pressure on the EU's next long-term budget negotiations on Tuesday, declaring the Commission's €2 trillion proposal "unacceptable" and "not balanced" and calling for cuts "totalling several hundred billion euros overall." Speaking at a press conference with Irish Taoiseach Micheál Martin in Dublin, Merz drew a further red line under the Commission's proposed corporate levy, calling it "out of the question." As [Euractiv reports](https://www.euractiv.com/), Ireland's Council presidency faces the task of distilling competing national demands into a revised negotiating box ahead of an October leaders' summit. The Euractiv Rapporteur newsletter describes an autumn agenda dominated by the clash between two entrenched camps: the "modernisers" (the frugal states) pushing for a leaner budget focused on defence and competitiveness, and the "Friends of Cohesion" determined to preserve agriculture and regional development spending. New own resources — ranging from levies on tobacco and electronic waste to a corporate contribution — remain another unresolved flashpoint. The [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/politico-forecast/) notes that Merz's intervention comes as Mélenchon, France's leading far-left presidential candidate, has also signalled he would end the Franco-German partnership as currently configured, adding fresh uncertainty to the EU's institutional core.

**FIFA World Cup sell-off sparks European backlash — and competition law warnings**
FIFA's proposal to sell a stake in the commercial rights to the World Cup and other tournaments has provoked a furious reaction across Europe, with Brussels warning it could breach EU competition law. According to [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/), a Commission official warned the reported scheme to "effectively auction off the World Cup" risks violating EU rules. The [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/) reports that Italian footballer-turned-MEP Carolina Morace told Playbook the sport "cannot be governed by the logic of the financial markets," while Bulgarian liberal MEP Nikola Minchev slammed the plans as "absurd." MEP Bogdan Zdrojewski, the Parliament's lead lawmaker on the European Sport Model, called for FIFA President Gianni Infantino to be summoned before the Parliament. UEFA declared it "will not stand by while FIFA seeks to reshape the future of football around the interests of private investors," and talk of a European boycott circulated on Tuesday evening. UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham added his voice, saying the tournament "belongs to the people who fill the stands."

**Italy hesitates over EU defence loans as Commission sounds the alarm**
The Commission has warned Italy it has "no time to lose" in activating its €14.9 billion allocation under the EU's SAFE (Security Action for Europe) defence loan scheme, according to the [Euractiv Rapporteur](https://www.euractiv.com/). Confusion erupted in Rome on Tuesday when Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani initially announced Italy would draw the full amount, before walking back the claim: Italy had only "booked" the maximum allocation and would decide by year end whether to request between €6 billion and €9 billion. Brussels warned that waiting until December would not leave enough time to redistribute unused funds. The uncertainty also exposed divisions within the coalition, with Matteo Salvini's League explicitly opposing taking on the loans. The [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/) adds that the Commission is meanwhile preparing to redirect any unspent SAFE funding to Ukraine's drone industry.

**Montenegro advances toward EU membership — and a 25th official language**
The EU is already doing the practical groundwork for Montenegrin to become the bloc's 25th official language, the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/) reports. The Council's legal service is recruiting assistants to help draft the country's accession treaty and translate legislation. Montenegro has closed over half of the accession chapters and aims to become the EU's 28th member by 2028. Montenegrin will use the Latin alphabet in EU documents, though both Latin and Cyrillic are taught in Montenegrin schools. Language politics in the Balkans remain fraught: Serbia has contested Montenegrin as a distinct language, while Podgorica has enshrined it constitutionally. Separately, the [Euractiv Rapporteur](https://www.euractiv.com/) flags that the autumn agenda will turn to whether further Ukrainian and Moldovan accession clusters can be opened despite Hungarian objections, and that EU leaders are expected to chart a revised accession roadmap at their October summit.

## Italian Politics

**Tajani floats pre-election budget wish-list — but the financing is a mirage**
With elections approaching and coalition arithmetic uncertain, Forza Italia secretary Antonio Tajani has unveiled an array of fiscal proposals for the 2027 budget law, outlined in an interview with *Libero*. These include the full or partial exemption of thirteenth-month pay from income tax, the abolition or reduction of the annual vehicle ownership tax (*bollo*), a cut in the IRPEF bracket from 35% to 33% for incomes up to €60,000, and a flat rate on incremental income above the previous year's declared figure. As [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/27/il-pozzo-di-tajani-e-i-risparmi-degli-italiani/) notes in a sharply critical analysis, the Berlusconi-era formula of "meno tasse, meno tasse, meno tasse" has been dusted off with little explanation of how the measures would be funded. Tajani's proposal to plug the gap by "tapping into" Italy's €1,800–€2,000 billion in private savings — directing them into infrastructure PPPs — is described by Phastidio as a candid articulation of financial repression, noting that such schemes are voluntary and therefore provide no actual fiscal cover. The piece also flags a factual error: the 35% IRPEF bracket Tajani proposes to cut no longer exists following recent reforms.

## Economy & Markets

**Google faces wave of private lawsuits after landmark €1 billion EU fine**
A decades-long crackdown on Google's European business practices is entering a new and potentially far more costly phase. As [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/) reports, Google's loss in the first case brought against it under new EU legislation has opened the door to a wave of private lawsuits that could demand up to $10 billion in damages. The [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/) describes this as "a costly new phase" for the company, with rivals now lining up to pursue their claims.

**Global finance watchdog chief warns of AI bubble**
John Schindler, secretary-general of the Financial Stability Board (FSB), has warned that global finance could be heading for an AI bubble akin to the dotcom boom or the 2008 financial crisis, the [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/politico-forecast/) reports. Schindler's concern centres on financial bets concentrated in a small number of large technology companies, combined with hedge fund leverage and bank exposures, which he says could amplify any price correction into a broader shock.

**Beijing ends two decades of tax exemption for offshore billionaires**
China's Ministry of Finance has announced a 20% tax on capital gains and income from offshore trusts used by wealthy Chinese nationals, after more than twenty years of benign neglect, as [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/07/28/ventanni-senza-tasse-per-i-miliardari-cinesi/) reports in depth. Authorities are also pressing companies to repatriate legal headquarters from offshore centres to the Chinese mainland, and are requiring that proceeds from share sales — including Hong Kong IPOs — be remitted to China for taxation before being reinvested abroad. Phastidio traces the origins of the loophole to the early 2000s, when Tencent and other Chinese tech giants used Cayman Islands holding structures to list abroad, creating fortunes outside Chinese capital controls. In a first enforcement action, a wealth management firm founded by a former Tencent employee was fined $271 million in May for facilitating illicit cross-border investments; the firm managed $156 billion in assets.

## Foreign Policy

**Zelenskyy secures diplomatic wins in Washington**
Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's visit to Washington for the funeral of Senator Lindsey Graham — who was among Kyiv's most forceful advocates in Congress — produced two significant outcomes. The [Euractiv Rapporteur](https://www.euractiv.com/) reports that during a White House meeting, Trump agreed to send envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner to Ukraine for the first time, in an attempt to revive peace talks; the two sides also discussed expanding Patriot interceptor production and further US military support. The US Senate voted to advance a bipartisan Russia sanctions bill that Graham had championed. The [Politico Forecast](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/politico-forecast/) contextualises Zelenskyy's smoother Washington performance against his disastrous February 2025 Oval Office visit, noting that he has since demonstrably learned "how Washington works in Trump's second term" — meeting far-right influencer Laura Loomer in Kyiv, attending in formal attire, posting effusively about Trump, and arriving from a position of military strength after Ukraine established drone supremacy over the battlefield. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has said Ukraine now has "the strongest, most powerful" army in Europe.

**Ukraine and US discuss aerial ceasefire — as drone war reaches new intensity**
According to [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/29/lukraine-pourrait-elle-amener-moscou-a-accepter-un-cessez-le-feu-aerien/), Kyiv and Washington have begun discussions on formulating a proposal for an aerial ceasefire to present to Russia. The Kremlin's spokesman Dmitri Peskov called the reports "not reliable" on Sunday and said it was "too early to comment." The backdrop is a Ukrainian drone campaign of unprecedented scale: between June 2025 and June 2026, Ukraine struck more than 350,000 Russian targets with drones; last month Ukraine launched twice as many drones as Russia (11,600 versus 5,700). Ukraine has recently begun targeting Wildberries warehouses — Russia's largest e-commerce platform — striking at least ten mega-warehouses that, according to Le Grand Continent, contain dual-use goods and military equipment. The idea of an aerial truce was first floated by Russia in the summer of 2025 ahead of the Trump-Putin Alaska summit; Le Grand Continent notes that Russia has repeatedly violated prior ceasefires.

**Iran: Trump claims "good talks," attacks continue**
President Trump said the US and Tehran are having "good talks" and a deal is possible, even as Saudi Arabia, Jordan and Iraq all reported drone attacks attributed to Iranian-backed militias, the [Thomson Reuters Daily Briefing](https://www.thomsonreuters.com/) and [Reuters Iran Briefing](https://www.reuters.com/) report. Iran said it is not seeking to formally resume peace negotiations, though it would pause its own attacks for as long as Trump's self-imposed ceasefire holds. The US military said it had thwarted a "surprise" Iranian attack on its forces and struck Tehran-backed militias in Iraq. A Reuters/Ipsos poll found that just one in three Americans supports the war on Iran — the lowest figure since the five-month-old conflict began — with most respondents saying Trump has failed to explain his goals.

**US-France diplomatic row at the United Nations**
A sharp diplomatic confrontation erupted between Washington and Paris after the United States voted on 24 July alongside Russia, North Korea, Mali, Burkina Faso and Nicaragua against renewing the mandate of UN Human Rights Commissioner Volker Türk. As [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/28/apres-un-vote-aux-cotes-de-la-russie-et-de-la-coree-du-nord-a-lonu-les-etats-unis-accusent-la-france-de-complaisance-a-legard-de-regimes-dictatoriaux/) reports in detail, 144 countries voted in favour of Türk's renewal. France's Geneva mission publicly accused the US of being "isolated" and declared that it was "no longer a beacon of human rights." Washington retaliated: American diplomats walked out of a Security Council meeting on Monday when France's representative took the floor, and US Ambassador Mike Waltz accused Paris of "coddling some of the worst human rights abusers." US Deputy Representative Dan Negrea said the protest would continue until France "renounces its condescending and disrespectful remarks." Le Grand Continent situates the episode within a broader pattern: since Trump's return to power, Washington has voted alongside Russia, North Korea and Mali at the UN on multiple occasions, including on Ukraine sovereignty resolutions in February and April 2025.
