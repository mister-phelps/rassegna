# Daily Briefing — Saturday, 19 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Von der Leyen's European Security Council runs into a wall of scepticism**
The centrepiece of Ursula von der Leyen's State of the Union address — a European Security Council bringing together Canada, Norway, Ukraine and the UK outside NATO — has been met with unusually blunt resistance in national capitals. According to the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/europes-security-council-plan-runs-into-a-wall-of-skepticism/), Denmark's Mette Frederiksen, touted in Danish media as a possible chair of the body after European Council President António Costa pitched it to leaders in Finland, wants nothing to do with it; a Danish official told the newsletter the prime minister "is very into all things involving security in Europe, but we are quite skeptical about this construction. Basically, we have a lot of meetings." A second complaint is confidentiality: one EU diplomat said defence officials "don't feel comfortable discussing sensitive matters in EU formats." [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/opinion/editors-choice-any-more-bright-ideas/) frames the episode more structurally, arguing the speech "serves only to highlight the gulf between rhetoric and reality" and that the accompanying European Emergency Security Protocol — likened by von der Leyen to NATO's Article 4 — risks duplicating an alliance that is already becoming more Europe-oriented. RUSI director-general Rachel Ellehuus asked whether it would not simply create "more process".

**Germany and Spain open the €2 trillion budget war in duelling op-eds**
The fight over the EU's 2028-34 Multiannual Financial Framework has moved into the open. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-and-spain-go-head-to-head-in-eu-budget-fight/) reports from Dublin, where Costa stopped on his tour of 25 capitals, that Spanish Economy Minister Carlos Cuerpo has [argued](https://www.politico.eu/article/a-common-budget-that-makes-common-sense/) for a ceiling equivalent to 2% of EU gross national income, plus a delay to post-Covid debt repayments that would free roughly €11 billion a year for agriculture, defence and competitiveness. Friedrich Merz, writing with the leaders of Denmark, the Netherlands, Austria and Finland, rejects any increase: "We cannot just add every new priority to every old one and send the bill to taxpayers." The five are pressing the Irish presidency to strip hundreds of billions of euros from its next negotiating box, though officials expect a compromise involving shallower cuts. After nearly a year of talks the sides remain far apart ahead of an informal end-of-year deadline.

**Commission accused of burying Russian visa figures**
The Commission circulated a non-public Schengen report to member states this week that omitted the customary figures on visas issued to Russian nationals, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-countries-split-as-russian-visa-numbers-climb-again/) reports, citing several diplomats. Around nine countries questioned the omission, with Nordic and Baltic governments — which have pushed for tighter restrictions on security grounds — among the most vocal; some diplomats said larger issuing states, particularly France, had lobbied for the numbers to be left out, an allegation a French diplomat rejected. The Commission told Euractiv it does not comment on non-public documents. The row resurfaced after the Leipzig/Halle airport case, in which a Belarusian man holding a Russian passport, believed to have entered the Schengen area on a tourist visa issued by Italy, is under investigation over an alleged drone attack on a Ukrainian cargo aircraft. Euractiv's own April reporting put the 2025 total at more than 477,000 tourist visas for Russian nationals — 77% of all visas issued to Russians and an 8.4% rise on 2024 — with France, Italy and Spain the largest issuers. Almost the same dispute erupted a year ago.

**Parliament's chief budget negotiator is nominated as Romania's prime minister**
President Nicușor Dan has proposed EPP MEP Siegfried Mureșan as prime minister, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/siegfried-muresan-proposed-as-romanian-pm-amid-political-crisis/) reports, with Romania still mired in the political crisis that followed the collapse of Ilie Bolojan's government in May. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/newsletter/rapporteur/) notes that Mureșan has ten days to assemble a cabinet and secure the 233 votes needed, and remains an MEP while he tries. Should he succeed, Parliament would lose its co-rapporteur on the 2028-34 budget alongside the S&D's Carla Tavares — at precisely the moment the MFF fight is intensifying.

**Merz braces for a double state-election test**
Germany's chancellor faces votes in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and Berlin on Sunday that may determine whether he survives as leader. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-friedrich-merz-political-survival-fight-regional-elections/) reports he is bracing for another brutal result and for renewed questions about his position. Euractiv puts the CDU close to the 5% threshold in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania, where the AfD narrowly leads the governing SPD, and level with the Left in Berlin; Merz has summoned his party executive for a post-election meeting and will skip the UN General Assembly in New York. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/merzs-fate-balance-second-eastern-german-state-goes-polls-2026-09-16/) emphasises his reliance on the popular centre-left state premier in the east to keep the far right out. In Berlin, Merz and Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil are meanwhile haggling over how to fund relief on fuel prices before the vote.

**Carney rebuffs Trump in Strasbourg as Attal calls for a "United States of Europe"**
Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney told the European Parliament that closer EU-Canada ties are "a positive initiative" and "not a reaction to something else," pushing back against Donald Trump's threat of fresh tariffs should von der Leyen's offer of "associate member" status be intended as a hostile act, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/newsletter/rapporteur/) reports; Carney added that the precise label was a matter for the EU and that "what matters is the substance". [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/17/mark-carney-et-la-nouvelle-alliance-transatlantique/) has published the full text of the speech. Separately, former French prime minister and 2027 candidate Gabriel Attal used a [doctrine piece](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/17/le-moment-des-etats-unis-deurope-est-venu/) in the same outlet to call for a "United States of Europe", dismissing the State of the Union address as "too little, too late". [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/time-to-build-the-united-states-of-europe-says-french-presidential-hopeful/) notes the awkwardness: Renew Europe chief Valérie Hayer has worked on Attal's European platform from the outset, while his partner Stéphane Séjourné is France's commissioner.

**The EU Kids Act lands, and avoids a blanket teen ban**
Von der Leyen presented her online child-safety initiative on Thursday. Per [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/news/eu-kids-act-targets-overly-friendly-ai-chatbots/), the proposal reaches well beyond social media to gaming, app stores and AI chatbots, and stops short of a blanket ban: under-13s would be denied accounts, while 13- to 15-year-olds could hold "mini accounts" requiring parental approval, with restricted social features and a one-hour daily limit. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/parents-privacy-parliaments-the-battle-begins-on-von-der-leyens-social-media-ban/) is more sceptical about delivery, reporting that bringing the plan to life will require "a whole new level of ambition". How member states eyeing their own national bans respond will determine whether the bloc-wide law supersedes them.

**The Bank of England signals rate rises as the Iran war feeds through to inflation**
The Bank is likely to raise interest rates if the war in Iran continues, and warns inflation is on track to reach 4% early next year, [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/bank-of-england-interest-rate-rises-inflation-iran-war-andrew-bailey-lfsdhqbxk) reports. ## Economy & Markets

**The Fed raises rates for the first time in three years**
The FOMC has lifted rates by a quarter point to 3.75-4%, unanimously, and signalled it is not finished, [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/17/fed-il-tasso-oltre-lostacolo-trump/) reports: sixteen of the eighteen participants project at least one further increase by year-end. Chair Kevin Warsh told the press conference the committee had "removed a dose of accommodation" — meaning policy is not yet neutral — and dismissed the neutral rate as "academically useful" but without operational bearing. Mario Seminerio reads the move as the Fed asserting independence from the White House while remaining beholden to markets, and flags the central contradiction: higher rates cannot conjure crude that is physically absent, yet US inflation has now run above target for five uninterrupted years, and if markets conclude the target is fictional "anything can happen". Trump, predictably, objected, restating his view that rates should fall when the economy is strong.

**The Bank of Japan goes to a 31-year high and pivots pre-emptive**
The BoJ raised rates to their highest level in 31 years and signalled readiness to keep going, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/bank-japan-set-raise-interest-rates-31-year-high-2026-09-17/) reports, with Governor Kazuo Ueda saying the bank had entered a new phase focused on preventing inflation from overshooting its target. Taken with the Fed's move and the Bank of England's warnings, the major central banks are now converging on the same posture — tightening into supply-driven inflation.

**War premia reach the real economy**

## Foreign Policy

**China presses Tehran on the Houthis as Riyadh runs out of options**
China has privately asked Iran to help rein in the Houthis following an appeal from Saudi Arabia after the group's military blitz, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/china-presses-iran-help-rein-houthis-after-saudi-appeal-sources-say-2026-09-17/) reports in an exclusive. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/17/que-peut-larabie-saoudite-face-aux-houthis/) sets out why Riyadh's hand is so weak: the group seized control of the Bab el-Mandeb strait in a matter of days, and the East-West pipeline carrying most Saudi export crude was struck on 11 September by drones launched from Iraq. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright expects transit to resume "within days", though analysts put repairs at several weeks; Abu Dhabi Commercial Bank estimates the kingdom's GDP could contract 4.5% in 2026 if the pipeline stays shut for a month with Hormuz blocked. Saudi Arabia has launched more than 450 strikes on Houthi positions but is far below its engagement of a decade ago, and Trump declined Mohammed bin Salman's request to intervene — while American diplomats met Houthi representatives at the US embassy in Oman last weekend, where the group reportedly reaffirmed it would spare commercial shipping other than Saudi-owned vessels. Separately, the State Department approved a possible $24.3 billion sale of F-35s to Riyadh, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/us-state-department-oks-possible-243-billion-military-aircraft-sale-saudi-arabia-2026-09-17/) reports.

**A repurposed target drone drives Russia's air campaign to record levels**
Russia's mass deployment of missiles originally designed as target practice has taken its aerial onslaught on Ukraine to record intensity, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/ukraine-russia-war/revamped-missile-leading-russias-ballistic-onslaught-ukraine-2026-09-18/) reports, at a moment when Ukraine has virtually exhausted its US-made Patriot interceptors. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/russia-hits-passenger-trains-to-break-the-backbone-of-ukraines-economy-morale/) covers a parallel line of attack, with Russia increasingly targeting the railways that carry Ukraine's troops, civilians and economy. Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said a "massive explosion" during a major strike on western Ukraine hit near Yahodyn, close to the Polish border — probably a petrol station near the Dorohusk crossing — prompting Warsaw to scramble fighter jets and issue air-raid alerts in the Lublin and Podkarpackie regions, per [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/newsletter/rapporteur/).

**The anatomy of a Russian sabotage network in Europe**
[Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/18/qui-est-oemis-romagoza-durruthy-le-professeur-de-danse-cubain-recrute-par-moscou-pour-mener-des-operations-de-sabotage-en-europe/) profiles Oemis Romagoza Durruthy, a Cuban dance teacher resident in Petrozavodsk who was indicted on 15 September by the US Justice Department, alongside four others, on terrorism-financing conspiracy charges. Operating under the aliases "Adrian" and "Dios", he recruited at least a dozen people through Latin American Facebook and Telegram groups from 2024, offering up to $1,500 for photographs of industrial sites before escalating to arson and sabotage — targets included building-materials firms in Poland, a bus garage in Czechia, a recycling centre in Romania and a Lithuanian manufacturer supplying the Ukrainian army. At least nine of his recruits have been arrested, two serving six- to eight-year sentences. The same network allegedly sought to recruit someone for $40,000 to assassinate a Russian dissident living in the United States — which [The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/world/russia-ukraine-war/article/russia-attack-dissidents-us-soil-8fv9vtmbs) reports on from the Justice Department's angle, asking whether Moscow is preparing to attack dissidents on American soil.
