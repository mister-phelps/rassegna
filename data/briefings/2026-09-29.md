# Daily Briefing — Tuesday, 29 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Brussels scrambles to head off a winter energy crisis**
EU energy commissioner Dan Jørgensen is chairing urgent talks in Dublin today on securing the bloc's supply, with ministers from the UK, Ukraine and Norway attending discussions on possible energy partnerships, according to the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/europes-scramble-for-energy/), which cites three diplomats and officials. The alarm level rose over the weekend when Jørgensen wrote to EU capitals [urging them to consider energy savings](https://www.politico.eu/article/eu-commission-calls-for-gas-electricity-demand-cuts/) on the scale of the measures adopted after Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022; gas stores are running low and NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte has pressed allies to expand oil-refining capacity. The EU's Oil Coordination Group also meets today to assess the consequences of a possible US ban on diesel exports, which Donald Trump said on Sunday he was still considering "very seriously" after the White House denied reports of a 90-day suspension. A Commission official told Politico that Brussels reads the diesel threat as driven by domestic politics ahead of November's US midterms. The price backdrop is unforgiving: [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/la-guerre-en-iran-entre-dans-son-huitieme-mois/) puts Brent at $107, more than 50 per cent above the same point last year.

**Commission overrode its own advice in weakening car CO2 rules**
An internal briefing document obtained by Politico shows the European Commission disregarded its own recommendation to maintain the bloc-wide combustion engine ban, bowing instead to carmakers and powerful national capitals, [Politico reports](https://www.politico.eu/article/commission-ignored-its-own-advice-before-weakening-car-co2-rules/). The disclosure sharpens a long-running argument over whether the EU's competitiveness agenda is being used to unpick its climate framework.

**Where does "Made in EU" end? Britain, Turkey and Canada all want in**
The Commission's Industrial Accelerator Act, presented in March, introduces "Made in EU" and low-emissions content requirements in public procurement and subsidies for strategic sectors including steel, cement, aluminium, cars and net-zero technologies — and a dispute has now opened over how wide that perimeter should be drawn. As [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/28/siamo-sovrani-fateci-entrare/) reports, France wants it confined as closely as possible to the single market, while Germany — invoking its free-trade tradition and its automotive supply chains — wants Turkey included via its 1996 customs union, preferring the formulation "Made with EU"; German-Turkish goods trade was worth roughly $52bn in 2025. Britain, meanwhile, is pressing to be recognised as a "trusted partner": UK-EU automotive trade alone is worth about €80bn a year and supply chains cross the Channel continuously. Phastidio frames the British position as a study in contradiction — London asking Brussels to acknowledge integrated supply chains while simultaneously moving towards "Buy British" in its own £400bn-a-year procurement — and notes that a 2019 European study already found 84 per cent of UK public purchasing went to domestic suppliers. Canada is also a candidate, with Ursula von der Leyen having spoken of an "associate membership" that Phastidio points out has no legal existence.

**Five member states set a 2027 timetable for deportations to Rwanda**
Migrants whose asylum claims are rejected in the EU could be sent to third countries such as Rwanda or Uganda from next year, Greek Migration Minister Thanos Plevris told [Politico's Brussels Playbook Capitals](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/first-big-test-for-italys-far-right-general/) in an interview. Denmark, Austria, Greece, Germany and the Netherlands — the "Core Group" — are aiming for a pilot in the first half of 2027 and full operation in the second; several diplomats say the first deal will be with Rwanda, with Uganda and Uzbekistan also under discussion. Plevris stressed the plan involves designated return countries rather than detention facilities: "If your asylum has been rejected, you will either return to your country of origin or to a third country outside the EU." He will put a separate Greek emergency scheme — temporarily suspending asylum applications for new arrivals — to EU interior ministers on Thursday. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/send-rejected-asylum-seekers-out-of-eu-asap-urges-greek-minister/) reports the same push from the Brussels end.

**The Commission weighs scrapping EPSO**
The European Commission is considering far-reaching changes to EPSO, its civil service exam board, and may abolish it outright, two EU officials told [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/europes-scramble-for-energy/). One official described the last open hiring round — which drew a record 174,922 applicants — as "completely unmanageable", adding that "the cost of administering this sort of mass exam is out of proportion to what we get out of it"; a second said the current model is "reaching its limits", with hiring taking two to three years. Cristiano Sebastiani of the Renouveau et Démocratie staff union argued EPSO should evolve rather than be abandoned, warning that "abolishing it entirely would be the wrong message to EU citizens."

## Italian Politics

**Vannacci's first electoral test ends in a modest but pointed 9 per cent**
The Reggio Calabria by-election — the first contest fought by Futuro Nazionale, the party Roberto Vannacci founded after leaving the League in February — gave Forza Italia's Fabio Roscioli a clear lead on 54 per cent, with centre-left candidate Francesco Benedetto on 36 per cent and Futuro Nazionale's Domenico Giannetta on 9 per cent, on partial counts from 263 of 487 polling stations, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/le-candidat-de-futuro-nazionale-le-parti-du-general-roberto-vannacci-obtiendrait-9-des-voix-en-calabre/) reports. Turnout collapsed to 22.34 per cent from 49.4 per cent in 2022. The two sources read the result differently: [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/le-candidat-de-futuro-nazionale-le-parti-du-general-roberto-vannacci-obtiendrait-9-des-voix-en-calabre/) frames it as reassuring for Giorgia Meloni while noting that Futuro Nazionale's growth comes at the League's expense and confronts her, for the first time, with an opposition to her right; [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/first-big-test-for-italys-far-right-general/) had framed the vote in advance as a test of whether Vannacci "just draws crowds or can drum up an army of voters", arguing he did not need to win to spoil the coalition's evening — beating his 8 per cent national polling would suffice. Giannetta had until recently led Forza Italia's group on the Calabrian regional council and defected after the party picked Roscioli, a Rome-based lawyer close to the Berlusconi family. The campaign closed on a sour note: Vannacci shared an AI-altered image of Forza Italia undersecretary Matilde Siracusano, drawing condemnation across the political spectrum, and claimed the resulting coverage lifted his party by 1.5 points.

**First judicial verdict in the Regeni case, ten years after the abduction**
A Rome court convicted three of the four Egyptian intelligence officers tried in absentia over the 2016 abduction, torture and death of Italian PhD student Giulio Regeni, sentencing Major Magdi Ibrahim Abdelal Sharif and Colonels Athar Kamel Mohamed Ibrahim and Uhsam Helmi to ten years each for kidnapping, while acquitting General Tariq Sabir; the murder charge did not stand (additional context via [Il Post](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/09/28/sentenza-primo-grado-omicidio-giulio-regeni/) and [Il Fatto Quotidiano](https://www.ilfattoquotidiano.it/2026/09/28/caso-regeni-sentenza-007-egiziani-oggi/8520383/)). Prosecutors had sought life for Sharif and 17 years and six months for the others. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook-capitals/first-big-test-for-italys-far-right-general/) had flagged ahead of the ruling that convictions could reignite tensions between Cairo and Rome over the enforcement of sentences and damages.

## UK Politics

**Mahmood retreats on indefinite leave to remain**
Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood is preparing to water down the government's migration reforms in the face of a backbench rebellion, [The Times reports](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/shabana-mahmood-migration-reforms-labour-rayner-5ckkf2zjk). Her original plan would have doubled the standard qualifying period for indefinite leave to remain from five years to ten, applied retroactively to migrants who arrived after 2021. More than 100 Labour MPs and the trade unions oppose it; Angela Rayner [told The Times](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/angela-rayner-housing-hmrc-tax-immigration-vzl2kflfq) the crackdown was unfair because it "change[s] the goalposts" for people already in the country. Mahmood told the Liverpool conference the government would "come to a position that balances fairness for those who came here legally, and fairness to British citizens across this country."

**Burnham's conference week: social care, the triple lock and the European question**
The Prime Minister has been warned that scrapping the state pension triple lock to fund free social care would be "electoral suicide", [The Times reports](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/politics/article/pensions-triple-lock-andy-burnham-labour-conference-tgdw9ckfw), as he prepares to set out a ten-year plan for reviving Britain in his address to the Labour conference. On Europe, a new [POLITICO Poll](https://www.politico.eu/article/the-politico-poll-britons-want-to-get-closer-to-the-eu-and-even-farages-voters-agree/) finds most British voters — including Nigel Farage's supporters — want a closer relationship with the EU, but [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/andy-burnham-eu-push-reopens-labours-brexit-divide/) reports that Burnham's push has reopened Labour's Brexit divide and that his Cabinet is far from united. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/09/28/siamo-sovrani-fateci-entrare/) emphasises the underlying tension rather than the politics, arguing London is asking Brussels to recognise cross-Channel supply chains at precisely the moment it is tilting its own procurement towards domestic suppliers — from October, public projects above £10m or using at least 500 tonnes of steel must record whether British steel was used and, if not, why.

**No explosives found at RAF Fairford; Rubio points at a "foreign actor"**
The five men arrested on suspicion of plotting a bomb attack near a British air base were released on bail after experts found no trace of explosives, [The Times reports](https://www.thetimes.com/uk/crime/article/raf-fairford-bomb-plot-suspects-arrested-news-7vmpptd92), though the investigation continues, including into whether the alleged plot was state-backed. ## Economy & Markets

**Gilt yields hit a post-crisis high as oil drives inflation fears**
UK government borrowing costs reached their highest level since the 2007 financial crisis as investors sold sovereign debt on renewed worries about inflation driven by rising oil prices, [The Times reports](https://www.thetimes.com/business/economics/article/uk-government-borrowing-costs-new-high-oil-prices-rise-9nc7fx3sg). The move sits directly downstream of the Gulf: [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/la-guerre-en-iran-entre-dans-son-huitieme-mois/) reports Brent at $107 a barrel, up more than 50 per cent year on year, even as Hormuz transit volumes recover to roughly two-thirds of pre-war levels on the back of surging Saudi exports averaging 5.28m barrels a day over the first 23 days of September.

**Russia budgets a 32 per cent rise in military spending for 2027**
Moscow plans to spend 17.1 trillion roubles — about €178bn — on defence next year, a 32 per cent increase on the 2026 budget and close to triple France's 2027 armed forces budget of €63.4bn, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/29/economie-russe-la-russie-de-poutine-na-jamais-autant-depense-pour-la-guerre/) reports, citing budget documents seen by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/russia-raises-2027-military-spending-by-27-budget-documents-show-2026-09-28/). The government has doubled its 2026 deficit forecast from 1.6 to 3.2 per cent of GDP, meaning the war effort is increasingly debt-financed: interest payments now run to roughly 4 trillion roubles a year, around 10 per cent of federal spending and more than education and health combined. With foreign investors excluded by sanctions, the state depends on Russian banks that Sberbank's finance chief said in July have funds only for lending to their own clients; the central bank is holding its policy rate at 14 per cent and warns a wider deficit would require tighter money. Economist Vladislav Inozemtsev estimates Russia will devote more than 10 per cent of GDP to military ends this year for the first time since the Soviet era.

**Anthropic's IPO prospectus lands as OpenAI pulls a model**
Anthropic is betting that AI will transform the global economy more profoundly than industrialisation, electricity and the internet, according to an IPO prospectus seen by [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/anthropics-ipo-prospectus-shows-sweeping-ai-vision-surging-costs-2026-09-28/), which also reports sharply rising costs. Rival OpenAI has [shelved a new model release](https://www.reuters.com/business/openai-shelves-new-ai-model-after-internal-safety-tests-wsj-reports-2026-09-28/) after internal safety testing, Reuters reports, citing the Wall Street Journal.

## Foreign Policy

**US forces leave Iraq after more than two decades**
American troops are due to vacate their last bases in Iraq by Wednesday, ending a presence in which 4,500 Americans died, [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/us-forces-exit-iraq-after-two-decades-leaving-opening-iran-2026-09-29/). Iran and its allies, who now hold deep influence in the country, are treating the withdrawal as a victory.

**The Iran war enters its eighth month with no settlement in sight**
The war launched by the United States and Israel against Iran passed the seven-month mark on Monday with no diplomatic resolution on the horizon, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/09/28/la-guerre-en-iran-entre-dans-son-huitieme-mois/) reports. Trump rejected an Iranian proposal at the weekend — Tehran had offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and restart nuclear talks in exchange for lifting the naval blockade and oil sanctions and a regional ceasefire — while telling Axios he expects American negotiators to hold fresh talks this week. Separately, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trump-denies-offering-iran-sanctions-relief-tehran-says-ready-talks-2026-09-29/) reports Trump denying media accounts that he had offered sanctions relief and the release of frozen funds for "concrete" steps on the nuclear programme. Le Grand Continent cites Vali Nasr's assessment that a durable settlement is unlikely because "Iran considers that it won the war in the first forty days", while Washington needs a victory it can claim; the outlet judges the conflict may run to the US midterms in early November.

**Ukraine advances on the eastern front as Russia deepens its manpower pool**
Ukraine's army pushed Russian forces back in a key sector of the eastern front and captured more than 250 prisoners, [Politico reports from Kyiv](https://www.politico.eu/article/ukraine-russia-war-territory-pows-front-line-operation-vivaldi/).
