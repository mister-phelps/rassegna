# Daily Briefing — Monday, 10 August 2026

## EU Affairs

**Brussels hunts for an off-ramp in the Italy–Spain border row**
The European Commission's central task this week is to defuse the dispute between Rome and Madrid over border controls that threatens the Schengen area, according to the [Politico Brussels Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/ending-the-border-check-battle/). Italy imposed the restrictions after roughly 72,000 people crossed from Morocco into the Spanish exclave of Ceuta at the start of the month; almost all have since returned. Three EU officials involved in the talks told Playbook that Brussels is targeting the online networks used by traffickers so that Italy can lift its measures without losing face, and is attempting to revive a long-stalled anti-smuggling directive; Tech Sovereignty Commissioner Henna Virkkunen summoned Meta and TikTok last week and continues those discussions today. Foreign Minister Antonio Tajani has signalled a climbdown, telling [Corriere della Sera](https://roma.corriere.it/notizie/cronaca/26_agosto_09/tajani-e-lo-stop-a-schengen-non-sottovalutare-rischio-terrorismo-controlli-in-vigore-fino-al-15-agosto-b6c5cdec-3dbb-458b-8629-0efdb9b45xlk.shtml) the measures will stay until 15 August, after which Rome is "ready to reinstate the previous arrangements". [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/spain-italy-reintroduce-border-controls-amid-migration-feud/) frames the episode primarily as an electoral collision between Pedro Sánchez and Italy's right-wing government, both facing elections next year, while [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/07/31/pourquoi-ceuta-nest-pas-une-crise-migratoire-pour-leurope/) argues the spectacular images do not support the "submersion" narrative at all and that Ceuta is not, in European terms, a migration crisis.

**Washington fans the flames as Ceuta protests swell**
The US State Department [accused Sánchez](https://www.politico.eu/article/us-ceuta-spain-territory-pedro-sanchez-defense/) of a "shameful refusal to defend its people from the migrant invasion", per Politico, adding an external dimension to what began as an intra-EU quarrel. An estimated 70,000 people took to the streets of Ceuta on Sunday demanding answers from the authorities and "solidarity" from Spanish society, [El País reports](https://elpais.com/espana/2026-08-09/ceuta-no-se-vende-la-ciudad-se-echa-a-la-calle-tras-diez-dias-de-tension.html). Commission spokesperson Guillaume Mercier told Politico the crisis — in which [an estimated 100 people died](https://www.dw.com/en/ceuta-leader-says-100-migrants-died-in-border-rush/a-78271067) — shows the bloc must "learn from the events [and] further strengthen infrastructure and deployments, combat smuggling networks and effectively and efficiently manage returns". Karel Lannoo of CEPS told Playbook that Schengen will remain weakened regardless, because "the Commission has been very weak on enforcement" when [countries such as Germany imposed their own checks](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-alexander-dobrindt-says-it-wont-drop-internal-border-checks/).

**Berlin edges towards naming Moscow over the Leipzig drone**
German Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt has warned the country faces ["daily" hybrid warfare attacks](https://www.politico.eu/article/germany-battling-daily-hybrid-warfare-attacks-minister-warns/) after a drone packed with explosives was detected at Leipzig airport last week, Politico reports. The [Politico Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/) adds that CNN, citing an unnamed US defence official in Europe, and the Wall Street Journal, citing US officials, both report the drone is assessed to belong to — or "probably" belong to — Russian intelligence, while Berlin is pointedly not yet pointing at Moscow: Dobrindt has spoken only of a "hybrid" attack by "foreign powers". Playbook notes the BKA has examined the drone and the explosives, so Germany should have its own findings, and that drones were sighted six times in recent days over the Bundeswehr site at Mechernich, where Patriot systems are serviced. Chancellor Friedrich Merz has so far stayed out of it, convening the national security council by telephone only. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/07/la-russie-prepare-t-elle-une-operation-sous-faux-drapeau-sur-le-territoire-de-lotan/) takes the question further, analysing warnings from Vilnius that Russia may be preparing a false-flag operation on NATO territory.

## UK Politics

**Burnham's income tax devolution: the devil in the equalisation**
Prime Minister Andy Burnham has announced one of the widest reforms of local government finance in decades, [Phastidio reports](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/03/burnham-e-il-fisco-di-prossimita/): from 2028, English combined authorities led by metro mayors — Greater Manchester, the West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, West Yorkshire, South Yorkshire and others — will retain a share of the income tax collected in their territory, with a larger share of business rates retained from 2027 and a tourist tax alongside. The government calls it "the biggest transfer of power and resources from Westminster in a generation"; Phastidio notes it is neither an additional local tax nor a power to set rates, but a redirection of revenue that today flows entirely to the Treasury. The analysis flags three unresolved risks: richer areas such as London, Greater Manchester and Cambridgeshire will generate far more revenue, so equalisation mechanisms remain to be designed; tying local budgets to income tax creates an incentive to attract high earners and exposes councils to the economic cycle; and Conservative mayors have already said they would return their share to taxpayers as rebates, which is not what Labour has in mind. The exact percentage will be set in the autumn budget.

**A "European Confederation" floated as the next post-Brexit settlement**

## Economy & Markets

**Europe's climate bill arrives — and the insurance market is retreating**
This summer's heatwaves have made clear that the economic impacts of climate change have already arrived rather than being a problem for future generations, [the Reuters Daily Briefing](https://www.reuters.com/newsletters/daily-briefing/) reports. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/10/la-france-et-leurope-deviendront-elles-inassurables/) puts numbers to it: on European Environment Agency data, 2021–2024 were the costliest four years for direct climate-related economic losses since 1980, with €208bn of damage recorded across the Union, a record likely to be surpassed once 2026 figures are in. The ECB and EIOPA calculate that only a quarter of climate-related damage in the EU is currently insured, and insurers facing unfamiliar, shifting risks are responding by raising premiums and narrowing cover. Guillaume Duval writes that the resulting protection gap transmits into the banking system — the LSE's Center for Economic Transition Expertise calls this the "insurance–bank nexus" — and that since 2023 the ECB and the insurance regulator have jointly called for a European climate reinsurance mechanism, potentially with a public fund for member states' infrastructure, though he warns such schemes should be conditional on credible adaptation plans to avoid moral hazard. Separately, Le Grand Continent's [summer data round-up](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/09/vers-un-monde-a-2-c-les-30-donnees-de-lete-du-basculement-ecologique/) notes the +2°C threshold could be crossed for the first time as early as the start of 2027.

## Foreign Policy

**Iran ties any Hormuz reopening to American concessions**
Iran says it is nearing a final pact with Oman defining new shipping lanes through the Strait of Hormuz, but has repeated that the United States must meet further conditions before the waterway reopens, [Reuters reports](https://www.reuters.com/newsletters/). [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/06/que-contient-laccord-que-liran-et-loman-seraient-en-train-de-finaliser-pour-le-transit-dans-le-detroit-dormuz/) sets out the reported terms, drawing on deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi's IRNA interview and reporting by Axios and Bloomberg: a temporary 60-day arrangement routing all inbound traffic through a northern corridor in Iranian waters and all outbound traffic through a southern corridor in Omani waters, with no tolls levied, and demining of the central corridor within 30 days — an operation Tehran is said to be willing to let European navies carry out. Gharibabadi said the arrangement could hold for "two to four months", but that it would not by itself guarantee resumption of traffic: Iran wants the US naval blockade lifted, sanctions reimposed after the ceasefire collapse withdrawn, and talks on frozen assets resumed. Donald Trump, meanwhile, is projecting calm — "we're only half-heartedly negotiating with them" and "we're holding back", he told Axios, in remarks relayed by the [Politico Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/).

**Netanyahu rejects Trump's 15-point Gaza plan**
Benjamin Netanyahu said on Sunday that he [would not accept the 15-point plan](https://www.politico.eu/article/israel-rejects-us-backed-gaza-peace-plan-hamas-disarmament/) to end the war in Gaza drawn up by Trump's Board of Peace — a significant blow to the White House's effort to freeze the conflict, Politico reports. The [Berlin Playbook](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/) sharpens the point for European capitals: Netanyahu made any withdrawal conditional on the complete disarmament of Hamas, which Berlin finds intelligible, but also declared there would be no Palestinian state in Gaza or the West Bank for as long as he is prime minister, which it does not.

**A Trump-linked oil company is preparing to drill in Greenland without permission**
Greenland Energy, a Texas-registered firm close to Donald Trump, has landed drilling equipment on Greenland's east coast without authorisation, [Le Grand Continent reports](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/08/groenland-energy-trump/), drawing on the Danish investigative outlet [Danwatch](https://danwatch.dk/en/without-permission-us-oil-company-has-just-shipped-equipment-to-jameson-land/) and a chronology assembled by [the Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/08/greenland-iwarning-trump-linked-oil-firm-prepares-drill). A tug towed a barge to the shore at Jameson Land in July and unloaded around a dozen containers; Nuuk issued a "strong warning" but judged removal disproportionate while the licence application remains under assessment. Rather than pausing, the company has accelerated: a vessel carrying 300 containers is due to sail from Canada on 12 September, with the first exploration well targeted for the fourth quarter. Greenland stopped issuing oil licences in 2021 on environmental grounds and the planned wells appear to fall within a Ramsar-protected wetland, leaving the autonomous government caught between disowning its own moratorium and handing Washington a pretext to renew pressure — pressure that, Le Grand Continent notes, has so far only tightened Nuuk's cooperation with Copenhagen, with 85% of Greenlanders rejecting any union with the United States according to Bloomberg polling.
