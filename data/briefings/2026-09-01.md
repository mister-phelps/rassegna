# Daily Briefing — Tuesday, 1 September 2026

## EU Affairs

**Iceland's "No" deals enlargement its first real setback**

Icelanders' narrow rejection on Saturday of reopening EU membership talks has punctured what had become an article of faith in Brussels — that expansion was regaining momentum. The [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/paris-and-berlins-plan-to-fix-eu-foreign-policy/) Brussels Playbook frames the result as a blow to the bloc's self-image, quoting one EU official who had hoped a Yes vote would be "an endorsement of Europe's attractiveness in the world" and another who compared the outcome to "being rejected by someone you haven't even asked out"; Marine Le Pen and Nigel Farage both celebrated on X. [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) emphasises the strategic consequence rather than the wounded pride: with Iceland gone, planners can no longer package a wealthy Nordic candidate alongside Montenegro and Albania to offset scepticism about poorer applicants, so Podgorica — already under pressure from Zagreb — now faces the full weight of scrutiny ahead of the leaders' summit in mid-October. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/iceland-votes-whether-start-eu-membership-talks-2026-08-29/) reports the vote more plainly as Icelanders siding with those who said the country's fishing waters were too important to risk. Prime Minister Kristrún Frostadóttir, who called the result she did not want "a victory for democracy", says she will shelve the question for the rest of her term.

**Paris and Berlin move to redraw the EU's foreign policy machinery**

Kaja Kallas would gain far-reaching powers to coordinate the Commission's external-facing units — including migration and development aid — under a Franco-German discussion paper circulating among capitals, according to a [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/paris-and-berlins-plan-to-fix-eu-foreign-policy/) exclusive based on three diplomats. The same paper would shrink the European External Action Service itself, moving part of its roughly 5,000 staff into the Commission amid pressure from Friedrich Merz and others to cut the long-term EU budget. Notably, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/merz-will-russland-konfrontieren/) presents this as a net strengthening of Kallas — "light and shadow", in the Berlin Playbook's phrase, since she would retain access to the transferred staff — while [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) describes essentially the same proposal as a German plan to fold the diplomatic service into the Commission that "would de facto spell the end of the standalone service led by Kaja Kallas" and strengthen Ursula von der Leyen's grip on foreign policy. Kallas has sent capitals a five-question survey and plans initial proposals this week, though [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/paris-and-berlins-plan-to-fix-eu-foreign-policy/) reports she wants her new leadership team, including incoming Secretary-General Kajsa Ollongren, in place first. One diplomat was blunt: "A big institutional change right now is not realistic." Foreign ministers take it up at the Gymnich in Wicklow, Ireland.

**Berlin prepares to name Russia over Leipzig as the EU readies its largest sanctions list**

Friedrich Merz intends to hold Russia formally responsible for the attempted explosives attack in Leipzig and announce what [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/merz-will-russland-konfrontieren/) Berlin Playbook calls "extensive" consequences, most plausibly this week and timed alongside the EU ministerial meetings in Ireland. Investigators reportedly lack a single smoking gun but have assembled enough evidence across Leipzig and other incidents — including a weapons cache near Berlin — to be confident of attribution; German measures under consideration include expelling diplomats, tougher action against the shadow fleet, and closing the Russia House and other Russian properties. In parallel, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/friedrich-merz-berlin-sanctions-russia-vladimir-putin-leipzig-attach-ascalate/) reports the EU is reviewing a draft sanctions list of roughly 1,600 individuals and entities — around 800 companies and 800 people, per the Berlin edition — which would be the most far-reaching package since the invasion began, with finalisation expected by the October European Council. "The strategy is to fire on all cylinders," one EU diplomat told Playbook. The timing is politically loaded: Saxony-Anhalt votes on 6 September, where the Russia-friendly AfD leads comfortably. [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/30/simulations-exclusives-saxe-anhalt-lafd-gouvernerait-dans-plus-de-6-scenarios-sur-10-en-cas-dabstention-du-bsw/) puts the AfD on 42–43% in three August polls against 22–23% for the CDU, and its simulations give the party an outright Landtag majority in 54% of 1,000 runs, rising to a governing position in more than six scenarios in ten if the BSW abstains at a third ballot. Merz warned in his ARD summer interview that the state would be "considerably damaged" and international investors deterred by an AfD government. Finland's President Alexander Stubb, meanwhile, urged a "clear and rational" European response and told the Axel Springer network he does not see evidence for German warnings that Russia could strike a NATO country by 2029.

**The recovery fund clock runs out, with Hungary's €10 billion in the balance**

Member states that had not completed the reforms needed to unlock Recovery and Resilience Facility money ran out of time at midnight, and [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/paris-and-berlins-plan-to-fix-eu-foreign-policy/) reports the Commission has told capitals that anything done after 31 August will not count. Countries have until 30 September to file final payment requests, after which Brussels assesses whether the changes suffice. Péter Magyar's government has been pushing reforms through in pursuit of roughly €10 billion, with a Commission official saying Budapest worked "until the last minute"; the verdict will not be clear until assessments begin next month. Romania has already conceded defeat, its president acknowledging on Friday that it will forfeit €770 million after failing to complete key reforms.

**France's 2027 field takes shape as Le Pen is measured as favourite for the first time**

Olivier Faure has confirmed he will contest the presidential election, [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) reports, pledging higher taxes on the wealthy, a minimum wage rise and a "green socialism" agenda — entering a crowded left field days after Raphaël Glucksmann declared, and with the centre-left still divided over whether to exclude Jean-Luc Mélenchon from any alliance. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/playbook-paris/faure-pret-a-sortir-du-blois/) adds that Faure's move has prompted others to weigh bids, including the investment banker Matthieu Pigasse. Against that fragmentation, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/31/marine-le-pen-peut-elle-encore-perdre-lelection-presidentielle/) publishes a Cluster 17 study by Jean-Yves Dormagen finding that for the first time Marine Le Pen leads in both rounds — around 30% in the first, and beating all five tested opponents in the second, with the tightest duel against Édouard Philippe at 52-48. Dormagen's central argument is that the *front républicain* has eroded rather than merely gone dormant: Le Pen is still rejected by nearly one French voter in two, but no more than Philippe, Gabriel Attal, Glucksmann or Bruno Retailleau. He also sets out five mechanisms that could still defeat her — chiefly a campaign that shifts from cultural cleavage to European, institutional and economic credibility, where her broadened electorate divides — and notes that at 52-48 roughly 700,000 direct switchers would erase her lead.

## Italian Politics

**Rome and Madrid trade border controls over Ceuta, putting Schengen in play**

Italy extended border controls on travellers from Spain on Monday, prompting reciprocal measures from Madrid in what [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/) calls "an extraordinary tit-for-tat feud" between two of the bloc's most important Mediterranean member states — with the Schengen area itself, in Euractiv's framing, the stake. The trigger is the unresolved question of how roughly 80,000 migrants surged into the Spanish exclave of Ceuta on 30–31 July. Pedro Sánchez broke his silence on Monday to clear Morocco of responsibility, attributing the influx to social media disinformation rather than Moroccan border authorities — a reversal from his earlier condemnation of a "violation of Spain's territorial integrity" that Euractiv notes will feed the political storm over his handling of the crisis. Sánchez called the Italian extension "absurd". Madrid has separately asked Frontex for ten additional officers to help register and screen arrivals, a first for a government that had until now avoided seeking extra agency support, and is preparing a €165 million aid package for Ceuta, per *El País* cited by [Euractiv](https://www.euractiv.com/); between 5,000 and 8,000 migrants, including unaccompanied children, remain in the enclave.

## Economy & Markets

**Bond markets sell off as the Iran strikes push oil higher**

Borrowing costs in the euro area and Japan hit multi-year highs on Monday as a 3% jump in oil prices, following the resumption of US–Iran strikes, compounded inflation anxiety that could keep central banks in tightening mode, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/global-markets-global-markets-2026-08-31/) reports. US stock index futures fell on the same fears. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/depleted-us-oil-stash-loses-potency-iran-war-grinds-2026-08-31/) also notes that Washington's depleted Strategic Petroleum Reserve is losing its capacity to cushion price shocks as the Iran war grinds on — context for Donald Trump's claim that oil from the new US–Venezuela deal will refill it, an arrangement that [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/experts-lawyers-puzzled-over-us-venezuela-oil-deal-call-contract-transparency-2026-08-31/) reports lawyers and experts are struggling to interpret without contract transparency. Trump is due to host oil executives after accusing refiners of gouging consumers.

**G20 finance ministers convene in Asheville with Washington pressing for a united front**

Lars Klingbeil is in North Carolina for the G20 finance ministers' meeting, where the dominant subject is the "economic D-Day" Trump has declared against Iran, alongside the US–Canada trade war, Chinese overcapacity and debt-driven financial stability fears, [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/merz-will-russland-konfrontieren/) Berlin Playbook reports from the delegation. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/g20-countries-should-consider-more-trade-barriers-with-china-cut-imbalances-2026-08-30/) reports that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent wants major economies to join US economic pressure campaigns against Iran and China. Russia's finance minister Anton Siluanov is also expected. Berlin has protested the Trump administration's refusal to accredit a Bloomberg journalist travelling with Klingbeil's delegation — a New York Times correspondent was eventually cleared — with the delegation calling the treatment of an international news agency "unprecedented and, from the federal government's perspective, unacceptable".

**American profits are booming — but the accounting deserves reading twice**

[Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/31/stati-uniti-boom-di-utili-malgrado-il-girotondo/) sets two contemporaneous studies against each other. Citing the *Financial Times*, Mario Seminerio notes Big Tech booked over $160 billion last quarter in gains on stakes in other AI companies, with "other income" more than doubling from roughly $69 billion the previous quarter — Alphabet more than doubling to $97.9 billion, Amazon more than tripling to $53.4 billion, and Nvidia recording $7.7 billion, much of it driven by SpaceX's listing. Mechanically, this depresses price/earnings multiples and can land such stocks in passive value funds. But Phastidio's conclusion, drawing on a *Wall Street Journal* analysis, is that the underlying boom is real: S&P 500 earnings per share rose 53% year-on-year in the second quarter on 16% revenue growth per LSEG data, and stripping out the AI-related "other income" still leaves the strongest earnings growth since autumn 2021, with upward revisions outnumbering downgrades two to one. Seminerio flags the comparison risk when the one-off gains stop, and reads consumer behaviour as a K-shaped economy — grim survey sentiment, still-resilient spending, and a savings rate hitting new lows almost monthly.

## Foreign Policy

**US and Iran exchange strikes for the first time since July**

American forces struck two Iranian launchers on Larak Island — the first known US strikes on Iran since late July — which CENTCOM said were preparing to lay sea mines in the Strait of Hormuz, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/iran-war-live-us-iran-resume-attacks-trump-says-kharg-island-is-being-blown-2026-08-31/) and [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/08/30/us-strikes-irans-larak-island-01056585). Iran's Revolutionary Guards said they retaliated with ballistic missiles against two air bases used by US forces in Jordan; [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/berlin-playbook/merz-will-russland-konfrontieren/) Berlin Playbook logs the strikes at roughly midnight and the response at 3am CET, with Jordan reporting eight missiles shot down. Separately, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/trump-posts-ai-video-irans-kharg-smithereens-no-evidence-attack-2026-08-31/) reports that Trump posted an AI-generated video he said showed Iran's oil hub Kharg Island "being blown to smithereens", for which there is no evidence of an attack; an Iranian official called it "laughable". Oil traded more than 3% higher.

**Russia escalates against Ukraine's cities and prepares to hit its energy grid**

Jet-powered drones have swarmed Kyiv and surrounding towns for four consecutive days, killing residents and damaging homes, in what [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/russian-drone-barrage-kyiv-enters-fifth-day-disrupting-life-across-capital-2026-08-31/) describes as an extended air campaign now entering its fifth day. [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/article/russia-preparing-massive-strikes-against-ukraines-energy-facilities/) reports Russia's defence ministry said on Sunday it is preparing a significant offensive against Ukrainian energy infrastructure. EU defence and foreign ministers meeting in Wicklow this week will discuss bolstering Ukraine's air defences and how to fund Kyiv in coming years; [Politico](https://www.politico.eu/newsletter/brussels-playbook/paris-and-berlins-plan-to-fix-eu-foreign-policy/) reports that Greece and Spain, which have not contributed significant interceptor stocks, will face pressure.

**The Vatican tests its Ostpolitik in Moscow, and comes away with little**

Archbishop Paul Richard Gallagher, the Holy See's foreign minister, spent four days in Moscow from 24 August — an unusually long visit at that rank — meeting Sergei Lavrov for an hour, Putin's foreign policy adviser Yuri Ushakov, and Metropolitan Anton of Volokolamsk, though not Putin himself, [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/31/leon-xiv-envoie-ministre-des-affaires-etrangeres-a-moscou-les-permanences-de-la-nouvelle-ostpolitik-du-saint-siege-au-service-de-la-paix/) reports. Jean-Benoît Poulle reads the outcome as thin: Gallagher reiterated the Holy See's general call to end the war but set out no conditions or technical proposals, which the piece takes as a sign that Moscow offers very little purchase for deeper negotiation. Timing is the more revealing detail — the visit coincided with CIA director John Ratcliffe's own trip to Moscow and effectively replaced a postponed Witkoff–Kushner visit, and Le Grand Continent notes Russian analysts are alert to the fact that Leo XIV is American and seen as an opponent of Trump. Cardinal Matteo Zuppi is expected in Moscow in September. Gallagher also celebrated a Mass for peace and, the piece suggests, sounded out the ground for a new archbishop of Moscow, a see vacant since 2 May.
