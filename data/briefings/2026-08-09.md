## EU Affairs

**Vilnius warns Moscow could stage a false-flag drone attack on Baltic infrastructure**
Lithuania's defence minister Robertas Kaunas made public a military intelligence assessment on Thursday suggesting Russia is considering using captured Ukrainian drones to strike critical infrastructure in the Baltic states, disguising the origin of the attack. As [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/07/la-russie-prepare-t-elle-une-operation-sous-faux-drapeau-sur-le-territoire-de-lotan/) reports, Kaunas described the scenario as realistic while stressing there is no indication Moscow has decided to act or that an attack is imminent; Vilnius has separately declined to allow Ukraine to use Lithuanian airspace for strikes on Russia, even as it weighs hosting a Ukrainian drone plant. The warning is not isolated — Le Grand Continent situates it alongside earlier statements from Polish foreign minister Radek Sikorski, Donald Tusk, Lithuanian president Gitanas Nausėda and Latvia's Edgars Rinkēvičs, all pointing to the coming months as decisive for eastern-flank security. It also follows the discovery of an explosive-laden drone near a Ukrainian cargo aircraft at Leipzig airport, which German interior minister Alexander Dobrindt has characterised as a possible hybrid attack without naming a perpetrator. The Kremlin has not responded; in July, spokesman Dmitry Peskov dismissed Baltic warnings as scare tactics intended to justify "militarisation against Russia".

**A summer of records puts a first +2 °C month within reach as early as 2027**
[Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/09/vers-un-monde-a-2-c-les-30-donnees-de-lete-du-basculement-ecologique/) has compiled thirty data points on what it calls the summer of ecological tipping, drawing on Copernicus, the WHO and national agencies. June 2026 was the hottest June ever recorded in western Europe at 20.74 °C, 3.06 °C above the 1991–2020 norm, with Germany breaking its all-time national record three days running (41.7 °C at Coschen on 28 June) and the United Kingdom setting a new June record of 37.3 °C at Santon Downham. Santé publique France counted 5,764 excess deaths during the 17 June–2 July heatwave, while the WHO puts partial European heat mortality this summer at close to 10,000 across just five countries; more than 500,000 hectares had burned across the Union by early August, over 200,000 of them in Spain. Projections from the University of Miami's Rosenstiel School cited in the piece give a 35–40 per cent chance that a single month exceeds +2 °C above pre-industrial levels for the first time in early 2027, amplified by what may be one of the strongest El Niño episodes on record. Copernicus now regards the Paris Agreement's 1.5 °C target as effectively out of reach on the current trajectory, with durable breach expected within roughly four years.

**Trump-linked oil company lands drilling equipment in Greenland without authorisation**
Greenland Energy, a Texas-registered company created in 2025 and traded as GLND, shipped drilling equipment to Jameson Land on Greenland's east coast in July without a permit, according to Danish investigative outlet Danwatch as relayed by [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/08/groenland-energy-trump/). Nuuk issued a "strong warning" but judged removal disproportionate while the permit application remains under review — a fait accompli complicated by Greenland's 2021 moratorium on oil licensing and the fact that the planned wells appear to fall within a Ramsar-protected wetland. Le Grand Continent reports the company is accelerating rather than pausing: a vessel carrying 300 containers is due to leave Canada on 12 September, with the first exploration well targeted for the fourth quarter, and Trump's special envoy Jeff Landry has suggested production could begin next year. The publication frames the episode within the administration's "Donroe" hemispheric-security doctrine and a shift from pressuring Copenhagen to pressuring Greenland's 57,000 inhabitants directly; company chairman Larry Swets denies any link to annexation plans. Bloomberg polling cited in the piece found 85 per cent of Greenlanders opposed to joining the United States.

## UK Politics

**Burnham promises metro mayors a share of income tax from 2028**
Prime minister Andy Burnham has announced what the government calls the largest transfer of powers and resources from Westminster in a generation, allowing English combined authorities led by metro mayors — Greater Manchester, the West Midlands, Liverpool City Region, West and South Yorkshire among them — to retain a share of the income tax collected in their areas from 2028, with a larger share of business rates from 2027 and a tourist tax already legislated for by former chancellor Rachel Reeves. [Phastidio](https://phastidio.net/2026/08/03/burnham-e-il-fisco-di-prossimita/) stresses what the plan is not: neither an additional local levy nor a power to set income tax rates locally, but a redirection of revenue already flowing to the Treasury, earmarked for transport, housing, regeneration and infrastructure. The site's critique turns on distribution and design — richer bases such as London, Greater Manchester and Cambridgeshire would gain disproportionately, equalisation mechanisms remain undefined, and tying local budgets to income tax exposes them to the cycle and creates incentives to court high earners over poorer households. Conservative mayors have already said they would return their share to taxpayers as rebates, which is not what Burnham's Labour has in mind, and the exact devolved percentage will only be set at the autumn Budget. Phastidio reads the whole exercise as further evidence of Britain's "Italian" trajectory, warning that decentralisation debates in Italy ended in a net rise in the tax burden.

## Economy & Markets

**Ukrainian drone strikes are pushing Russia to export crude it can no longer refine**
Preliminary S&P figures published on 4 August and reported by [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/08/economie-russe-les-drones-ukrainiens-font-flamber-lexportation-de-brut/) show Russian seaborne refined-product exports falling to 1.18 million barrels per day in July from 1.51 million in June, the lowest since the series began in 2016, after Ukrainian strikes knocked out more than a third of Russian refining capacity and cut throughput below 4 million b/d. Moscow has redirected the unrefined barrels: seaborne crude exports reached 4.321 million b/d, down 7 per cent on June but up 26 per cent year on year and the second-best month of 2026. Exports to China rose 18 per cent month on month to 1.017 million b/d, while flows recorded as going to Singapore more than doubled to 456,000 b/d — a figure that includes ship-to-ship transfers in South-East Asian waters that obscure final destinations. The publication reads the paradox as bad news for the Kremlin: volumes hold up but revenues fall, since crude fetches less than products, and Russia grows more dependent on a shadow fleet S&P now estimates at around 22 per cent of the global total.

**Hormuz traffic thins as an Iran–Oman transit framework nears completion**
Iranian deputy foreign minister Kazem Gharibabadi told IRNA that Tehran and Muscat have reached agreement in principle on almost all points of a framework for commercial navigation through the Strait of Hormuz, including the map of inbound and outbound lanes. Per [Le Grand Continent](https://legrandcontinent.eu/fr/2026/08/06/que-contient-laccord-que-liran-et-loman-seraient-en-train-de-finaliser-pour-le-transit-dans-le-detroit-dormuz/), citing regional sources and a US official quoted by Axios, the arrangement would run 60 days with inbound traffic using a northern corridor through Iranian waters and outbound traffic a southern corridor through Omani waters, no tolls levied, and the central channel to be demined within 30 days — Bloomberg reports Tehran would accept European navies doing the clearing. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/middle-east/iran-says-deal-strait-hormuz-is-close-will-not-open-waterway-by-itself-2026-08-08/) frames the same story around Iranian insistence that a deal with Oman is close but will not by itself reopen the waterway, with Tehran conditioning resumption on the lifting of the US naval blockade and oil sanctions. Meanwhile [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/vessel-traffic-through-hormuz-dwindles-this-week-markets-watch-iran-oman-talks-2026-08-07/) reports vessel traffic through the strait dwindling this week as markets watch the talks.
